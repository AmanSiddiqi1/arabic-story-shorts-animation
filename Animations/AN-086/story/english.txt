One day Caliph Hisham went hunting and rode away from his companions while pursuing a gazelle. He stopped beside a young shepherd and asked for directions.

Hisham did not greet him. He spoke as if ordering a servant. The boy looked up and boldly asked whether a king, too, should not speak with courtesy.

Hisham angrily declared that he was the caliph. The youth replied that high position did not excuse bad manners and that everyone deserved a greeting.

The caliph’s men arrived. Hisham had the youth seized and ordered an executioner to kill him. The young shepherd stood firm as the executioner awaited the signal.

The executioner asked three times whether to carry out the order. The boy remained calm, then suddenly laughed. Hisham demanded to know why he laughed so near death.

The boy told of a hawk that caught a little sparrow. The sparrow asked what use such a tiny meal could be. The hawk considered the question and released it.

The youth then asked what a mighty ruler could gain by killing a poor shepherd. The court fell silent; Hisham burst out laughing at the boy’s wit and courage.

Hisham ordered his release and had precious jewels placed in his mouth as a reward. The boy left alive and honored, having faced the ruler’s anger with a sparrow’s story.
