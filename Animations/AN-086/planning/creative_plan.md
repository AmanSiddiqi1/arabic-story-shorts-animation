# AN-086 The Caliph Hisham and the Arab Youth: creative plan

Film length 168.13 s of narration + 3.0 s THE END = 171.1 s. All times are seconds on the final MP3 and every cue is a word start from `inputs/timeline.md`. The machine-readable storyboard (9 scenes, every action with its cue word) is `creative_plan.json`; this file explains the direction.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A caliph out hunting barks at a shepherd boy without a greeting. The boy answers him back, is sentenced to death, and laughs his way to freedom with a tale of a hawk and a sparrow.

**Conflict.** Rank against manners. The caliph has all the power (horse, crown, guards, sword); the boy has only calm and words. The picture must keep showing that imbalance, and keep showing that the boy does not bend.

**Emotional arc.** Swagger (hunt) -> friction (the order) -> surprise at the boy's nerve -> heat (rage) -> cold (the sword, the raised hand) -> a held breath (three asks) -> release in the wrong place (the boy's laugh) -> curiosity (the tale) -> recognition (the caliph mirrors the hawk) -> silence -> big warm laugh -> generosity.

**Surprises.** The boy's laugh at 97.02; the sparrow flying out of the story cloud into the real sky at 124.2; the caliph's laugh after 2.7 s of total stillness; the gem between the boy's teeth.

**Ending and moral.** Good manners bind kings too, and a calm, clever word can turn away a ruler's anger. The closing picture pays off the first offence: the caliph, who would not greet, greets first (159.3), and the boy returns it.

## 3. Character acting

- **Hisham** (`person` costume `caliph`, age 0.45). Starts all chin and half lids (`lid` 0.3-0.4), speaks with jabbing arm shapes (`aR` angles so they read at phone size). Anger is the planned `flush` plus `tremble` on the crown, with `anticipate` before each outburst. The **signal hand** is his through-line: up at 73.44, sinking at each count, dropping at 98.04. In S7 he strokes his beard (`PERSON_HANDS.chin`) and copies the hawk's head tilt 0.5 s late. The laugh needs a real wind-up (140.84: twitch, cheeks, `sq` 0.94) before it breaks; afterwards `wet` eyes, crooked crown, loose arms. One look to camera only, at 98.04.
- **The youth** (`bedouin`, `youth: true`, s 0.84). Rule: he **never trembles and never steps back**. Calm is shown by slow blinks, `breathe`, loose shoulders, and by small ordinary attentions (the lamb, a butterfly, a sheep) while everyone else is rigid. His gestures are open palms and hand on chest (`palmsUp`, `chest`), never a pointing finger at the caliph's face; the only point is up at the crown. His head lift at 30.2 has eyes arriving last (`look` gaze plan). Laugh at 97.02: head back, `lid` 1, three `sq` bounces.
- **Executioner** (`executioner` preset, s 1.08). Deadpan professional: heavy lids, eyes move before the head, a shrug at 143.18. The comedy is his patience. The sword never points at the boy.
- **Guards.** A keen, B soft (glances at the boy and away at 68.48, shuts his eyes at 89.26, nods at 150.0). They tremble slightly while holding the calm boy. Always staggered by 3-5 frames (`stagger`).
- **Vizier.** Out of breath in S1 and S5, hand over mouth at 73.44, wipes his brow at 143.18.
- **Sheep and horse** are the innocent audience: heads turn one after another; the lamb hides, returns, follows. Horse ears and stamp show impatience matching its rider.
- **Hawk and sparrow** use the bird mime set: `headRot`, `beak`, `sag` wing pointing, saccade thinking (`gazePlan(t, plan, 0.07)`), `swing` for the carried sparrow.

## 4. Camera and composition

- S1 tracking loop, closing from zoom 1.0 to 1.25 so the companions leave the frame: he is alone.
- S2-S3 a **steep diagonal two-shot**: mounted caliph's head near y 470, seated boy's near y 730. When the boy stands and the caliph later dismounts (hidden by the cut at 43.3) the eye line flattens: they argue as two people.
- S4 opens on the only extreme facial close shot (the flush), then a level two-shot with a slow push on the boy's answer.
- S5-S8 share one blocking (boy 600, guards 520/690, executioner 800, caliph 980 or 860, vizier 1120, horse 1300; ground y 900). Wide for arrivals and the silence, push-ins for the boy's stillness (75.98) and the caliph's laugh (141.5).
- S7 lowers the camera centre (y 560) so the sky is free for the story cloud; tellers stay in the bottom band.
- S9 pulls out to the widest frame of the film and pans left with the boy. THE END sits in open sky.
- All faces and key action stay above world y 900; labels and lettering stay out of the head band y 640-760 in the group scenes. No decorative tilts, no shake.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Running sign:** the `greetingIcon` (hand over heart). Crossed out at 23.1, raised again by the boy at 35.64, offered to all at 58.52, finally given by the caliph at 159.3. It makes the abstract argument visible without extra words.
- **Suspense:** the hand that must fall and does not; the counter 1, 2, 3; blinks suppressed in the two holds (81.2-83.4, 139.1-140.8).
- **Humour:** companions on foot chasing a horse; the horse's stamp; the bored executioner asking "now?"; guards more frightened than their prisoner; the sheep's BAA in the silence; the gem in the grin.
- **Surprise:** the laugh at 97.02 arrives after a visible but ambiguous wind-up; the sparrow leaves the cloud for the real sky and returns in the last shot.
- **Emotion:** the caliph recognising himself (mirrored head tilt, hawk icon over the crown, sparrow icon over the boy), then choosing to laugh.
