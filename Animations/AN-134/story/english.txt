One day Caliph al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah rode out in a great procession and passed a beautiful garden surrounded by trees. He saw a man sitting there with his servants and asked him for a drink of water, and the man hurried to bring him something to drink.

The garden’s owner invited the caliph to dismount and rest, and he agreed and went in with those who came with him. Suddenly servants appeared carrying many rugs and cushions, and prepared wide places for the guests to sit beneath the trees.

Then came platters of fruit and sweets, vessels of cool drinks and many kinds of food. Some of these things numbered a hundred of each kind, so that the caliph marveled at how quickly a table this large had been prepared.

Al-Hakim asked the garden’s owner whether he had known in advance the procession would pass and had prepared all this awaiting him. The man replied that he had heard nothing of the visit, and that what the caliph saw was part of his household’s usual daily routine.

The merchant explained that there were a hundred maidservants in his house, and each one sent food, drink and things for resting every day. When the caliph stopped, the servants gathered what usually came from all of them and served it to the visitors.

It was not meant as a special banquet, but the spread the caliph saw was what reached the garden’s owner on an ordinary day. Al-Hakim grew even more amazed when he grasped how much money this merchant could spend.

The caliph thanked God for the abundant provision he saw given to one of his subjects, then ordered the dirhams minted that year and collected in the state treasury to be brought, and they numbered three million seven hundred thousand dirhams.

He gave all the money to the merchant, and said that such generous hospitality deserved more than that. Then he mounted his horse and returned to his palace, and a short stop for water became the reason for a gift the garden’s owner never expected.
