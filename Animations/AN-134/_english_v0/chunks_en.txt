Caliph al-Hakim rode in a great procession|past a beautiful garden.|He saw a man among his servants|and asked for water, which the man readily provided.
The garden’s owner invited the caliph to rest.|He accepted,|and attendants quickly spread many rugs and cushions|beneath the trees for his entourage.
Platters of fruit and sweets, cool drinks and food|followed—some items numbering a hundred each.|The caliph wondered how such an enormous feast had appeared so quickly.
Al-Hakim asked whether the merchant had expected his visit|and prepared in advance.|The host insisted|that he had known nothing of it.
The merchant explained that a hundred women in his household|each sent daily food, drink and furnishings.|His servants had simply gathered the usual contributions|for the visitors.
It was no specially arranged banquet:|the lavish spread|was part of an ordinary day.|The caliph was even more astonished by the merchant’s wealth and hospitality.
The caliph thanked God for the prosperity of one of his subjects.|He ordered the treasury to bring the dirhams minted|that year—three million seven hundred thousand.
He gave all the money to the merchant,|declaring such hospitality deserved even more,|and rode away.|A simple request for water had brought an extraordinary gift.
