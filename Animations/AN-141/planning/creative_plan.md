## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A wife hides a live fish in a water jar, vanishes to a wedding for a week, and on her return uses the still living fish to convince the neighbours that her truthful husband is mad.

**Conflict.** His true story against her false one, judged by neighbours who trust one piece of "hard" evidence: a fish cannot live seven days, the fish is alive, so the fish came today.

**Emotional arc.** Cheerful and domestic (0-19), mischievous (19-48), lonely and slow (48-78), a quarrel that turns into a magic trick (78-99), a courtroom farce (99-136), then cold and quiet (136-164). The ending is unjust on purpose: no rescue, no gloating, no smile after 139.60.

**Three visual ideas carry the film.**
1. **The fish is always alive and cheerful.** It flaps at 4.46, gulps at the pot at 15.26, swims in the jar's cutaway window from 45.44, and is on screen beside the waiting husband all week (dramatic irony: we know, he never looks).
2. **The week is a row of seven dots** (`weekDots`) with the tag FRIDAY (2.52) and FRIDAY AGAIN (76.54). The same dots return in his balloon (114.44), are struck out by the neighbours (127.52) and go out one by one in the cell (143.68).
3. **The wife's mask.** She smirks only when nobody is looking at her (36.88, 88.28, 108.90, 132.06) and is innocent or tearful otherwise.

## 3. Character acting

- **Husband** (`villager`, s 1.2). Opens proud and bouncy (chest out, half lid 0.3). Hunger is played in the belly (`sq` pulse 0.96) and the eyes (saccades with `gazePlan` ease 0.07). The wait is stillness: `sit`, chin in hand, `breathe`, a nod-off and snap awake. Anger: planned `flush`, `brow` 1, `tremble`, hard heel `footfalls`. Pleading: `brow` -0.6, `palmsUp`, gaze moving from face to face; seven fingers shown with **arm angles** (`aL/aR`) so the shape reads at phone size. Collapse: hands slide down the bars, `kneel` 0 to 1 over 1.2 s with the head following late, `wet`, one tear, a 0.5 s blink.
- **Wife** (`villageWoman`, s 1.15). Fast and light: `anticipate` + `hop` + `landSquash` at 28.16, the freeze with `settleTo` at 30.00, the tail-pinch carry with the fish on `swing`. Thinking is `PERSON_HANDS.chin` plus darting eyes that stop on the jar. Her only look to camera is the wink at 46.14. From 78.10 she is relaxed (lid 0.3, loose arms) against his rigidity; fake tears use `wet` with `brow` -0.6. Mask rule as in section 1.
- **Friend** (`maiden`, blue palette). Doorway only: wave, scroll held at full arm, a little sway as if dancing, thumb over the shoulder, a wink, gone.
- **Neighbours.** Old woman (`oldWoman`, stick): sharp, first to react, taps her chin, laughs behind her hand. Carpenter (`carpenter`): slow, arms folded, counts on fingers, lifts one eyebrow, does the grabbing. Bean seller (`beanSeller`): eager, speaks the verdict with a teacher's finger, twirls the finger at his temple, laughs bent double. Always `stagger(i)` by 3-6 frames; never in unison.
- **Fish.** Tail `flap` bursts, a round eye with `gaze` and `lid`, a gulp (`sq` 0.9 then 1.05) at the pot, lazy loops in the jar, a jump at 123.10, one bubble at the end. It never knows what it caused.
- **Secondary motion:** the string and the dangling fish lag each step (`swing`); the stool rocks when he springs up; water sloshes in the cutaway; the bars vibrate 1 unit for 0.4 s after the slam.

## 4. Camera and composition

- World 1000 x 1230, floor/ground y 900; faces and action stay in the upper two thirds of the picture panel; the caption panel holds only subtitles.
- **Home blocking:** door left, table centre (x about 520-620), pot x 720, jar x 840. Dialogue scenes use `twoShot` with both faces in frame (S2, S8). Group scenes (S10, S11, S13) are wide at zoom about 1.15: neighbours left, wife centre, husband alone on the right with the jar behind him.
- **S5 and S7 are locked wide**: only the window sky, the lamp and the dots change, with the jar in every frame.
- **Push-ins on decisions:** her eyes finding the jar (33.6-38), the fish rising (95.0), his seven fingers (114.4), his face behind the bars (139.6-145).
- **S12** is the one close shot: three big faces round the bowl, the husband small between their heads at 130.02.
- **S15** pulls out to a two-half picture: cell left, lamp-lit fish bowl right, scale above; then locked.
- Street scenes track with the walker on a 2400 strip. Shake is panel-only (bars, heel steps, IMPOSSIBLE!).
- Transitions: cuts; one LATER card (72.4, "DAYS PASSED..."); the bars themselves wipe into the jail (136.2).

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour:** the fish gulping at the pot; the pot kicked aside and X-ed; two neighbours pointing opposite ways; the wedding cloud pushed behind her back; a drop of water on the old woman's nose in the middle of the solemn inspection; three different laughs.
- **Suspense:** the held beat on the jar (37.9); the silent room with the fish circling beside the hungry man; the silence at 118.82 before every head turns to the bowl.
- **Surprise:** the fish lifted out, centre panel, at 95.02.
- **Irony made visible:** the neighbours' balloon uses the husband's own seven dots against him and strikes them out; at the end three ticks sit on PROOF while TRUE hangs on the bars.
- **Emotion:** the tear at 142.80, his balloon going out dot by dot, and the wife's single unsmiling glance back at 139.60.
