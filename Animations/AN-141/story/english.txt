On Friday a man brought home a fish, asking his wife to cook it while he went to his work and the congregational prayer.

Soon a friend invited the wife to a wedding celebration. She wanted to attend, but the fish still had not been prepared.

Instead of cooking the fish, she placed it alive in a jar of water and went to the wedding. She did not return that evening.

The husband searched house after house for her and asked everyone he could think of. A whole week passed before the next Friday arrived.

When she finally returned, her husband angrily asked where she had been. Instead of explaining, she produced the fish from the jar, still alive.

The woman gathered the neighbors and contradicted her husband’s account. He insisted the fish had been brought home seven days earlier.

Seeing the living fish, the neighbors refused to believe it could have survived a week. They sided with the wife, called the husband mad, and imprisoned him.

The husband wept at being disbelieved. The satirical tale ends without justice: evidence the neighbors treated as decisive led them to punish the truthful man.
