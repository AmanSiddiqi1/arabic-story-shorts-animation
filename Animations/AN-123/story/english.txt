A freeborn boy and an enslaved girl studied in the same school. He loved her deeply but did not know how to tell her without attracting his classmates’ attention.

One day, while the other students were distracted, he took her writing tablet and wrote two verses about a heart exhausted by secret love.

She read the poem, understood he meant her, and wept with feeling. Beneath his words she wrote two verses promising kindness to a sincere lover.

Neither noticed their teacher arrive. He read both messages, but instead of becoming angry, he remembered his youth and added verses encouraging her to comfort the boy.

Soon the girl’s owner came to school and found the tablet. He read the boy’s poem, her reply, and the teacher’s advice, understanding their secret.

He did not oppose their union. He added a blessing that the lovers never part, then jokingly rebuked the teacher for acting as such a bold matchmaker.

The owner summoned a judge and witnesses, and the pair were married at once. He arranged a wedding feast and treated the newlyweds generously.

Verses on a little writing tablet had led to marriage. The pair lived happily together until death, as the tale ends, with their teacher part of the story.
