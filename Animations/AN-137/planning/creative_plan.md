## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A fisherman sells a king one fish and, by answering two traps set by the queen with quick wit, walks out with three rewards instead of one.

**Shape.** One room, three rounds, one running total. Each round has the same four steps so the audience learns the pattern and enjoys it being repeated: (1) a challenge, (2) the fisherman thinks, (3) his answer, (4) the king's arm snaps toward the treasury and the servant fetches another sack. The counter DIRHAMS at the top left goes 4000, 8000, 12000.

**Conflict.** Shirin's calculation against the fisherman's presence of mind, with the king as the prize who swings between them. **Surprises:** "both" (79.1), and the coin's portrait (136.9). **Emotional arc:** delight, unease (the whispering), suspense, relief and laughter, a second and sharper danger (the king's anger at 120.2), then warmth. **Ending:** the script reports the king's harsh proclamation about women's advice and immediately says the events showed the fisherman's skill; the picture sides with the second half (section 5). **Moral:** a quick, courteous mind can turn every test into a reward.

## 3. Character acting

- **Khusrau** (`person` costume `caliph`, age 0.45): impulsive and warm. Everything is big and immediate: fingertip kiss, clap, belly laugh (shared `person laugh`: mouth open, lid 0.9, tip -0.12, shoulder shake), stamp. His **order pose** (arm snapped toward the vault) is identical at 17.34, 83.86 and 147.20: setup and two payoffs. Listening to Shirin he strokes his beard (`PERSON_HANDS.chin`) and his gaze drifts up. Anger at 120.22: brow 1, stretch `sq` 1.05 on the rise, a foot stamp with panel-only shake; it melts at 137.58 when he sees his own face on the coin (brow 1 to -0.4 over 0.6 s, a double take between coin and lens). In S18 he is pompous: chin up (`tip` -0.06), lid 0.4, then eyes fully shut while he wags the finger.
- **Shirin** (`person` costume `lady`, `crown: true`): still, upright, economical. She acts with the **fan**: slow fanning (neutral), snap shut on 4000 (20.18), open as a screen for whispering (24.24), tapping on the palm (thinking, 43.4), snap open with the idea (44.08), frozen half open on BOTH! (79.7), raised to the eyes (85.66), lowered when the coin gives her a new opening (107.98), over her whole face on "third" (145.94). Eyes: half lids (lid 0.3 to 0.5), saccades when scheming (`gazePlan(t, plan, 0.07)`), one deadpan look to camera at 165.44. She is never clowned; her defeat is only the fan going up.
- **The fisherman** (`person` new costume `fisherman`): courteous, unhurried, bright. Bows are `tip` 0.3. Under pressure he goes **still** and only the pupils move (67.9, suspense by stillness), then the eyes settle, one brow lifts, and he taps his temple (70.60; repeated at 144.50 and 170.08). His danger beat at 122.4 is a flinch (`sq` 0.9), a "me?" hand on the chest, one gulp on the walk back, then calm returns before he speaks (128.9, `breathe`). Walks always use `walk(FX, t)` + `footfalls`; under a sack he walks bent (`tip` 0.3, short stride, sack swaying a beat late with `swing`).
- **The servant** (`person` costume `servant`): the audience's stand-in. Three trips to the vault, each slower (stride 1.0, 0.8, 0.55), shoulders lower, the coin mound smaller. He looks at his empty palm on لخدم 116.36, gets the rescued coin at 149.5, and peeks round the scroll in S18.
- **Listeners always react**: every speaking beat has the other gazes set with `lookAt`; group reactions are staggered (`stagger(i)`), never in unison. `LIFE.t = t` gives everyone blinks.
- **Secondary motion**: fish tail swing on each footfall, sack squash and rebound on every drop (`impactSquash`, `rebound`), sack sway, hanging lamps swinging after the stamp, coins hopping at the sack mouth.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Rule of three with a meter**: counter, order pose, vault, servant. By the third time the audience is ahead of the picture, which is the joke.
- **The emptying vault**: the cost of the queen's advice is visible without a word; in S18 it stands bare behind the king while he blames her. That is how the film handles the proclamation: the picture agrees with the narrator's last sentence, not with the king.
- **Suspense**: the fisherman frozen with darting pupils (67.9); the slow walk back under the king's anger (123.4); the closed fist before he explains (128.9).
- **Surprise**: two fish icons snapping into one (78.48); the portrait appearing on the coin and the real face arriving beside it (136.9 to 137.6).
- **Size joke**: tiny coin against the giant sack (105.38), turned against him by Shirin's hands (118.56), then turned again when the coin is shown huge in the insert.
- **Setup and payoff**: the loosely tied sack with one coin peeping (92.66) pays off at 97.92; the temple tap three times; the rescued coin ends in the servant's palm, answering Shirin's charge in action.
- **Warmth**: the fisherman helps the servant up (149.5); the final image is a man on a pile of sacks waving, with a sparrow.
