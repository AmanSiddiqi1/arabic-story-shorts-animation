King Khusrau loved fish. When a fisherman brought him a fine large fish, he ordered a reward of four thousand dirhams as Queen Shirin watched.

Shirin thought the payment excessive: courtiers might resent receiving as much as a fisherman. Khusrau replied that a king should not withdraw a promised gift.

Shirin proposed asking whether the fish was male or female, then demanding the opposite kind so the reward could be reclaimed.

Summoned back, the quick-witted fisherman replied that the fish was both male and female. Khusrau laughed and granted him four thousand dirhams more.

The fisherman collected eight thousand dirhams in a sack. As he left, a single coin fell, and he set down the sack to pick it up.

Shirin called him miserly for not leaving one coin to the servants. Angered, Khusrau summoned the fisherman to explain himself.

The fisherman said he had rescued the coin because it bore the king’s image and name, which might otherwise be trampled. Khusrau awarded him another four thousand.

The fisherman left with twelve thousand dirhams instead of four. The old tale ends with the king making a harsh proclamation about women’s advice, while the fisherman’s cleverness had won every test.
