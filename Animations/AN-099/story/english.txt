Caliph al-Maamun loved learning and gathered scholars twice a week to debate difficult questions. At one gathering a stranger who looked poor came in and sat far from the others.

Few noticed him, assuming his simple clothing meant little education. The debate began, and the caliph posed a question that some scholars could not answer clearly and convincingly.

The stranger calmly raised his hand and asked permission to speak. Then he gave a precise answer and explained his reasons in simple words. Al-Maamun was impressed by his understanding and invited him to sit closer to the scholars.

A second question came, and those present disagreed on its meaning. The man spoke again, bringing the evidence together until the meaning was clear. The caliph signaled him to come forward, and he drew the attention of those who had ignored him.

On the third question the stranger showed more knowledge than anyone expected, so al-Maamun invited him to sit beside him. When the debate ended, they sat down to eat, and the forgotten stranger became an honored guest.

The gathering then turned to drinking. The caliph offered his guest a cup of wine, as he did with some of his companions. The man politely declined and did not take the cup, and al-Maamun, surprised, asked him why.

The scholar answered: Commander of the Faithful, neither my clothes nor my wealth brought me near you, but my mind and knowledge. If I drink what takes away my mind, I will ruin the very thing that raised me in your court today.

The caliph was more pleased with this answer than with his learned answers, and awarded him one hundred thousand silver coins. The scholar departed honored, having kept his place through his knowledge, then kept the reason for that place through his mind.
