Caliph al-Maamun loved learning|and assembled scholars twice a week.|One day an unknown man in poor clothing entered|and sat far from the others.
Few noticed him,|assuming his simple clothing meant little education.|The debate began, and the caliph posed a question|that several scholars could not answer clearly.
The stranger asked permission to speak.|His precise, plainly explained answer|impressed al-Maamun,|who invited him to sit nearer the scholars.
A second question divided the gathering.|The stranger weighed the evidence and clarified the answer.|The caliph moved him closer still,|and those who ignored him took notice.
His third answer surpassed expectations.|Al-Maamun invited him beside him.|After the debate, the company ate together,|and the forgotten stranger became an honored guest.
The gathering then turned to drinking.|The caliph offered his guest a cup of wine.|The man politely refused,|and al-Maamun asked him why.
The scholar replied:|It was neither wealth nor clothing that brought me near you, but my mind and knowledge.|Why drink something|that could take away what earned your respect?
The caliph admired this answer even more|and awarded him one hundred thousand silver coins.|The scholar departed honored,|having protected the very reason for his rise.
