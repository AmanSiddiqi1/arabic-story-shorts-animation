An old tale says two brothers brought a young Bedouin before Caliph Umar, accusing him of killing their father in his orchard. Umar asked for an account.

The youth admitted causing the man's death. One of his camels had entered the orchard, and its owner had killed the youth's valuable horse with a stone.

Enraged, the youth threw the same stone at the man and killed him. He admitted his act, and Umar ruled that retaliation was due, as the tale relates.

The youth requested three days: his father had left money for a younger brother, and only he knew where it was. He promised to return after making arrangements for the child.

Umar asked for a guarantor. The youth chose Abu Dharr, a man he had never met. Abu Dharr accepted the responsibility, and the accused was allowed to leave.

Three days passed without the Bedouin. The gathering feared Abu Dharr would be punished in his place, while the dead man’s sons initially refused compensation.

At the last moment the youth returned, exhausted by heat. He had entrusted his brother and the money to relatives. He came back to keep his promise; Abu Dharr explained why he had trusted him.

Moved by the youth’s honesty and Abu Dharr’s generosity, the brothers forgave him. They refused public compensation, and the tale ended with mercy instead of further bloodshed.
