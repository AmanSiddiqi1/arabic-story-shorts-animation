# AN-144 Omar bin al-Khattab and the Young Bedouin: creative plan

Film length 170.4 s (MP3 166.90 s + 3.5 s THE END hold). 16 scenes, 174 actions, 18 lettering items plus the LATER card, 6 sound effects. All times are seconds on the final MP3; every cue is a word start from `planning/inputs/timeline.md`. ## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Conflict.** A young Bedouin has killed a man in a moment of rage. The dead man's two sons want justice; Caliph Umar rules retaliation. The story is not about guilt (he admits it at once) but about whether people keep faith with each other.

**Spine (beats B1-B17 in the JSON).**
1. Setup 0-13.4: two brothers drag the youth into Umar's plain council and accuse him.
2. Account 13.4-25.2: their father went to his orchard and died there (picture balloon).
3. Surprise 27.62: the youth says yes, I did it.
4. Flashback 29-46: his she-camel strays into the orchard; the owner kills the youth's prized horse with a stone (38.38); in rage he throws the same stone back (41.60) and the man dies (44.54). He stares at his own hand.
5. Ruling 49.38: a life for a life.
6. Request 53-69: three days, for an orphan brother and money only he can find; he promises to return.
7. Comic and suspense beat 71.98-75.0: who will vouch? Everybody studies the floor.
8. Surprise 78.08: he points at a stranger, Abu Dharr, who accepts (84.70) and then sits down in the accused's place (90.50).
9. LATER card 91.12. Suspense 91-116: the sun sinks in the doorway, the door stays empty, blood money is refused (114.50).
10. Reveal 118.12: he runs back over the dunes through the heat and bursts in (121.80).
11. Moral in three steps: LOYALTY (133.60), HONOUR (140.84), and after the brothers forgive (149.60), MERCY (154.02).
12. Resolution 155.5-166.6: the treasury money is gently declined; the brothers raise him to his feet. THE END at 166.9.

**Emotional arc.** Anger and grief, a hard ruling, a quiet act of trust, a long anxious wait, a burst of relief, tears, pardon. The ending is warm but bittersweet: the last acting beat (165.98) is the younger brother's downward look for his father.

**Moral.** Loyalty, honour and mercy keep each other alive. Shown, not preached: three word tags collect at the top of the panel, with an empty dashed slot waiting for the third.

## 3. Character acting

- **Youth** (`person` bedouin, age 0): honest, never defiant. Eyes down while accused, level when he speaks. Signature gesture: hand flat on the chest (25.24, 65.18, 87.72). Rage is one short burst (brow 1, `tremble`, 40.0-42.5) followed by the long horrified look at his own hand (44.54). After the run: `person pant` pose, sweat, `tip` 0.14, then a slow straightening (129.36). At the pardon: eyes wide 6 frames, then `kneel` 0 to 1 with `impactSquash`, hands to face, `wet`.
- **Brother A** (hard): leads every move, `brow` 0.8-1, folded arms, sharp gestures with `settleTo`. His face lets go only at 143.56 (swallow, brow to -0.3).
- **Brother B** (soft): always 3-5 frames behind A (`stagger`) at 70 % amplitude, wet eyes from 11.68, looks away first (67.28), hand stops in the air (134.26), cries first (141.96). The pair must never move in unison.
- **Abu Dharr** (`ascetic`, age 0.6): the calm centre. Planted early: at 12.34 and 51.0 he watches the youth while others watch the accusers. He is the only one who meets the youth's eyes (76.2-78.0). Surprise is one raised brow and one blink (80.48). Waiting: seated on the floor, `lid` 0.35, `breathe`, a single glance at the door and a near smile (103.64). He smiles before he turns when the youth returns (122.2).
- **Umar** (`physician` preset, plain palette, age 0.55): grave, economical. Seated on a bench; rises only to rule (48.06) and to offer the money (155.48). Gestures: open palm, beard stroke (68.34), sweep to the door (86.04). Wet eyes at 154.72.
- **Seated men** (elder, merchant, bystander): the chorus. Staggered head turns, the avoidance gag at 72.6-73.9 (floor, sleeve, pillar), shame at 85.48, worry gestures (`cheeks`, `mouthCover`, `headHold`) at 98.56, arms up and a `hop` at 152.0.
- **Animals**: camel `neck`, `chew`, `headTurn`, pacing `walk` with `GAIT.camel`; horse ear flick and `tailSwish` under the youth's hand, then `rear` 0.7 and `lie` 1 with `lid` 1.
- Everyone: `LIFE.t` auto blinks, `lookAt` targets at all times, `noBlink` in the two held silences.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Suspense:** the sinking sun in the doorway, the creeping light shaft, three lit sun discs, the scale tipping toward Abu Dharr, the dashed ghost of the absent youth.
- **Surprise:** the instant confession (brothers' grips loosen); the choice of a stranger; Abu Dharr smiling before he turns.
- **Humour (light, human):** nobody will meet Umar's eye at 72.6-73.9; the same men stare at their laps when a stranger does what they would not (85.48); the second purse (112.30).
- **Emotion:** the horse stroked before it is lost; the boy alone under the palm (63.90); the guarantor sitting in the accused's place (90.50); the offered wrists (131.18); the knees giving way (150.46); B's eyes shut for his father (151.08).
- **Setup and payoff:** the hard NO! at 114.50 is mirrored by the soft, wordless refusal at 160.64; the empty third slot (140.84) is filled by MERCY (154.02); the promise arrow out and back (65.64) is paid off by the run (118.12).

## 8. Warnings and open questions

- Showing Umar and Abu Dharr with faces is sensitive for part of the audience; the default is plain, dignified figures (no crown, no throne). See `owner_questions` for the alternative.
- Violence is softened (flashes, no blood, no body); confirm.
- The English translation is condensed against the Arabic in several paragraphs; both are kept verbatim (a `SCRIPT:` entry is in `owner_questions`).
- Eight figures share the hall: check stills at 8.9, 78.1 and 149.6 for covered heads.
- The front-facing figure has no profile: the throws must read through arm angles, body `tip` and the stone arc; test at phone size.
- Lettering is near the limit; cut I DID IT. and JUSTICE! first if it feels crowded.
- Few pauses in the narration: do not add sound effects beyond the six listed.
