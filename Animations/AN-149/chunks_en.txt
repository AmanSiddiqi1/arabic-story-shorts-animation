A scholar passed a small school|and saw a well-dressed teacher teaching the young children.|The teacher invited him to sit, and the scholar talked with him about language, grammar and poetry,|and was impressed by his precise answers.
The scholar visited repeatedly|and discovered more knowledge each time.|He wondered to himself, for he had thought that children's teachers|did not always combine learning with good thinking.
On a later visit the school was closed.|He asked the neighbors why it was closed,|and they said the teacher was at home, grieving for someone he had lost.|The scholar went to him to comfort him and learn what had happened.
The teacher sat, showing signs of grief.|His guest asked who had died: his father, his brother or a relative?|The teacher said no,|then said he was grieving for a woman he loved.
The scholar tried to calm him, saying there were many women in the world.|but the teacher admitted he had never even seen the woman.|The scholar grew even more astonished,|and asked how he could love someone he had never met.
The teacher said he had heard a man passing under his window|singing about a woman named Umm Amr.|He thought poets praised her only for her great beauty,|so he became attached to her from that day.
Two days later he heard the same man singing|Umm Amr and her donkey had gone and not returned.|The teacher took the words to mean she had died, so he closed the school and put on mourning clothes,|although the song never said so.
The scholar left the house more astonished than before.|He knew the man had memorized much learning,|yet he had built love and grief on two lines of song,|without knowing the truth about the woman or her fate.
