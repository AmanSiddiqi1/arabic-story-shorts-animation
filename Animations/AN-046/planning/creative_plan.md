# AN-046 The Sparrow and the Peacock: creative plan

Film length 176.33 s (MP3 172.83 s + 3.5 s hold). 16 scenes, 175 timed actions. All times are seconds on the final MP3; every cue word and time is in `creative_plan.json` (the machine-readable storyboard, which is the authority for timings, coordinates and Library calls). This file explains the direction.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A careful little sparrow makes his friend the peacock king and becomes his minister. Told to stay beside a hunter's net because nobody escapes his lot, he avoids it faithfully, until his duty as minister sends him down to stop a fight, and the net takes him.

**Conflict.** Caution against fate, and then caution against duty. The sparrow does everything right; the thing that undoes him is the best thing about him.

**Surprises.** The snap of the net in the flashback (63.06); the still beat after he has made peace, when the audience sees the grain and the shadow before he does (132.6-137.5).

**Emotional arc.** Delight (S1-S4), alarm (S5-S6), fear overruled by authority (S7-S8), strained vigilance (S9-S10), pride and resolve (S11-S12), shock (S13-S14), quiet recognition (S15-S16).

**Ending and moral.** No gloating and no harm shown. The badge slips through the bars; the hunter walks into the dusk with the cage; the badge lies in the dust; far away a tiny peacock waits beside an empty place. Moral: neither rank nor caution saves you from what is fated.

## 3. Character acting

- **Sparrow (hero).** Small, quick, sincere. He is always looking at something: the peacock in act one, the net in act two, the fight in act three. Hop steps with `anticipate`/`hop`/`landSquash`; `gazePlan` with a 0.07 ease for scanning; `tremble` and `wet` for fear; `sq` 1.08 and chest out whenever the badge matters (31.82, 117.10). The lookout swivel is taught in S9 and repeated in S11 so its interruption (109.90 head snap) reads. No smile at the decision (125.26): a set face. In the cage: slack wings (`sag` -0.5), slow breathing, a long blink, then the head drop; no flapping.
- **Peacock (king).** Slow, tall, sure of himself. Long-neck acting: `bow`, `neck`, `headRot`, crest stalks that lag the head by 2 frames. The fan is his voice: a snap open with overshoot and feather stagger for showing off (9.76), half open for joy (27.7), a slow three second opening while he lectures (77.32-80.22). **THE POSE** (fan 1, wing to the sky, lid 0.5) at 80.22 is a setup; it returns unchanged inside the thought cloud at 153.34. He is loving, not a villain: the bow at 28.12 and the foot tapping wait at 42.0 (echoed as a silhouette at 171.34) carry that.
- **Flock.** Six Library birds, never in unison (`stagger`), each with one trait: the crow lands heavily, the songbird overshoots, the yellow one ducks, one always arrives late in the row.
- **Big birds (crows).** Greedy and trusting: heads low, tails up, the second copies the first 5 frames late; eyes shut on each swallow.
- **Hunter.** Patient. `sneak` steps in, sly brow -0.4, a look round between every throw of grain; behind the bush he is a feather, then two eyes and four knuckles (suspense by stillness). The throw is anticipation (hidden crouch), full stretch with both arms raised, a 3 frame whip forward and a follow-through step. Afterwards he is unhurried and pleased, never cruel.
- **Companion.** Younger eager copycat with a shorter stride; holds the cage with both hands.
- **Listeners.** Every dialogue shot sets both gazes; the listener nods on stresses and blinks slowly.

## 4. Camera and composition

- World 1000 x 1230 (garden, summit) and 1600 x 1230 (glade); ground y 900. Faces and key action stay above the caption panel; lettering never covers a face.
- Establishing wides open each location (S1, S2 crane up from clouds, S6). Two-shots (`twoShot`) for every exchange between king and minister. A low angle with a slow push for the king's speech (S8) makes the sparrow small. Close-ups for thought (S10, S12, S15).
- S9 pulls out to the whole 1600 width to make "far from it" a visible distance. S13 follows the dive with keys on start and impact, then holds perfectly still for 1.5 s before a whip to the hunter. Panel-only shake at 63.06 and 137.56.
- S16: wide, slow push down to the badge, then a lift to the far mountain; THE END in the upper sky.
- Transitions: wipe (11.0), iris into and out of the flashback (50.3, 64.7), match cut on the flying sparrow (84.4), dissolve to night (99.2), the one LATER card at 107.18 ("ANOTHER DAY..."), cuts elsewhere.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour:** the peacock's "me?" look behind himself (25.10); the latecomer squeezing into the row (19.58); the sparrow's tiny bindle, already packed (68.26); the two enemies hugging under the falling net (137.08).
- **Suspense:** the grain and a taut rope in the flashback; the feather behind the bush from 113.3; eyes in the bush at 120.75; the still beat at 132.6 where only pupils move and a mesh shadow grows.
- **Dramatic irony:** his own thought cloud shows him as peacemaker (122.74) while we have seen the hunter.
- **Setup and payoff:** THE POSE (80.22 and 153.34); the foot-tapping wait (42.0 and 171.34); the lookout swivel (89.66 and 108.84); the badge pinned (31.82), touched with pride (117.10), fallen (161.44), left in the dust (170.48).
- **Emotion:** the long look back at the nest tree (149.0, 167.86); the empty nest swaying at the end.

## 7. Implementation notes for Opus

- Copy `Library/template/`; bake `garden`, `summit` and `glade` once. Glade camera clamps with WW 1600. Set `LIFE.t = t` every frame.
- Cue every action from `timing.json` words; the JSON gives each cue word and its start. Word starts can carry up to 0.4 s of leading silence, so check the four hard hits (27.30 crown, 63.06 snap, 137.56 net lands, 149.90 door) against the audio envelope.
- Sound: narration is nearly continuous. Effects only at 5.0, 7.9, 21.3, 86.9, 120.8 and 172.75 (`SND`, master 0.6). The big hits are silent and carried by lettering and panel shake.
- Motion helpers: `walk` + `footfalls` for the peacock and the men; `hop`, `anticipate`, `landSquash` for every bird hop; `fallY`, `impactSquash`, `rebound` for the crown, the failed flight and the badge; `swing` for the branch, cage and sack; `gazePlan`, `lookAt`, `blink`, `tremble`, `breathe`, `stagger`, `settleTo`, `twoShot`.
- Keep `FATE_POSE` as one constant used at 80.22 and 153.34. Thought clouds call the scene draw functions with a remapped clock.
- Draw order: hunter then bush; net back pass, birds, net front pass; palm, bird, fingers; cage back bars, bird, front bars.
- Reach: person arms are about 74 units, so for 146.40 use lean 0.25 and kneel 0.5 with `handRW`.
- Call `finish` last every frame. Check with stills and a contact sheet before a full render; test renders go to scratch with `--out`.
- When assets move into `Library/`, run `node Library/tests/regression.mjs` (all identical) and rebuild the catalogue.
