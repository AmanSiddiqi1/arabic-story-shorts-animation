A little sparrow visited a peacock every morning and stayed with him until evening. He knew him well and admired him. One day birds gathered on a mountain to choose a king who would settle their disputes and keep order among them.

The sparrow proposed the peacock, and the birds agreed. The peacock thanked his friend and made him a minister close to him. The sparrow looked after the kingdom’s affairs and brought the king food and news every day.

One day the sparrow was late, and the peacock asked him why. He said, “I saw a hunter set a net near my nest and put grain inside it. Soon two large birds went into the net, and he caught them.”

The sparrow told the king he wanted to leave that place for fear of the hunter. But the peacock said, “Do not leave your home. No one can escape his fate, however careful he is.” The sparrow obeyed the king and stayed near his nest.

The sparrow kept watching the place carefully. He avoided the net whenever he saw it and looked for food far from it. Still, he never forgot the two birds the hunter had taken that day.

Another day he saw two sparrows fighting on the ground. He said to himself, “How can I be minister of the birds and let them fight? I must go down and make peace between them before one hurts the other.”

The sparrow quickly flew down between the two fighters, and at that moment the hunter cast his net over all three. The minister tried to fly, but found himself trapped. The hunter came and seized him, then handed him to one of his companions.

The sparrow remembered the peacock’s words and thought of the danger he had feared from the start. Neither his position nor his caution helped him this time. The hunter went off carrying the bird who, a moment before, had been minister to the king of birds.
