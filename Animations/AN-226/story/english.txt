Yunus the Scribe traveled toward Damascus in Caliph Hisham’s reign with an enslaved girl he had taught poetry and music. He needed money to repay debts.

The caravan stopped by a lake. An elegant young rider with two servants approached Yunus, asked for hospitality, and was welcomed.

The visitor enjoyed Yunus’s song and was even more impressed when the young woman improvised and sang verses. He stayed until evening.

Learning of Yunus’s debts, the stranger offered to buy the girl for fifty thousand dirhams plus travel support. Yunus let him take her before paying, though he did not know his name.

As soon as the stranger left, Yunus regretted trusting him. He waited outside Damascus the next day, fearing he might miss the messenger.

At last a servant returned and revealed the buyer was Walid bin Sahl, the heir apparent. Yunus saw the girl safe at his palace, and Walid admitted he had acted hastily.

Walid paid the full price plus fifteen hundred dinars, praised the girl’s training, gave Yunus a horse and mule, and promised more when he ruled.

Yunus later returned when Walid became caliph. Walid kept his promise and enriched him. Yunus remained at court until Walid was killed, as the tale concludes.
