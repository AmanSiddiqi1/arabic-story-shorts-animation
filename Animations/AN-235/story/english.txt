The vizier Abu Amir bin Marwan served King al-Nasir. He received an enslaved Christian boy praised for his beauty. The king saw the boy and expressed admiration.

The vizier sent the enslaved boy as a gift, with verses saying he parted reluctantly to please the king. Al-Nasir rewarded him with wealth and favor.

Later the vizier received a beautiful enslaved woman. Fearing the king would demand her too, he sent her with gifts and compared the two captives to moon and sun.

The king was pleased and drew the vizier closer. But an enemy claimed Abu Amir still longed for the boy and privately regretted giving him away.

The king threatened the slanderer, yet decided to test the vizier. He forged a letter supposedly from the boy asking the minister to request his return.

A page delivered the letter, adding that the king ignored the boy. The vizier recognized a trap: seeking the boy’s return could be used to prove disloyalty.

On the back he wrote that a wise man does not walk into a lion’s den and will not let longing overcome judgment or ask back a royal gift.

The king saw the vizier had understood the test. Asked how he escaped, Abu Amir answered that his judgment was not captive to desire. The king stopped believing slander about him.
