One night Caliph Harun al-Rashid could not sleep, so he asked his vizier Ja’far for a story to make him laugh and ease his worries. Ja’far suggested sending for a man who knew funny tales, named Ali the Persian, so Ali came and sat before the caliph.

Ali recalled traveling with a small leather bag of provisions. While he was shopping in the market of a strange city, a Kurdish man grabbed him and snatched the bag from him, and claimed before the people that he was its true owner.

They appeared before a judge, who asked the claimant what was in the bag. The man began listing reasonable items, then claimed it held two tents, two rugs, a cow and two calves, sheep, camels and wild beasts, and even whole rooms inside the little bag.

Ali was stunned by the exaggeration, but answered with a bigger one. He said his bag held an old house, a school and a garden, the cities of Baghdad and Basra, armies of soldiers, and thousands of people who would testify that it was his.

The judge lost patience and said no one had ever heard such strange claims. He asked the two men: is this bag a bottomless sea or a place where all people gather? He ordered the bag opened before the court.

Ali opened the bag, and no one found a city, an animal or an army in it. Inside there was only bread, a little cheese, olives and a lemon. Ali threw the bag before his opponent and left, leaving the judge to face the absurd dispute.

Harun al-Rashid burst out laughing when Ali reached the end of his story, and gave him a generous gift. So the quarrel over the little bag ended, not with a complicated ruling, but with a simple look inside that exposed two unbelievable claims.
