One sleepless night Caliph Harun al-Rashid|asked Ja’far for an amusing story.|Ja’far brought Ali the Persian,|a man known for entertaining tales.
Ali recalled traveling with a small leather bag of provisions.|At a market,|a Kurdish stranger seized it|and loudly claimed ownership.
They appeared before a judge,|who asked the claimant to describe the contents.|The man listed tents, rugs, cattle,|camels, wild beasts, and even rooms.
Astonished, Ali retaliated with a grander inventory:|houses, a school, gardens,|Baghdad, Basra, armies,|and thousands of witnesses—all supposedly in his bag.
The judge finally asked|whether this was a bottomless sea|or a place containing all humanity.|He ordered the bag opened before the court.
The bag contained no cities or animals—only|bread, cheese, olives, and a lemon.|Ali dropped it before his rival and walked away.
Harun al-Rashid laughed heartily at the ending|and rewarded Ali.|A simple inspection|had exposed both men’s absurd claims.
