# KD-012 The Hare and the Elephants: creative plan

Film length 168.79 s (narration 164.79 s + 4.0 s THE END). 11 scenes, 137 timed actions. All times are seconds on the final MP3. The machine-readable storyboard is `creative_plan.json`; this file explains the direction behind it.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A thirsty elephant herd tramples the hares' burrows on its way to a lake, until one small white hare stands on a rock, calls himself the moon's messenger and lets the king frighten himself with the moon's reflection.

**Conflict.** Big against small, but without a villain. The elephants are thirsty and simply do not look down. The hares cannot fight; they can only think.

**Emotional arc.** Ease (pond) to hardship (drought) to relief (lake) to shock (the crushed home, seen from inside) to fear (the meeting) to nerve (the climb) to wonder (moonrise) to hushed suspense (the lake at night) to comic panic (the vanished moon) to gentleness (trunk and kit) to joy (homecoming).

**Surprises.** The cutaway that shows families under the mounds before the feet arrive; the moon rising exactly behind the hare on the word "moon"; the king undoing himself with his own forgotten trunk.

**Ending and moral.** The elephants leave at sunrise with a salute, the hares rebuild and the counter climbs back to 5. Button after the last word: the sun's disc lies on the lake, the kit pokes it, it wobbles as the moon did, and the hares laugh. Moral (not shown as text): a clear head and a clever word can protect the small from the mighty without a blow.

## 3. Character acting

- **King** (elephant s 0.85, crown). Arc in the eyes and ears: `eyes:'smug'`, `brow -0.6` on arrival; ears flatten and gaze climbs to the moon at 85.48; `lift` frozen at 94.98; humble low head and question-mark `trunk 0.75` at 98.84; reverent at the lake; `noBlink` freeze at 133.1; `tremble`, `wet`, ears back at 137.86; soft half lids with the kit at 152.44. He speaks with the trunk as a hand, `hbob` on stressed words, and the new `talk` jaw.
- **Scout** (s 0.56). Always a beat late (`stagger`), comic trot, tongue out at any picture of water, bumps and skids (`lurch`). **Cow** (s 0.70) is the steady middle: nods, drinks, follows.
- **Hero** (white hare, s 0.60). Still when others shake. Ideas show as saccades (`gazePlan` ease 0.07) then ears snapping up. Every hop has `anticipate`, `hop`, `landSquash`, ears trailing a frame. On the rock: one moment of real fear (`sq 0.92` + `tremble`), then `sq 1.08`. Orator poses with `raise`, `talk`, `brow`. **Mask on, mask off:** the small smile appears only while the king looks elsewhere (100.6, 125.58) and once to the lens at 163.68.
- **Elder** (grey, s 0.62): stooped `tip 0.06`, slow head shakes, the last to hope.
- **Colony** (four tan hares, different sizes) and **kit** (s 0.38): never in unison; fear shrinks in three steps during STOMP (full duck, half duck, ears only); ears droop in a wave at 60.82; the two "witnesses" at 91.42 over-act their sadness, one nudging the other.
- **Gaze rule.** Elephants look up and ahead until 94.98 and down at their feet afterwards; that change is the story. Hares look up at feet in S4, at each other in S5, and at the hero from 62.9.
- **Secondary motion.** Crown lags head moves by 2 frames; tails and ears on separate phases; pebbles and hares hop on `kick(t, FALLS)`; drops fly when the trunk leaves the water.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour.** The cloud that brings nothing; PFFT of dust instead of water; the scout always late; the herd's BONK pile-up; the elephant king tiptoeing between mounds; the over-acting witnesses; the slipped crown; the kit poking the sun at the end.
- **Suspense.** Only the hero hears the first thud (35.8). The foot hangs over B2 for three counted steps with a family underneath. The silent beat before the king bends (120.6 to 121.5). The audience and the hare see the trunk sinking toward the water; the king does not.
- **Surprise.** The cutaway homes; the moonrise; the reflection breaking into slices.
- **Emotion.** The mother and kit looking up at their ceiling; the counter falling 5 to 2 and rising 2 to 5; the king's trunk tip and the kit's nose (152.44); the salute at 155.84.
- **Dramatic irony.** The elephants drink happily with their backs to the ruin (50.38). The moon is perfectly safe in the sky while the king panics at the water.

## 8. Warnings and open questions

1. **Herd size and crown.** The script says "a herd" and "one elephant"; the plan shows three elephants (king, cow, small scout) and gives the king a small crown so he is recognisable from the first shot.
2. **Cloud over the moon (97.72).** Added so the king's question "Where can I see the moon?" makes sense while the moon has just risen; the narration does not mention it.
3. **Hare colours.** The hero is the Library's white hare (the same drawing as the hero of The Lion and the Hare); the colony is tan and the elder grey.
4. **Cutaway versus oblique lake.** Both live in one side view. If they clash on stills, draw the cutaway as an inset panel with its own brush border.
5. **Word timings.** A few word starts carry a long stretch (82.70, 96.52, 109.90); check those cues against the audio on the first stills.
6. **Rig limits.** The elephant has no rear-foot lift (the 142.04 gag uses ears and head) and the hare has no wink (70.34 uses a long blink).
7. **Density.** S7 and S9 carry 16 and 19 actions in about 21 s each; if a still looks crowded, thin the labels before the acting.
