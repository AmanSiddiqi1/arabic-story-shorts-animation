# KD-009 The Monkey and the Carpenter: creative plan

Halftone Pop, 9:16. Narration 162.29 s, THE END held to 165.29 s. Nine scenes, 160 timed actions. All times are seconds on the final MP3; the machine-readable storyboard is `creative_plan.json` (every action there carries its Arabic cue word).

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A curious monkey watches a carpenter all morning, decides the work is easy, pulls out the wedge that holds a sawn log open, and the log snaps shut on his own tail.

**Moral.** Do not meddle with work you do not understand. Watching is not the same as knowing.

**Conflict.** Curiosity and overconfidence against a thing whose purpose the monkey never learned. The film must make the audience learn that purpose (S3) so that they see the danger before he does.

**Emotional arc.** Calm, then amused (the copycat), then mounting dread from 78.7 (the audience can see the tail in the gap), a shock, real sympathy, relief through kindness, and a wry, quiet ending. **Surprises:** the delayed realisation after the snap (he is still smiling); the carpenter is not angry. **Ending:** the opening wide returns with the same eye line, but the monkey is deeper in the leaves with his palms turned away from the tools.

## 3. Character acting

**Monkey (Library `monkey`).** A copycat with a short attention span. Always has a gaze target: the carpenter's hands in S1-S4, the door when guilty, the wedge when greedy, the seam when hurt. Saccades (`gazePlan(t, plan, 0.07)`) when thinking; negative `brow` for curiosity and pleading; `mouth: 'smirk'` for overconfidence. Copies the carpenter two frames late (hammering at 15.9, the one-eyed peer at 33.74, sawing at 50.06) so the final refusal at 155.2 pays off. Every hop has `anticipate` and `landSquash`; the tail follows late (`swing`). Pull: straight arms, `tip` -0.25, `tremble`, red dot shading on the face. After the snap: comedy beat (hold, realise, look to camera `gaze [0, 0.05]`, look down), scream with `sq` 1.12, the clipped leap with `sq` 1.2, then smaller and smaller attempts, and finally stillness with `wet` eyes and a 1-unit tremble. In the rescue he flinches (`lid` 1) and relief arrives as a long breath (`breathe` slowing, tremble keyed to 0). At the end he is small (`sq` 0.93) and hunched.

**Carpenter (person, `villager` costume `carpenter`).** Unhurried, capable, kind. Eyes on his work; he never notices the monkey until the scream. Work has weight: the strike cycle for the mallet, whole-body rocking for the saw, bent knees under the log, `settleTo` on every stop. Tiredness: `sq` 0.96, `tip` 0.08, `lid` 0.5, yawn. In the rescue the reading order of his eyes (tail, wedge, monkey) is his understanding; then slow hands, an open palm, tongue-tip concentration, and he backs away without turning his back. One glance up at the tree and a small smile at 151.4.

**Bird (`songbird`).** The witness: reacts to the monkey's miming, is shaken off the branch, bolts at the scream, and returns to perch on the mallet, the only one who touches a tool at the end.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Suspense by knowledge:** S3 teaches the audience what the wedge does; the arrow at 79.55 shows the tail in the gap. For 26 seconds they wait for what the monkey cannot see. At 83.52 he looks into the gap and still misses his own tail.
- **Humour:** the copycat gags; the thought-cloud fantasy of himself as a master carpenter; the strain face; the triumphant pose with the wedge one second before the snap; the delayed realisation.
- **Surprise:** the snap comes after a held, trembling pause; the carpenter's first reaction is care, not anger.
- **Emotion:** falling effort after the snap (big leap, weak tug, tiny wiggle, stillness) with pain rings that grow; then gentleness and distance.
- **Payoff:** his hand starts to copy the sawing by habit and he stops it himself. The lesson is shown as a choice.

## 8. Warnings and open questions

1. **The snap is silent.** There is no narration pause between 102.48 and 109.26, so no effect can be placed at 105.78 under the sound rule. Question for the user: allow one very short, quiet thud exactly on that word boundary?
2. **Staging liberty:** the split is drawn as a horizontal mouth at the end of the log so that gap, wedge and tail read from the side. A real rip cut would not be visible in a side view.
3. **Monkey reach:** needs the new `armLen`. Fallback: seat him at the very end of the log with his legs either side of the wedge.
4. **No monkey walk cycle:** travel is by hops, the climb by a trunk slide and two hops.
5. **LATER card at 50.9** overlaps the first two words of "after a short while" in the picture for about 0.6 s; subtitles stay visible.
6. **Unnarrated additions** (they do not contradict the script): the bird, the carpenter tapping the wedge back in at 148.8, the bird on the mallet at 160.1.
