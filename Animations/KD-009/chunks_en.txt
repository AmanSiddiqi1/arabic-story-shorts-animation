In a small village, a carpenter worked near the forest.|Every morning he sat outside his house making doors and tables from wood.|A curious monkey watched him from a nearby tree.
One day, the carpenter placed a large log on the ground.|He wanted to split it in two, so he used a saw to make a gap in its middle.|Then he put a small piece of wood inside the gap.
The small piece kept the two halves apart so the carpenter could continue his work easily.|After a while, he grew tired.|He left his tools and went inside to eat and rest.
The monkey climbed down from the tree and approached the log.|“I have watched the carpenter all morning,” he thought.|“This work looks easy. I can do it too!”|Then he sat on the large piece of wood.
The monkey looked at the small piece in the gap and did not understand why the carpenter had put it there.|He reached out, took hold of it, and began pulling it with all his strength.|It moved a little, so he pulled harder.
Suddenly, the small piece came out, and the log closed around the monkey’s tail!|The monkey cried out and tried to jump away, but he could not.|His tail was trapped, and every movement hurt more.
The carpenter heard the cries and hurried back.|He saw the monkey on the log and understood what had happened.|He used a tool to open the gap again, carefully freed the monkey’s tail, and stepped back to let him calm down.
The monkey leaped into the tree and sat among the branches, still hurting.|He watched the carpenter return to his work, but this time he did not try to imitate him.|He stayed at a distance and did not touch the tools.
