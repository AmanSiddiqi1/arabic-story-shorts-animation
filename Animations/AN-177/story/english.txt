A judge left for Jerusalem and entrusted his devout wife to his brother. The brother harassed her, and when she refused, he plotted a false charge.

The brother arranged false witnesses. A ruler sentenced the innocent wife to stoning, but a passerby found her alive and rescued her.

The man and his wife gave her shelter until her wounds healed. They then asked her to care for their child, and she agreed.

Another man stalked her and, after rejection, attacked at night. He killed the child by mistake. The grieving mother falsely blamed the caregiver, who fled.

In a village, the woman spent her money to save a man from execution. He thanked her, built her a small shelter, and brought her food.

She lived in worship, and the tale attributes healings to her prayers. Later, the three people who harmed her suffered serious illnesses.

They arrived seeking her prayers, unaware of her identity. She required honest confessions: false testimony, wrongful blame, and the child’s killing.

She prayed for them and, as the tale recounts, they recovered. Then she uncovered her face, and her husband recognized her. Those who had wronged her asked for forgiveness, and she forgave them.
