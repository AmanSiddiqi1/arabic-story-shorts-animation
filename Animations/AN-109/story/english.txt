Ibrahim bin al-Mahdi told al-Ma'mun that he had gone out for a stroll and stopped in front of a large house after smelling delicious food.

He glimpsed a woman's hand behind a window and forgot about the food. He asked a tailor and learned that the house belonged to a merchant who did not receive strangers.

He followed two men who knew the merchant. Each man thought he was the merchant's friend, while the merchant thought he was their friend, so they all welcomed him.

After a lavish meal, an enslaved singer performed with a lute. Ibrahim admired her music but said something was missing from the performance, offending her and embarrassing the guests.

Regretting his remark, Ibrahim took a lute and performed beautifully. The singer apologized, the guests praised his skill, and eventually he was left alone with the host.

The merchant pressed for his guest’s identity and learned he belonged to the caliphal family. Ibrahim admitted that both the food and the hand behind the window had drawn him in.

The merchant called the women of his household in turn. When his sister finally appeared, Ibrahim recognized her hand. The merchant immediately offered her in marriage and brought witnesses.

They married with a generous settlement and lavish gifts. Ibrahim told al-Ma’mun that their son now stood before him. The caliph admired the merchant and brought him into his service.
