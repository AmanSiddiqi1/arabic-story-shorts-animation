After pilgrimage, Abdullah bin Maamar heard a young man reciting mournful poetry near the Prophet’s Mosque in Medina. He asked why the young man was weeping.

The young man, Otbah, said he had met a woman at a mosque. She hinted at affection and departed, leaving him unable to find her.

The next day the women returned without her. They told Otbah that her name was Rayya and that her father had taken her to Samawah. Abdullah offered his wealth to help.

Abdullah gathered a party of Ansar and traveled with Otbah to propose marriage. Rayya’s father received them but angrily opposed the match.

Rayya suggested an enormous dowry to discourage the visitors. Her father demanded gold, silver, cloth, and perfume, but Abdullah promised everything.

The party brought the dowry from Medina, celebrated the marriage, and stayed forty days. Then the newlyweds departed with generous gifts.

A day from Medina, raiders attacked. Otbah fought to protect the caravan but was mortally wounded, despite help arriving.

Rayya fell beside her dead husband and died of grief. They were buried together. Seven years later Abdullah found a tree over the grave, called the Bride and Bridegroom’s Tree.
