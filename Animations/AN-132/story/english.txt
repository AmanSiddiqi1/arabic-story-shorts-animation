A simple man walked along leading his donkey by a rope. Two tricksters saw him, and one promised his partner he could steal the animal without its owner noticing.

One trickster quietly unfastened the donkey and gave it to his partner. He looped the rope around his own neck and followed the unsuspecting man.

The man kept walking until the trickster stopped. Pulling the rope did nothing. When he turned, he saw a man where his donkey should have been and demanded an explanation.

The trickster claimed he had once been human but beat his mother while drunk. Her curse had turned him into a donkey, he said, for many years.

He said his mother had finally forgiven him, restoring his human form. The simpleton believed the tale, apologized for riding him, and released the thief.

At home he told his wife the extraordinary story. She believed him too and gave charity, fearing they had unknowingly mistreated a human being.

Days later his wife asked him to buy another working donkey. At the market he recognized his own animal among those offered for sale.

The man whispered angrily to the donkey that it must have beaten its mother again. He refused to buy it, never realizing the tricksters had fooled him.
