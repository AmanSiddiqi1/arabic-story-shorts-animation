A pilgrim slept so long that his caravan left without him. Lost in the desert, he searched for shelter.

He found a simple tent, an elderly woman at its entrance, and a sleeping dog. Hungry from his journey, he asked for food.

The woman said the available food was snakes from a nearby valley. She asked him to catch some, but he was frightened and had never eaten them.

She went to the valley with him, caught snakes herself, and roasted them. His hunger overcame his reluctance, and he ate.

She directed him to a spring. Its water was bitter, but his thirst forced him to drink. He asked why she chose such a difficult life.

The woman asked about his homeland. He described spacious houses, sweet water, fresh fruit, and abundant food.

She asked whether their ruler ever oppressed people, seized property, or expelled families. The traveler admitted that such things could happen.

She answered that fine food under tyranny was like poison, while coarse food enjoyed in freedom and safety was medicine. He understood her lesson.
