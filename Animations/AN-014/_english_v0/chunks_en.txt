Two men lived in one neighborhood.|One was loved for his good character.|His neighbor envied this affection|and wished he would lose everything he had.
The kind man tried to avoid trouble,|but his neighbor’s envy did not stop.|Eventually he left town and settled far away,|opening a house of worship where people came for advice.
Hearing that his neighbor had become famous,|the envious man visited pretending friendship.|He asked to speak privately near an old well.|As the man approached it, the envier pushed him inside and fled.
The man did not die in the well.|At night he heard strange voices beneath the ground.|They belonged to jinn who knew that the sultan’s daughter was ill because of a possessing spirit,|and knew the cure.
One jinn said the treatment required seven white hairs from the tail of a black cat.|The man remembered the words.|In the morning his followers searched for him,|heard his voice, and pulled him out.
Later the sultan arrived seeking help for his daughter.|The man took the hairs from the cat and burned them|as he had heard in the well.|The spirit left the princess, and she recovered before her father’s eyes.
The delighted sultan married his daughter to the man.|When the vizier died, the man became vizier;|after the sultan’s death,|he became king of the country where he had arrived as a stranger.
One day the king recognized his former neighbor among the people.|The envier realized he faced the man he had pushed into the well and feared punishment.|But the king ordered gifts and money for him|and let him leave safely.
