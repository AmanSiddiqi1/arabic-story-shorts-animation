A miller lived with his wife and worked each day at a mill turned by a donkey. He loved her, but she was drawn to a neighbor who kept his distance.

In a dream someone told the miller to dig at a particular place along the donkey’s circular path, where money lay buried. He told his wife and urged secrecy.

Instead, the wife told the neighbor she loved. They agreed to dig at night and, while the miller slept, uncovered a hoard of coins in the mill.

She suggested splitting the treasure, leaving their spouses, marrying, and combining their shares. But the neighbor wanted all the money and feared she would leave him.

She refused to surrender her share. Driven by greed, the neighbor killed her, threw her body into the empty pit, took the coins, and fled before dawn.

The miller woke to find his wife missing. At the mill he harnessed the donkey, which stopped in fright at the body in the pit. The miller did not understand.

He shouted and beat the donkey, but it would not move. Angry, he repeatedly stabbed it until it died, unaware that it had been terrified rather than stubborn.

At daylight the miller found his wife dead in the pit, his donkey dead beside it, and the treasure gone. The neighbor had escaped with the money.
