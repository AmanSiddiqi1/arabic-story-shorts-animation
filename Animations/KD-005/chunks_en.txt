A raven lived in a tall tree, with a nest among its branches.|Whenever his chicks hatched, a black snake came from a nearby hole|and climbed the tree to eat them.|The raven was sad and did not know how to protect his nest.
One day, his friend the jackal saw him sitting alone.|“What is making you sad?” he asked.|“That snake takes my chicks,” the raven said.|“I will wait until she sleeps and then peck her eyes with my beak!”
“Do not do that!” said the jackal.|“The snake is fast and dangerous, and she could hurt you.|Strength is not always the answer. Listen to my plan first.”|The raven moved closer and listened carefully.
The jackal said, “Find a valuable necklace in the village|and take it where people can see you. Fly slowly so they follow you,|then drop the necklace beside the snake’s hole.|They will come to retrieve what they lost.”
In the morning, the raven saw a woman washing clothes near the river.|A golden necklace glittered on a rock beside her.|He swooped down, picked it up in his beak, and flew a short distance away.
The woman cried out, and several people ran after him.|The raven did not fly very high. He stayed close,|moving from one tree to another.|They could see the shining necklace all along the way.
The raven reached the snake’s hole and dropped the necklace at its entrance.|The people rushed over and saw the black snake come out.|They killed the snake, took back the necklace, and returned home.
The raven watched from high in the tree.|One day passed, then another, and the snake never returned.|Before long, the nest was full of new chicks,|and their little voices filled the branches again.
