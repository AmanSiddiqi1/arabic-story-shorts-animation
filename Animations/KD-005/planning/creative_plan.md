# KD-005 The Raven and the Snake: creative plan

Film length **163.3 s** (MP3 159.30 s + 4.0 s THE END). 15 scenes, 160 timed actions. All times are seconds on the final MP3; cue words and times come from `planning/inputs/timeline.md`. The machine-readable storyboard is `creative_plan.json` (every action, cue, camera key and asset); this file explains the intent.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Conflict.** A small bird against an enemy he cannot beat by force. The snake is not afraid of him: she takes his chicks whenever they hatch and slides home unhurried.

**Arc.** Tender (the nest) -> dread (the climb) -> grief (the empty nest) -> anger (his peck plan) -> correction (the jackal: she is faster than you) -> sly delight (the real plan) -> comic chase -> one sharp scare at the burrow -> release -> quiet joy.

## 3. Character acting

- **Raven** (`bird {kind:'crow'}`). His body is the emotional gauge of the film. Grief: `crest` flat, `sag -0.6`, `drop 8`, `wet`, `lid 0.35`, `sq 0.9`. Anger: crest up, lids narrow, `tip 0.08`. Fear: `sq 0.85`, `tremble`. Purpose (from S9): tidy landings, tall stance, saccade scans (`gazePlan(…, 0.07)`). Every landing uses `landSquash`, every take-off `anticipate`. He mimes his own plan (sleep, creep, two beak jabs on 41.32 and 41.92) with the bird mime set. In the chase he looks **back** while flying forward; that look is what tells the audience he is leading them.
- **Jackal** (`fox {pal:'jackal'}`). Calm and economical: ears move before the head, lids half, one gesture per sentence (stop paw 46.54, temple tap 54.28, beckon 55.94, whisper paw 61.06). He never gloats; his last act is a small nod.
- **Snake** (new). No limbs, so: speed (always slow, except the 3-frame strike in the cloud), the tongue, one cold half-lidded eye, and the way the body pours. Her unhurried exit at 22.14 is what makes the raven helpless.
- **Woman.** Work rhythm from one phase value (2 strokes a second), humming with half lids; the brow wipe at 96.12 is the suspense beat. Scream = full stretch with arms up, then point and wave.
- **Villagers.** Three temperaments: man1 bold and first, the woman furious and second, man2 puffing and last (holds his cap, hands on knees, never gets to stop). Use `stagger()` on every group reaction; they never move in unison. At 131.44: hold 6 frames, then a 3-frame recoil with `settleTo`.
- **Chicks.** `nestDraw` `ck` array: each has its own first look; beaks shut one by one when the shadow falls (16.36).
- Listeners always react: jackal ears fold at the tear (37.12); raven deflates at SNAP!; the raven's reaction is visible in every S12 and S13 frame.

## 4. Camera and composition

- World 1000 x 1230, ground y 900. Faces and key action stay above y 1000; the caption panel is never touched (panel-only shake in S13).
- **Vertical story.** The tall tree is used as an axis: nest at the top, burrow at the bottom. S1 cranes up; S2 tilts up with the snake; S3 pulls out along the diagonal; S14 joins the two with a dashed eye-line; S15 cranes out to hold both.
- **Screen direction.** Home is to the right of the village; the chase runs left to right; villagers enter the home set from the left; the burrow is on the right third.
- **Two-shots** for all dialogue (S4 to S7) with `twoShot`; thought clouds sit above and to the side, never over a face.
- **Push-ins on decisions:** raven's eye at 91.7, the hand and the snake at 130.4, the burrow cobweb at 148.7.
- **Mirror framings:** S2 and S15 nest close are identical on purpose.
- Transitions: wipe at 23.3 ("one day"), zoom into the plan at 63.4, dissolve to morning at 81.4, whip into home at 120.4, the single **LATER...** card at 150.4 with the camera cut hidden at its midpoint.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Suspense:** the burrow is on screen, silent, before the first word about it; eyes in the dark three times (10.42, 51.7, 126.9); the brow wipe beside the frozen thief.
- **Mercy cuts:** both deaths are out of sight (sky at 17.0; fight cloud at 134.4), which keeps the film suitable for children and keeps the picture clean.
- **Humour:** the raven's heroic toy fantasy against the jackal's replay; man2 arriving as the others leave (rule of three at the trees); the staff swipe that misses by a feather; the raven shaking the necklace at them.
- **Dramatic irony:** the audience knows what is in the hole while the hand reaches (130.0 to 130.9).
- **The plan is the picture:** S8 needs no extra words; three numbered chips and arrows carry it.
- **Emotion:** the worm falling from the open beak (18.9); the wing spread over an empty nest (21.6); the first smile only at 149.9.
- **Counters as bookends:** CHICKS 3 -> 0 in S2, 1, 2, 3 in S15.

## 7. Implementation notes for Opus

- Start from `Library/template/`; cue everything with `w(p, n)` from `timing.json`. Land actions 2-3 frames after the word start unless anticipation is specified.
- Home anchors: trunk x 560; nest fork (500, 345); low branch tip (430, 690); lookout twig (600, 215); root stone (395, 862); burrow (860, 890); jackal mark x 200, s 0.8. Keep them in one constants block.
- Raven s 1.2 at home, 1.0 at the river, 0.5 in the plan board. `LIFE.t = t` each frame; hand blinks where `lid` is set; a gaze target at all times (`lookAt`, `gazePlan`).
- Walks and runs: one `kfn` position function per walker into `walk()` and `footfalls()`; `dustBurst` on footfalls; `lurch` on the group stop at 129.9; `stagger(i)` for all group reactions.
- Flight: Bezier paths (the Lark `flyBz` pattern); flap rate from speed; the necklace hangs from the beak tip and uses `swing()` with a kick on each direction change.
- Falls: `fallY` + `rebound` for the worm (18.9) and the necklace (124.8 and in the plan board).
- Snake: draw order is burrow back, snake, burrow front lip; on the trunk, split the helix into behind and in-front segments.
- Style primitives: `S.label` + `S.arrow` (THE NEST, THE SNAKE, JACKAL, GOLD!, RIGHT HERE, NO SNAKE!), `S.counter` (CHICKS, TREE, DAY), `S.box` step chips 1-3, `S.emphasis` (temple tap, cloud turning red), `S.transition` (LATER...), `S.title` (THE END), `S.finish` last.
- Sound: nine effects only, all in pauses: 6.75 chime, 17.75 thud, 23.1 whoosh, 63.1 chime, 71.3 pop, 78.4 thud, 120.1 whoosh, 127.25 chime, 159.5 chime. `master: 0.6`.
- Lettering: 25 items including labels and counters; minimum 44 px headlines and 28 px labels; none over a face.
- After the Library additions (fox `pal`, person costume and `kneel`, optional bird `carry`): defaults unchanged, `node Library/tests/regression.mjs` must report "all identical", rebuild the catalogue, update the Capabilities Guide.

## 8. Warnings and open questions

1. **Snake effort.** The only large new drawing. Priority order: path, `out`, wave, `rear`, `limp`, tongue; `coil` and `strike` appear only inside the small cloud.
2. **Small people in the plan board** (s 0.45, about 110 px) are at the legibility floor; check a phone-size still and swap to bold icons if needed.
3. **River set positions** (roof ridge, right-bank ground height) are read from the catalogue image, not measured.
4. **"Several people"** is staged as three. Add a fourth only if the chase looks thin.
5. **Carrying the dead snake away** (137.6) is my staging choice to explain the empty burrow. Open question for the client: keep it, or leave the snake behind the mound and remove it under the day cycles.
6. **Day cycles are fast** (two in 3.7 s) because the narration allows no more; everything else in the frame holds still.
7. The **LATER... card** overlaps spoken words (150.73 to about 152); it is picture only, and subtitles remain readable.
