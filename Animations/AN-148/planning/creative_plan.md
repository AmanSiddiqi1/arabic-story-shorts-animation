# AN-148 The Devotee Prince: creative plan

Film length 177.42 s (MP3 173.42 s + 4.0 s THE END hold). 19 scenes, 162 timed actions, 20 lettering items, 6 sound effects. All times are seconds on the final MP3; every cue word and time is in `creative_plan.json` (this file explains the intent).

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** Harun al-Rashid's sixteen-year-old son gives up the palace for a wool robe and a bricklayer's wage in Basra, and dies unknown on bare earth with a royal ruby still unspent in his pocket.

**Conflict.** Not father against son as enemies, but two ideas of worth: the crown (father, courtiers) and the plain grave (son). The son never argues; he answers with actions (a bird, a walk, a brick).

**Spine.**
- Setup (0 to 21.8): a prince ill at ease among gold; in the graveyard he sees that a king's grave and a rich man's grave look like everyone's.
- Inciting (21.8 to 36.3): his father finds him in coarse wool; the courtiers wince; he is scolded.
- Surprise (36.3 to 51.1): the bird he calls comes to his hand and will not go to the caliph's. This is the film's one light, almost comic beat: the royal hand left empty.
- Turn (51.1 to 63.2): he reads a sign, lets the bird go, walks out of Baghdad. LATER card in the 1.18 s pause at 62.04.
- Middle (63.2 to 105.3): Basra. One dirham and one daniq, work stops for prayer, the small coin feeds him and the big coin is given away. Abu Amir admires him, tries to pay double and is gently refused: warm humour of a generous man who cannot give his money away.
- Suspense (105.3 to 113.8): third Saturday, an idle trowel, an empty place at the wall.
- Emotional core (113.8 to 134.3): the hut, the brick pillow, two requests, a quiet death.
- Reveal (134.3 to 152.4): the ruby in the wool robe; Harun knows it as the mother's gift (memory inset).
- Resolution and moral (152.4 to 173.2): the parents weep; the caliph kneels at a grave exactly like the others, the ruby glowing in his palm: wealth owned and never used.

**Emotional arc.** Unease, calm resolve, quiet contentment in work, worry, tenderness, grief, a still ending. No jokes after 105 s.

**Visual motifs that carry the meaning.** (1) Identical graves: seen in a thought cloud at 16.7, for real at the end. (2) The white dove: comes to wool at 43.9, leaves the hut at 132.1, settles on the headstone at 172.8. (3) Brick: the wall is the clock of the middle act, then one brick is his pillow. (4) Big coin and small coin against the single red ruby.

## 3. Character acting

- **Prince.** Stillness is his character. Small steps (amp 0.6 to 0.8), steady pupils, `lid` 0.3, soft smile; he never squashes under the scolding (35.08) while everyone else in the frame does. Gestures are small and exact: an open palm to refuse (7.30, 92.7, 99.74), two fingers for "only a little" (69.52). Costume change: `prince` in S1 and S2 (crown in his hand in S2, laid on the ground at 19.16), new `devotee` wool robe from S3. Sick: `lie: 1`, `lid` 0.45 to 0.7, slow `breathe()` that fades to zero at 129.3 with `lid` to 1.
- **Caliph.** Big, proud shapes at first: chin up, finger wag with `anticipate()` (33.6), confident palm (48.08). His arc is in the hands: the empty palm whose fingers curl (49.96), the trembling hand that takes the ruby (145.9, `tremble`), the flat hand on the grave (162.80), the palm that opens and closes on the gem (165.62, 172.28). Weeping: `wet`, `teardrops()`, `lid` 0.85, turban tipping forward; kneel with `landSquash` at 162.2.
- **Abu Amir.** Round, quick, generous: wave, belly bounce, turban scratch (100.6). Admiration is played as a slow nod with `lid` 0.3 and a finger run along the mortar line (90.50). In the hut he is the audience's eyes: `wet`, one tear at 124.5, shoulders shaking at 130.94, gaze following the dove.
- **Courtiers.** Never in unison (`stagger`): one whispers behind a hand (`cupMouth`), the other points flat-handed with `lid` 0.5; both look up a beat after the caliph (39.5) and gape at 45.9.
- **Dove.** `bird {kind:'dove'}`: head cocks, `sq` 0.85 crouch before the flight, `landSquash` on the hand with the hand dipping 10 units, the lean toward the royal hand and back (48.7), `turnFlip` and `puff` 0.4 for the snub.
- **Listeners react.** Servant looks to the throne (7.78); foreman shrugs at the two little coins (73.1); beggar's eyes go `wet` (82.26); the vizier bows his head at the edge of the frame in S17.
- Everyone has a gaze target each frame (`lookAt`), and `LIFE.t = t` gives blinks.

## 4. Camera and composition

- Faces and hands stay between y 560 and 860 of the world; lettering sits in the top band; nothing important below y 930.
- Establishing wides open each new place (S1, S2, S7, S9, S12, S19); two-shots for the exchanges (S3, S10, S13, S17); push-ins on decisions and reveals: the son (2.7), the sign (51 to 56), the coins in the hands (96.4), the brick (119.0), the pocket (126.4), the ruby (140.0, 166.7).
- One tilt up to the battlement and down with the bird (38.3 to 44.8): the only vertical move, saved for the film's surprise.
- Track shots on `desertRoad` (S6 right, S16 left, S18 right): direction of travel is consistent (Basra is screen right, Baghdad screen left).
- The reveal in the hut is by push (115.5): door first, then the figure on the floor.
- Last shot: push to gem and headstone in one frame (167.6), then a slow pull-out to the widest frame of the film under THE END.
- Transitions: cuts, one LATER card (62.0 to 63.6). The Saturday jumps are a sky sweep and labels.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour (gentle, first half only):** the caliph's lurch at the sight of wool; the bird's snub and the slowly curling royal fingers; the foreman's shrug; Abu Amir staring at the fist he was not allowed to empty.
- **Suspense:** S11 opens on the trowel alone; the empty place at the wall is held in the centre of the frame before anyone speaks.
- **Surprise:** the red glint through the wool at 127.0 plants the ruby 13 s before it drops into the palm.
- **Emotion:** the brick insert; the hand that stops the cloak; the lamp going out; the memory inset of a small boy hugging the same gem; a caliph alone on a horse behind a donkey.
- **Moral without words:** the son's grave cannot be told from the others, and the ghost icons of what the ruby could have bought fade without being used.
