Caliph Harun al-Rashid had a sixteen-year-old son who was uneasy with palace life. He visited graveyards and reflected on how even rulers ended there.

One day Harun saw his son in coarse wool. Courtiers considered the sight embarrassing, and the caliph reproached him. The youth pointed to a bird.

The bird came to the prince’s hand but refused his father’s, according to the legend. The youth decided to leave Baghdad and its riches.

In Basra he worked building walls for one dirham and one daniq, pausing for communal prayers. He ate with the daniq and gave the dirham as charity.

A man named Abu Amir hired him to repair a wall and admired his discipline. He offered extra wages, but the young worker refused and returned the following Saturday.

On the third Saturday Abu Amir found the youth ill in a hut near the graves, resting his head on a brick. The youth asked him to bury him and deliver something to his father.

The youth died. Abu Amir buried him and found a precious ruby sewn into his clothing. In Baghdad Harun recognized it as a gift from the boy’s mother.

Both parents grieved. Harun traveled to the grave in Basra with Abu Amir. The unused ruby became a reminder of the riches the prince had renounced.
