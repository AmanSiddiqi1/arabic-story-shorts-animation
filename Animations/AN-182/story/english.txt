A pious traveler heard of a blacksmith who handled glowing iron without injury. He observed the marvel and stayed three nights, but noticed no unusual worship.

Asked about the fire, the smith confessed he once pursued an enslaved woman who rejected him. Then famine struck the town.

The starving woman begged for food. He demanded intimacy as payment, and she refused. He exploited her hunger instead of helping.

Two days later she returned exhausted. He offered food only under the same coercive condition, and she left it untouched.

She returned a third time, barely able to speak. While preparing food, he recognized the cruelty of his conduct, repented, and fed her unconditionally.

She prayed that he be forgiven and spared harm from fire if his repentance was sincere. The legend says a coal fell on him without burning him.

The visitor learned the marvel followed repentance. Yet the smith’s regret does not erase his harm; the story warns against exploiting those in need.
