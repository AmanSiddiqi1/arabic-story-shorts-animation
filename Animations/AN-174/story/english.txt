A moral tale tells of a tyrannical king of Israel sitting on his throne when a fearsome stranger entered without seeking permission.

The king demanded to know who had admitted him. The stranger said no guard could bar the one sent by the true Master of the house.

The stranger said no ruler could escape him. Recognizing the Angel of Death, the king fainted and then regained consciousness in terror.

The king begged for one day to repent and return the money he had taken unjustly. The Angel said his appointed lifetime was over.

He asked for just an hour. The Angel replied that even that hour had passed unnoticed; only his final breath remained.

The king asked who would accompany him to his grave. Only his deeds, good or evil, would go with him, the Angel answered.

The king admitted he had neglected good deeds. The Angel warned him of the consequences of his injustice and took his life; he fell from the throne.

The court mourned, but none could restore even an hour. The tale warns against postponing repentance and restitution until life is over.
