On his travels, the tale says, Iskandar met a poor people who owned no gold or silver, ate plants, and kept graves near their doors.

Iskandar summoned their leader, who refused because he needed nothing from the conqueror. Curious, Iskandar went to meet him himself.

The leader explained that no amount of wealth satisfies greed. Their nearby graves reminded them daily that life would end.

Asked why they ate only plants, the leader said he did not want his stomach to become a grave for animals; the pleasure of eating was brief.

The leader showed two skulls: one, he said, had belonged to a tyrant who oppressed the weak; the other to a just and caring ruler.

He asked Iskandar which kind of king he wished to be. Moved to tears, Iskandar invited him to become his minister.

The leader refused. Great possessions brought Iskandar enemies, he said, while contentment left him at peace with others.

Iskandar embraced him and departed. The encounter ended without gifts or office: a man with almost nothing considered himself rich in contentment.
