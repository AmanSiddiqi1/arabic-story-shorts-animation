Abu Hasan was a wealthy merchant in Yemen whose wife had died young. Friends persuaded him to remarry, and he arranged a grand wedding feast.

Guests filled the house, fine food was served, and the bride dressed beautifully. Abu Hasan ate far too much before being summoned to meet her.

As he rose solemnly, Abu Hasan unexpectedly passed wind with a loud noise. The guests pretended not to hear and began talking to their neighbors.

Overwhelmed by embarrassment, he could not face his bride. Pretending to step out briefly, he saddled his horse and fled into the night.

He reached the coast and sailed to India. Arab traders introduced him to the local king, who trusted him and appointed him captain of his bodyguard.

Ten comfortable years passed, but homesickness drew him back. He returned secretly to Yemen in plain clothing and approached his old town cautiously.

Near a house, he heard a girl ask her mother when she was born. Her mother answered: on the night Abu Hasan passed wind at his wedding.

Abu Hasan realized his embarrassment had become a local way of marking time. He fled back to India and lived in self-imposed exile until his death.
