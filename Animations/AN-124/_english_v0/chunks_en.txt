The poet al-Mutalammis fled King al-Numan|and remained away for a long time.|His wife Umaymah waited anxiously|until people began to believe he was dead.
Umaymah loved him deeply|and refused to remarry.|Her relatives urged her to begin anew,|arguing that years without news made further waiting hopeless.
Many suitors came.|Finally she agreed reluctantly,|and the family arranged a wedding.|Songs and drums sounded through the camp on the appointed night.
On that very night al-Mutalammis returned|and saw the celebration.|Children told him that Umaymah,|the wife of the man everyone thought dead, was marrying someone else.
Shocked and hurt, the poet entered unnoticed among the guests.|He saw Umaymah grieving beside her new groom,|and she began to recite a sorrowful verse.
Umaymah wondered aloud in verse|where her absent husband might be.|Al-Mutalammis answered with his own verse:|he was close at hand and had never forgotten her.
Umaymah recognized her husband’s voice.|The new groom understood at once|and, without quarreling, spoke graciously|and withdrew, leaving the reunited couple alone.
After an absence everyone mistook for death,|al-Mutalammis and Umaymah were together again.|The wedding night became the night of their reunion.
