# AN-124 Al-Mutalammis and His Wife Umaymah: creative plan

Film length 171.61 s of narration + 4.5 s THE END = 176.1 s. 16 scenes, 166 timed actions. All times are seconds on the final MP3; every cue word and time is in `creative_plan.json` (the authority for timing). This file explains the direction.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A poet who fled a king comes home after years, on the very night his wife, believing him dead, is being married to another man. A verse answered by a verse reunites them, and the new groom steps aside with grace.

**Conflict.** Not person against person: love against absence and rumour. The only antagonist (the king) is on screen for 2.5 s. The pressure on Umaymah comes from people who love her; the "rival" turns out to be the most generous person in the story.

**Emotional arc.** Fear (S1), patient worry (S2), warm loyalty (S3), gentle pressure (S4-S5), a joyless feast (S6), hope then a blow (S7-S8), held breath (S9-S11), release (S12-S13), a second held breath and a kind surprise (S14), rest (S15-S16).

**Three running objects** carry the story without words: the **tally post** (years of waiting: set up 10.94, counted 41.62, shown to him 153.84), the **verse scroll** (her keepsake: 21.6, tucked in her sash 54.1, falls open 130.86) and the **red heart** (exactly three times: 24.66, 128.56, 168.78). The **X on his portrait** is drawn at 19.66, repeated by the children at 82.46 and falls off at 155.64.

**Moral.** Faithful love outlasts absence and rumour, and true generosity is knowing when to step aside.

## 3. Character acting

- **Umaymah** (`person` costume `lady`): the still centre. Vocabulary: far gaze with slow saccades along the dune line; hands clasped or on the scroll; `lid` 0.4-0.5 and `wet` for sorrow (never open crying); `sq` for breath (the sigh at 103.60 is `sq` 1.04 held, then 0.95, settle). Her refusal has a 2-frame lean toward the suitor before the turn away (anticipation); her consent has a head lift (she almost refuses) before the drop. She smiles for the first time at 132.00.
- **Al-Mutalammis** (`bedouin`, `age` 0 then 0.35): on the camel he looks back (gaze only). From 88.5 to 131.5 his face is covered (`faceCover`), so everything is **eyes, brow, head height and hands**: frozen pupils under the singer's stare, `tremble` under her verse, a checked hand, chest filling before he speaks (`anticipate`). One look to camera only (82.46).
- **The groom** (`merchant`, green and yellow): introduced at 45.14 as the one suitor who does not push. Laughs and claps at the feast (person laugh), shy when he offers the flower. Understanding = three saccade snaps (132.74). The unreadable face at 136.50 and the hand into the sash at 137.08 create the fear; the flower pays it off. He exits upright and unhurried: dignity, not defeat.
- **Parents:** father stooped with a stick (taps mark the years), mother all hands. They arrive 6 frames apart and lean in from both sides; relief at 50.86 while her face stays closed.
- **Children:** boy bold (hops on each stress), girl shy and sharp: she looks from the portrait to the stranger (80.0), tugs a sleeve (84.22), and gets her "told you" at 165.9.
- **Guests and drummers:** never in unison (`stagger`); drummerB half a beat behind; heads turn one after another at 107.3 and 123.3; all lean back at 137.08 and release at 138.98.
- **Camel:** stretched neck and flat ears in flight; tired pace on return; `kneel` with a grumble; chews by the fire; the comic button at 170.44.
- Everyone has a gaze target in every shot; `LIFE.t` auto-blinks except in held shocks (`noBlink` at 85.9 and 127.5).

## 4. Camera and composition

- One continuous world for S2-S16 (camp, 1600 wide): **left = the past and the exit** (small tent, tally post, drummers, the groom's way out), **centre = the tent and the two cushions**, **right = the horizon, the fire, the way he comes home**. Umaymah always looks right; he always travels right to left toward her.
- S1 tracks with the camel and lets the king fall off the left edge. The LATER card hides the change of set.
- Push-ins on decisions: the tally (41.5), the nod (47.4-50.0), her wet eyes (97.5), the groom's understanding (133.8). Pull-outs for release: S14, S16.
- S11 is one composed irony: the question cloud above, the answer standing under it. S12's pan follows the ribbon so the camera move *is* his voice reaching her.
- `twoShot()` for S10, S13 and S15; never crop the listener. Faces stay above the caption panel: seated heads at world y about 700-760, so keep camera y near 700-740 at zoom 1.3-1.9.
- Lettering and balloons sit over sky or tent roof, never on a face. No shake anywhere.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Suspense:** the singer's stare (91.28-92.30); the groom's hand going into his sash (137.08).
- **Surprise:** the keffiyeh rising behind the dune (66.48); the flower instead of a blade (137.80).
- **Dramatic irony:** the children announce his death to his face (82.46); her WHERE? cloud hangs over his head (S11).
- **Humour (light):** he pats himself to check he is alive; the girl who guesses first; the camel joining the song.
- **Emotion by contrast:** one still figure in a drumming picture (63.68, 99.28); half a second of dimmed lamps on "had seemed sad" (166.74).
- **Setup and payoff:** tally, scroll, heart, X, flower (see section 1).
