# AN-124 Al-Mutalammis and His Wife Umaymah: Fable review, round 1

Reviewed: `renders/AN-124_v1.mp4` (176.1 s) through all 30 strips of `review/v1_pack/strips/` plus 28 full-size frames in `review/v1_pack/extra/` (montages `m1.jpg` to `m9.jpg`, subtitle crop `sub.jpg`).

**Summary.** The story is told in the right order, the ending is there, and nothing is broken. Subtitles are verbatim-looking, well placed and readable in both languages (checked at full size, 42.8 s); the English matches the Arabic in every chunk. The first minute (flight, waiting, rumour balloon, refusal, parents' pressure) and the wedding two-shot with her tear (97.5-101 s) work well. The icon balloons in S8 (her portrait, his portrait, the X, the ring and the groom) are the clearest storytelling in the film.

The reason for REVISE is blocking in the second half. From 88.5 s to 146 s the three leads are repeatedly drawn behind or on top of other figures, so the film's main beats (the disguised husband among the guests, his answering verse, the recognition, the groom's flower) happen where the viewer cannot see them. These are staging fixes on an existing set and cast, not new drawing. No BLOCKER.

Do not change narration, subtitle text or subtitle timing for any note below.

## Notes (most important first)

### 1. 88.5-129.7 s (S9 to S12): the disguised poet is hidden behind a seated guest. MAJOR
**What is wrong.** After he covers his face and walks in (89.5), he stands at x about 1195 directly behind the drummer guest (x about 1150) and beside the singer. For 40 seconds only his wrapped head shows above the guest's keffiyeh (frames 92.0, 112.6, 123.0). The plan's acting for this stretch is "eyes, brow, head height and hands": the hands and body are never visible, so the checked hand at 112.54, the hand on the chest, the chest filling at 119.06 and the step forward at 119.98 do not exist on screen. His verse ribbon (121.8) appears to come out of the guest's head. The singer's stare (91.28-92.30) is also unreadable: she keeps facing front with an open mouth.
**Why it matters.** This is the suspense act and the climax. The hero must be the clearest figure in the frame, and the audience must see that the answering verse is his.
**Fix.**
- Stand him in clear air in the gap between Umaymah's cushion (860) and the drummer guest (1150): x about 1010-1030 for S9 to S12, feet about 15-20 px lower than the seated guests' base and drawn after them, so his whole figure is visible from head to feet. Nothing may overlap his torso or hands. If the gap is too tight, move the two guests and the fire about 80 px to the right.
- Keep the S11 cloud centred over him (it already sits over about x 1020, so the irony composition holds).
- Singer's stare: on "دون" (91.28) stop her singing mouth, snap her `gaze` hard toward him and tip her head for 0.6 s while his pupils freeze (`noBlink`); on "يعرفه" (92.30) shrug (both shoulders up, `sq` 0.96) and resume clapping. Add a small "?" over her for those 0.6 s if the turn still does not read.
- 112.54 ("زوجها"): hand rises toward her and stops, fully visible. 119.06 ("لم"): `anticipate` chest fill. 119.98 ("المتلمس"): one visible step toward her (about 40 px, foot planted with `walk`).
- 121.80/122.36 ("يصمت" / "فأجابها"): the blue ribbon must start at his covered mouth, with his head lifted and one hand open toward her.

### 2. 137.0-146.3 s (S14): the groom's flower and the handover are unreadable; the trio is one clump. MAJOR
**What is wrong.** Groom, Umaymah and the poet stand within about 150 world px (frames 137.9, 139.6, 142.2, 144.7). Umaymah is sandwiched and half hidden. The "hand goes into the sash ... and comes out with only a flower" surprise is a 16 px prop on a green robe in a wide shot: it cannot be seen. At 145.3-146.2 the groom walks *behind* Umaymah to reach the poet (only his turban shows behind her head at 145.7 and 146.2), so he seems to vanish, and the flower simply appears on the poet's chest. The groom's yellow ribbon rises from behind his turban and does not travel between the couple.
**Why it matters.** This is the second suspense beat and the moral of the story (generosity). It is the one moment the plan built a setup for (flower at 45.14 and 101.72), and the payoff is lost.
**Fix.**
- Spread the three: groom at about x 690-710, Umaymah at about 880, poet at about 1020, with visible gaps. At 137.08 the poet takes his half step in front of her without covering her face.
- 137.08-138.4: cut or fast push to a close shot of the groom alone (zoom about 1.9, his head and sash in frame). Hand goes into the sash on "حدث" tail / "لم" (137.08-137.60), face unreadable (`lid` 0.3, brows level). On "يغضب" (137.80) the hand comes out holding the flower, drawn at least 30-34 px with a short stem, and the rueful smile arrives 4 frames later. Hold to "الرجل" (138.98), then pull back to the spread three-shot for the open palms on "يخاصمهما" (139.54).
- 141.44 ("أنشد"): the yellow ribbon starts at his mouth and bows as an arc from him over to the pair (land its end between the two), not straight up.
- 144.44 ("سيتنحى"): one clear step back (left) and the open arm toward the couple.
- 145.30 ("ثم"): the groom steps in *front* of the carpet line (drawn after Umaymah) or the poet steps to him: the two men's hands meet in clear air to the left of Umaymah, flower visible between them for at least 0.5 s, the poet's fingers close on it. Then the exit left on "ترك" (146.28) as now. The wave at 147.86-148.2 already reads well: keep it.

### 3. 47.0-54.9 s (S5): Umaymah's nod of consent is hidden behind her parents. MAJOR

### 4. 129.7-137.0 s (S13): the recognition and the unveiling have no clean two-shot. MAJOR
**What is wrong.** At 131.5-132.7 the poet pulls the cloth down while the drummer guest's head and arm overlap his chest (frame 131.8), and Umaymah plays to the camera: arms wide, big smile, facing front, standing next to the groom. **Why it matters.** This is the emotional peak of the film ("عرفت أميمة صوت زوجها"). The viewer should get one clear picture of the two of them.
**Fix.**

### 5. 88.5-131.5 s: the face cover reads as a white medical mask or bandage. MAJOR (look)
**What is wrong.** `faceCover` is a flat white band across the lower face with small side loops (frames 92.0, 112.6, 123.0). On a white head cloth it makes a mummy-like white ball; at phone size the eyes are hard to find.
**Why it matters.** The disguise is on screen for 43 seconds, and a modern mask shape breaks the period. The eyes are the only acting channel he has.
**Fix (story-level pose, before it goes to the Library).** Draw the cover as the keffiyeh's own tail pulled diagonally across nose and mouth: a slanted top edge from one cheek up to the opposite temple, two or three fold lines, a loose end hanging to the shoulder, tinted a dusty tan (travel dust) or with the keffiyeh's dot shading so it separates from the white head cloth. Cap the height so the full eyes and brows stay clear (plan warning: about 0.7). Enlarge his pupils slightly or give him a thicker brow line while covered.

## Reusable techniques worth adding to the Library
- **Icon balloon strip** (S8): portraits, an X and a ring appearing word by word inside one speech balloon. Clear, language-free and reusable for any "they told him that..." line. Promote `iconBalloon` + `portraitMedallion` after approval.
- **Verse ribbon** as visible speech that travels between two people, with a prop (the heart) appearing on its end.
- **Rule to add to the guide (from notes 1 to 4):** "A lead who stands among a seated crowd is drawn after the crowd and in a clear gap; nothing overlaps a lead's torso or hands during a beat that belongs to them. A character never crosses behind another lead to hand something over: the hands meet in clear air."
- **Rule for small props that carry a payoff:** give the prop its own close shot at the payoff (zoom about 1.9) and draw it at 30 px or more.
