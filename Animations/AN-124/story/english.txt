The poet al-Mutalammis fled from King al-Numan ibn al-Mundhir and was away from his family for a long time. His wife Umaymah waited anxiously, with no news of him to reassure her, until word spread among the people that he had died and would not return.

Umaymah loved him deeply and refused to remarry. But her family said she must begin a new life, and that waiting was useless after all those years without news of him.

Many men asked for her hand, and her family kept pressing her until she finally agreed, though uneasy about her decision. They set a night for the wedding, and people gathered in the camp, and the sound of singing and drums came from the tents.

That very night al-Mutalammis returned from his journey and saw the decorations and heard the celebration. He asked some boys the reason for the celebration, and they told him that Umaymah, wife of al-Mutalammis, whom they thought dead, was marrying another man.

The poet was shocked and hurt, then slipped in among the guests without anyone recognizing him. He saw Umaymah near the new groom, sad despite the celebration. When the groom came near her, she sighed and began repeating a line of verse.

In her verse Umaymah wondered where her absent husband had gone, and in what far land he lived, if he was still alive. Al-Mutalammis could not stay silent, and answered her with a verse of his own saying that he was near and had not forgotten his love for her.

Umaymah recognized her husband’s voice, and the new groom understood what had really happened. The man did not grow angry or quarrel with them, but recited words showing he would step aside, then left the place to them and went away, never to return.

Al-Mutalammis returned to Umaymah after an absence people had thought was the end of his life. The couple stayed together in peace and happiness once the misunderstanding was cleared, and the wedding night that had seemed sad became a day of their return to each other.
