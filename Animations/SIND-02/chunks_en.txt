After a long stay in Baghdad, Sinbad returned to sea. | One fine day, he went ashore with the traders on a wooded island. | He rested under a tree and fell asleep.
When he awoke, nobody was there: the ship had sailed without noticing his absence. | Searching for a way to survive, he saw an enormous white dome in the silent island’s center.
Sinbad approached the dome and realized it was the egg of a giant bird called the roc. | Soon the sky darkened. The bird landed by its nest, | its wings like clouds.
Sinbad thought quickly and tied his turban to one of the bird’s legs without hurting it. | When the roc flew, | it carried him over the mountains and landed in a distant valley.
Sinbad untied the cloth and looked around. | Diamonds glittered on the ground, but high cliffs surrounded the valley, with huge snakes among the rocks. | He collected a few stones and searched for a safe way out.
Suddenly a large piece of meat fell from above. | Sinbad noticed diamonds sticking to it, | then saw an eagle carrying another piece to its nest near the mountaintop.
Sinbad hurried to tie himself to a piece of meat above the shining stones. | A strong eagle carried it upward. When it placed the meat near its nest, | Sinbad untied himself and moved away quietly.
Traders waiting for the eagles saw him and marveled at his escape. | He gave them some diamonds, | and they helped him board a ship to Baghdad with a wondrous tale.
