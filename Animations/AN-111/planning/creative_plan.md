# AN-111 The Devout Israelite: creative plan

Film length 177.87 s of narration + 3.0 s THE END hold (ends 180.87). 28 scenes, 160 actions. All times are seconds on the final MP3 and come from `planning/inputs/timeline.md`. The machine-readable storyboard is `creative_plan.json`; this file explains the direction behind it.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A poor spinner gives his whole day's earnings to a friend in need, trades his last two worthless things for a rotten fish, finds a pearl inside it, and when he is rich gives half away again at his own door, to a stranger who turns out to be a messenger sent to test him.

**Spine (beats, cue word times).**
- Setup: the family lives day to day from spinning cotton (5.28); the routine ends every day at zero coins (15.86).
- Inciting: a friend in desperate need (28.56). Turn: he gives every coin (32.86). Cost: home with no food, no cotton (39.50).
- Rising: only a cracked platter and an old jar remain (54.54); nobody wants them (63.46).
- Comic relief and turn: the swollen, stinking fish nobody wants (71.18); the swap of two unwanted things (77.72).
- Surprise: the pearl (94.34), never drilled (100.90); the honest expert (113.78); seventy thousand dirhams (124.28).
- Second test: the poor man at the door (136.54); he divides the fortune and offers half (145.66).
- Surprise and reveal: the money is returned (158.20); a messenger sent to test him (162.14); he has passed (166.24).
- Moral picture: rich now, still giving (175.18).

**Conflict.** Not man against man: generosity against need. Each gift costs the family something we have been shown (the bread, the cotton, the child's empty tray), so the second gift at the door has weight.

**Emotional arc.** Warm routine, a pang (the friend), quiet worry (the bare room), comedy (the fish), wonder (the pearl), dizzy luck (70,000), then the real climax, which is small and quiet: a man on one knee drawing a line in the dust between his sacks. The reveal is light and stillness, not spectacle.

**The structural idea: one gesture, three times.** The hero gives with the same pose (his hand to the other's open palm, his eyes on the person's face and never on the money) at 32.86 poor, 146.52 rich, and 175.18 in the last scene. The poor man at the door stands exactly where the friend stood in the market. The audience should feel "he is doing it again" before the narrator says so.

**Ending.** After the last word he leads the friend from the market through his own door; a small heart, THE END, the door closes on a lit window.

**Moral.** True generosity does not depend on how much you have.

## 3. Character acting

- **Hero.** Open face, kind brow (about -0.2). His tell is where he looks: at people, not at things. When he gives, his eyes stay on the other person's face. Anticipation before every lift and gift (10 px draw-back); a slow single blink marks each decision (31.08, 143.12). Shame-free: at 44.64 he is sheepish (hand to the back of the neck, gaze away and back), not guilty. At 150.06 there must be no glance back at the remaining half: "without hesitation" is acted, not labelled.
- **Wife.** The film's conscience check. Three key moments: open palms and worried brow (41.80), then her hand on his arm (49.20); the held stare at the pearl (95.06); the hand to her mouth and then the small nod (151.84-154.48). Her nod is what lets the audience accept the second gift.
- **Child.** Barometer of hunger and joy: the empty tray (43.34), the upturned basket with one wisp (52.98), the nose pinch (86.22), the stomach rub on "hunger" (153.32), the date and the laugh (171.16). Always reacts 4-6 frames after the adults (`stagger`).
- **Friend.** Heavy walk (`walk` amp 0.6, `tip` 0.08), `wet` eyes, `tremble` 1 px; after the gift he walks out taller. Returns unchanged in S28.
- **Fish seller.** Comedy by mask: a salesman smile held too wide while his eyes water and one hand pinches his nose (72.02); collapses onto his elbows when the last customer flees (74.48); a deep free breath when the fish is gone (83.22).
- **Jewel expert.** Bored half lids (`lid` 0.5, chin on hand) to bolt upright (109.12, `sq` 1.06 then `settleTo`). His honesty beat (114.82): he pushes his own coins forward, compares, shakes his head and pulls them back.
- **Rich merchant.** Quick and decisive: one look, one nod, one clap.
- **Poor man / messenger.** Begins as an echo of the friend. The first sign is posture: the stoop leaves him at 155.64, before any light. Serene from then on; no gloating, no theatrics.
- **Everyone:** `LIFE.t = t` for blinks, gaze targets via `lookAt`/`look`, `breathe` on holds, `noBlink` during the two held shocks (95.0-95.9, 162.8-163.6).

## 4. Camera and composition

- World 1000 x 1230 per set (market 1600 strip), ground or floor y 900; faces and key props kept between y 520 and y 880 so nothing important nears the caption panel.
- **Rhyming frames.** S27 repeats the S1 hut framing exactly so the change of fortune is read by comparison. S20 repeats the S6 two-shot geometry (hero left at x 430, the needy man right at x 640).
- **Two-shots** (`twoShot`) for every exchange: the gift (S6), the swap (S12), the expert (S17), the door (S20, S22, S24).
- **Push-ins on decisions and discoveries only:** the giving hands 32.0, the fish hollow 92.6, the offer 147.6.
- **One insert:** the pearl and the eye (S15), the only extreme close-up, reserved for "never drilled".
- **One pull-out with meaning:** S23 widens from the offer to reveal the family watching.
- **Tilt up** with the rising light at 165.2; final slow pull-out for THE END.
- Lettering and labels go on walls and sky above heads or on the ground strip below y 950; the coin counter lives in the top left of the panel and only in S3, S6 and S18.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour.** The routine counter that always ends at a cheerful 0; the fish that inflates on the word "swollen"; the seller's cracked smile; the mirrored head tilts of two men each judging the other's rubbish; the jar reused as a vase for the palm fan; the child's skid at the smell.
- **Suspense.** S20-S22: we know what he did last time and what it cost. Hold the cupped hand in frame while he remembers.
- **Surprise.** The glow comes before the pearl is seen (93.0). The returned sacks (158.20) land on a confused face, not on a knowing one.
- **Emotion.** The child's empty tray is shown twice: for real (43.34) and inside the memory cloud (143.12). The wife's nod. The final repeat of the giving gesture with the same friend.
- **Irony made visible.** The thought cloud at 130.64: the worthless fish turning into coins.
- **Restraint.** Eleven lettered words in the whole film; no sound on the pearl, the swap or the count, because the narration leaves no pause there.
