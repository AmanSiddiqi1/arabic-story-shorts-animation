A devout Israelite supported his family by selling the cotton thread they spun. He used the proceeds to buy more cotton and enough food for the day.

One morning a friend complained of desperate need. The man gave him all the money from that day’s thread and returned home without food or fresh cotton.

His family wondered how they would eat. Only a cracked wooden platter and an old jar remained to sell, but nobody in the market would buy them.

A fish seller had a swollen, foul-smelling fish that no one wanted. They exchanged their unwanted goods, and the man carried the fish home.

When the family opened the fish, they discovered a large pearl. It had never been drilled. The next day the man took it to a jewel expert.

The expert recommended a wealthier buyer. That merchant paid seventy thousand dirhams for the pearl, giving the family a fortune they had never imagined.

At his door a beggar asked for help. Remembering his own poverty, the man immediately divided the money in half and offered the beggar one share.

The beggar returned the money and revealed he was a messenger sent to test the man’s generosity. The family lived comfortably, and the man’s willingness to share had endured.
