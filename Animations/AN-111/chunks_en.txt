A devout Israelite|lived with his family by spinning cotton.|Every day his family spun thread, and he sold it,|then bought new cotton and food with the money left, enough for that day.
One morning the man went out to sell the thread as usual.|He met a friend who complained of poverty and need,|Moved by his plight, he gave him all he had been paid for the thread,|and returned home without food or fresh cotton.
His family asked him what had happened,|and he told them he had helped a man in need.|Nothing was left in the house to sell but a cracked wooden platter and an old jar,|so he took them to the market, but no one wanted them.
He saw a fish seller offering a swollen, foul-smelling fish|that he could not sell to anyone.|The two men agreed to swap their goods,|so the man took the fish, left the platter and jar with the seller, and went back to his family.
The family cut open the fish to prepare it for a meal|and found a large pearl inside it.|The man examined it and saw that it had never been drilled.|The next day he took it to a gem expert to ask its value.
The expert said the pearl was valuable,|but advised him to visit a merchant with more money.|The man went to him, and the merchant paid seventy thousand dirhams for the pearl,|and he returned home carrying a fortune he had never expected.
At the door of his house a poor man asked him for charity.|The man remembered how needy he had been the day before,|so he split the money in two and gave half to the poor man without hesitation, though his family had only just gone hungry.
But the poor man gave the money back|and said he was a messenger sent to test his generosity, and that he had passed the test.|The man thanked God and lived in comfort with his family,|having stayed ready to give even after becoming rich.
