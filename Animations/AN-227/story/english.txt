Harun al-Rashid walked with Jaafar and approached a group of young women drawing water. One recited eloquent verses about longing.

Harun asked whether she had composed the verses. She said yes, so he challenged her to keep their meaning while changing the rhyme.

She immediately produced a new rhyme. Harun again questioned her originality and demanded further versions, which she supplied without changing the theme.

Impressed, he asked about her place in the tribe. Her figurative answer revealed that she was the chief’s daughter, and his reply hinted at his rank.

Recognizing him as the caliph, she saluted him and left. Harun sent Jaafar to her father with a marriage proposal, which the father accepted.

She became dear to Harun, who generously supported her father. She also learned to read her husband’s feelings in his expression.

When her father died, Harun entered looking unusually sorrowful. Before he spoke she understood what had happened and put on mourning clothes.

Harun asked how she knew. She said she had never seen him so distressed and had feared most for her elderly father. The tale says she later died in grief.
