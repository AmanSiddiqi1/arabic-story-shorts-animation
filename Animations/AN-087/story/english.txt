Ibrahim bin al-Mahdi opposed his nephew, Caliph al-Ma’mun, in a struggle for power. After losing, he hid in Baghdad, fearing the large reward offered for his capture.

Ibrahim took refuge with a barber-surgeon. The man welcomed him and fed him, but Ibrahim feared that he might hand him over for the reward.

The barber produced a lute, revealed that he knew Ibrahim’s identity, and promised safety. They sang together, and the host refused any payment for his help.

Ibrahim went out disguised in women's clothing. A soldier recognized him and tried to arrest him. Ibrahim pushed him near a bridge and fled.

A woman hid Ibrahim, even though the injured pursuer was her husband. After three days he moved to a former freedwoman’s house, but she betrayed him for the reward.

Ibrahim was brought before al-Ma'mun while still disguised. He admitted his rebellion and asked for pardon. Those present were divided between those who called for his death and those who urged mercy.

The caliph pardoned his uncle and restored his property. Hearing about the escape, he learned who had sheltered Ibrahim and who had betrayed him.

Al-Ma’mun punished the informer, assigned the trooper to learn barbering, and honored the trooper’s wife. He rewarded the loyal barber with the trooper’s house, a robe, and an annual allowance.
