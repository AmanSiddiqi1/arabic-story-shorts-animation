Caliph Harun ordered his guard Salih|to collect a million dirhams from Mansur before sunset,|threatening severe punishment if the money did not arrive.
Mansur possessed only a small part of the sum.|Seeing his distress,|Salih took him to Yahya ibn Khalid al-Barmaki,|a man renowned for generosity.
Yahya heard the story|and checked his treasury,|finding only five thousand dirhams.|He immediately asked his sons al-Fadl and Jaafar for help.
Each son sent a large sum,|enough to cover the debt and more.|Yahya added a valuable jewel familiar at the caliph’s court|and handed everything to Mansur.
Mansur thanked Yahya|and departed with Salih.|Yahya hoped the caliph would recognize the jewel,|understand his generosity, and show mercy to the debtor.
Yet on the way|Mansur recited verses suggesting he had sought help only from fear.|Salih rebuked him|for such ingratitude toward the man who had saved him.
Salih presented the money and jewel and reported Mansur’s words.|Harun recognized the jewel,|was moved by Yahya’s kindness,|and returned it to him.
Yahya did not resent Mansur.|He said fear and sorrow can make a person speak badly.|The crisis ended,|and his generosity stood without any demand for gratitude.
