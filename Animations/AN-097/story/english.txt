Caliph Harun al-Rashid ordered one of his guards, named Salih, to collect a million dirhams from Mansur before sunset, saying that if he did not pay on time, he would be severely punished.

Mansur possessed only a small part of the sum. His fear grew and he could not think. Salih pitied him and took him to the house of Yahya ibn Khalid al-Barmaki, who was famous for his generosity and for helping the needy.

Yahya heard the story and asked his treasurer to bring whatever money he had. The treasury held only five thousand dirhams, so he sent at once to his sons al-Fadl and Jaafar for help.

Each son sent a large sum, enough to cover the debt and more. He added to the money a precious jewel with a well-known history at the caliph’s court and handed everything to Mansur.

Mansur thanked Yahya then left with Salih, carrying the money and the jewel. Yahya hoped the caliph would recognize the jewel, and so realize how much help he had given, and soften his heart toward the debtor.

But Mansur, overcome by fear, recited verses on the way suggesting he had turned to his benefactor only out of need. Salih rebuked him and said it was a poor reward for the man who had saved him from ruin.

Salih brought the money and the jewel to the caliph, and also told him what Mansur had said. Harun recognized the jewel, was moved by Yahya’s kindness, and returned it to him. Yahya also heard what the man had said after he helped him.

Yahya did not resent Mansur. Instead he said fear and grief can make a person say what he does not mean. The crisis ended, and Yahya’s stand remained a testament to generosity that expects no thanks.
