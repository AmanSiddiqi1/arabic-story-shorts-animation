Abu al-Hasan knew pilgrimage routes well and usually guided others. One year he decided to travel to Mecca alone and reached a mosque in al-Qadisiyyah.

In the mosque he met a man with leprosy who asked to travel together. Abu al-Hasan refused, partly because of his prejudice.

After a long journey he reached al-Aqabah and found the same man already there. The traveler smiled and spoke of help granted to the weak.

Abu al-Hasan traveled on to Arafat and again found the man ahead of him. His rejection turned into admiration, and he asked for companionship.

The man declined, reminding him of his earlier rejection. Abu al-Hasan continued and repeatedly found him ahead, until he lost sight of him near Medina.

In Medina he asked respected teachers about the stranger and learned he was Abu Ja’far, reputed in the tale for piety and prayers for rain.

According to the tale, Abu Ja’far appeared again and eventually invited a request. Abu al-Hasan asked for prayers for contentment, freedom from dependence on stored provisions, and closeness to God.

Abu Ja’far prayed and departed. Abu al-Hasan said the first two wishes were fulfilled and still hoped for the third. The lesson concerns prejudice and spiritual worth.
