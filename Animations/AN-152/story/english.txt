A king went out in disguise and asked for water at a house. A married woman served him kindly while her husband was away.

The king suggested a romantic encounter. Recognizing him, the woman calmly brought out a book warning against adultery and injustice.

The king read, felt ashamed, and left without touching her. The woman believed the matter was over and resumed her ordinary life.

When her husband learned of the royal visit, he grew fearful despite having no proof of wrongdoing. He withdrew from his wife, leaving her distressed.

Her relatives appealed to the king through a metaphor: a man had rented a fine field but left it uncultivated. Was such neglect fair?

Understanding that the field meant the marriage, the king questioned the husband. The man replied that a lion had entered his field, and he dared not return.

The king replied that the lion had entered but damaged nothing and taken nothing. The husband understood this as confirmation that his wife had done no wrong.

The couple reconciled, and the tale says the king rewarded them. The woman’s courage had checked a powerful man, and trust returned to her marriage.
