A little sparrow flew over a sheepfold and sometimes watched the sheep from its fence. One morning it saw a great eagle swoop toward a young lamb.

The eagle seized the lamb in powerful talons and carried it high away. The admiring sparrow thought, “Why not do the same? I too have wings.”

The sparrow sought a large animal to carry. It saw a fat ram with thick, tangled wool and did not consider the difference between its own strength and the eagle’s.

It rose and swooped onto the ram’s back like the eagle, digging its feet into the fleece and beating its wings. The ram did not lift an inch.

The sparrow tried to fly away alone, but its feet were caught in the filthy matted wool. It pulled and beat its wings, growing more frightened and exhausted.

The shepherd had seen both the eagle’s success and the sparrow’s imitation. He approached the ram and easily seized the trapped little bird.

Angry, the shepherd pulled out the sparrow’s wing feathers, tied its feet with string, and took it home. His children asked about the strange bird that could not fly.

The shepherd told them it had tried to do what a far stronger eagle had done. The sparrow remained before the children, unable to fly, after its painful attempt.
