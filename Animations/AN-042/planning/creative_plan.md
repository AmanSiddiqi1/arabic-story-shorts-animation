# AN-042 The Sparrow and the Eagle: creative plan

Film length 163.50 s of narration + 3.0 s THE END hold (ends 166.5). All times are seconds on the final MP3. The machine-readable storyboard (11 scenes, 162 actions, subtitle chunks, asset specs) is `creative_plan.json`; this file explains the direction behind it.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A little sparrow watches an eagle carry off a lamb, tries the same trick on a fat ram, gets his feet tangled in the wool and ends up wingless in the shepherd's hut.

**Moral.** Know your own strength: copying someone far stronger ends badly.

**Conflict.** The sparrow's picture of himself against his real size. Nobody opposes him: the ram does not even notice. That indifference is the engine of the comedy.

**Spine (cue word, time).**
1. Setup: the sparrow is a regular on the fold's fence (السور 8.20).
2. Inciting: the eagle dives on a lamb (ينقض 15.96) and lifts it away (وارتفع 25.30).
3. Turn: "Why not do the same? I too have wings" («لماذا 35.18).
4. Rising: he picks the biggest animal, the fat ram with tangled wool (كبشًا 48.08, متشابك 54.02), and never compares strengths (الفرق 58.08).
5. Comic peak: a perfect eagle dive at quarter size (اندفع 66.40); the ram does not move (لم 80.38).
6. Surprise: his feet are caught (علقتا 90.70); fear and tiredness (وتعبه 101.96).
7. Climax: the shepherd picks him off with one hand (أمسكه 118.32).
8. Resolution: feathers plucked, feet tied (فنتف 126.62); LATER; the children's question (سألوه 137.96).
9. Moral: "a far stronger eagle" (أقوى 149.42); the sparrow stays, unable to fly (للطيران 156.46).

**Emotional arc.** Cheerful (0-10), awe (10-41), cocky ambition (41-66), comic deflation (66-82), rising fear (82-103), helplessness (103-132), quiet regret (132-end). The audience laughs with affection until 82 s and then feels for him; the film never mocks him at the end.

**Surprises.** The shadow before the eagle (11.2). The landing with no effect at all (67.92). The snap back when he tries to leave (85.4). The eagle silhouette in the window at the end (157.5).

## 3. Character acting

- **Sparrow (bird, kind lark, s 0.7).** Quick, busy, sincere. Head moves are 2-frame snaps (`gazePlan(t, plan, 0.07)`); he always flaps (flap 1.4-2.4) where the eagle glides. Feelings read through `crest` (up = alert or proud, flat = fear), `sq` (puffed 1.08 when proud, 0.85 when cowed), `sag` (wings hang when tired), `lid` and `wet`. One look to camera only, at 81.58. Tiredness in S7 is a three-step decay: each pull has less flap, lower rise, slower recovery. After 128.66 use `clipped: 1`. Ending: the Library shame pattern (hold, eyes break away, head down), then `wet` and a long blink; no smile.
- **Eagle (bird, kind hawk, eagle palette, s 2.6).** Economy is his character: locked wings, level head, half lid (0.3) until the lock-on at 15.18 when the lid opens and the brow drops. Anticipation 0.7 s before the dive, stretch 1.15 in it, overshoot on the flare, three heavy lift strokes with the lamb swinging late (`swing`).
- **Ram (sheep, ram, fat 1).** Never changes expression: lid 0.45, sideways chew at a constant rhythm through the whole of S6 and S7. His only actions are an ear flick (68.6), a slow blink (74.56), eating again (80.38) and one look back (97.48). Do not let him react to the sparrow's weight.
- **Ewes and lamb.** A chorus that reacts in stagger (`stagger(i)`, 0.1-0.2 s): heads up at the shadow, scatter, heads following the lamb, two ewes judging the performance at 78.44. The lamb: spring hops (`hop`, `landSquash`), frozen wide eyes, one bleat.
- **Shepherd (person, villager, age 0.45, staff).** Stern, tired, not cruel. Eye line always motivated: sky (the lost lamb), then the bird. Anger is brow 1 and a set mouth for 6 s, easing to 0.6 once the string is tied; with his children his face softens (brow 0.2) and his hands do the explaining (`armsOut`, then a pinch).
- **Children (person child, servant and maiden).** Boy leads reactions, girl follows 5 frames later and is the gentler one: she kneels to the bird's level (154.08) and offers the grain (161.04). Listening poses: chin on hand, sitting back on heels; eyes go cloud, father, bird.

## 4. Camera and composition

- The panel shows the top two-thirds only; keep faces above panel y 1250 and lettering in the sky, never over a face.
- **Repeat the framing to sell the copy:** the sparrow's dive in S6 uses the same wide framing and diagonal as the eagle's in S2 (top right to the target), at a quarter of the size.
- Establishing crane down (S1), tilt up to the sky and a fast tilt down with the dive (S2), close grab then crane out as the fold shrinks (S3), close-up with the thought cloud at upper right (S4), pan reveal of the ram and push-in to the fleece (S5), inserts on feet (69.94, 89.5) at zoom 3.2, `twoShot` for shepherd and bird (S8-S9), slow 8 s push-in to end (S11).
- The eye line sparrow-to-eagle (lower left to upper right) is the film's main diagonal; the ending reverses it (sparrow lower right of the window, looking up-left at a far silhouette).
- One LATER... card at 132.0 hides the set change; no other transitions than cuts.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour:** scale. Eagle landing = flash, dust ring, panel shake; sparrow landing = "pff" and three specks of lint. The HEIGHT 0 counter that flickers and stays at zero. The ewe he measured and rejected. The ram's single ear flick.
- **Suspense:** the shadow before the eagle; the TANGLED WOOL label planted at 54.02, 36 s before it pays off; the hand hovering over him for 0.6 s at 116.92.
- **Dramatic irony:** the size chart at 58.08 is for the audience; the sparrow never turns his head to it.
- **Surprise:** BOING! at 85.4 after we have relaxed into the joke.
- **Emotion:** the ending holds on small actions (two short wings lifted and dropped, the window, the grains) and lets the narrator carry the moral.
