Wardan was a butcher in Cairo. Every morning a mysterious woman paid a valuable dinar for a whole lamb. After many such visits, he grew curious.

He questioned the porter carrying her purchases. The man said she bought food, fruit, candles, and wine, then led him into gardens and blindfolded him before their destination.

Next morning Wardan left his shop to an assistant and followed her secretly. He watched her blindfold the porter and lead him toward a large stone near a mountain.

Through an opening he saw an underground room. The woman cooked for a huge bear, ate and drank with it, and engaged in sexual intercourse with it. Wardan was stunned.

When both lay exhausted, Wardan entered and killed the bear. The woman woke grieving and demanded her own death. He proposed repentance and marriage; she refused and threatened him.

Wardan killed the woman and found gold, gems, and pearls. He filled the porter’s crate and headed back to Cairo, believing the adventure was over.

At Cairo’s gate Caliph al-Hakim asked him about the woman and bear. The ruler said an enchanted hoard awaited someone exactly like Wardan, the only person able to open it.

They returned, and Wardan opened the door and brought up the rest. The caliph took the larger treasure but let him keep his crate. Wardan opened a market bearing his name.
