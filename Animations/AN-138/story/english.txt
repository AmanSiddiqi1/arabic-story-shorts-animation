One day Yahya ibn Khalid al-Barmaki returned from the caliph’s palace to his house in Baghdad. At the door of his house he saw a poor man waiting for him, The man stood out of respect, greeted him, then said he had come to ask for his help.

The man told Yahya that he badly needed money, and asked him to be generous with him. Yahya asked neither about his family nor his occupation, but ordered a comfortable room prepared for him inside.

Then Yahya summoned his treasurer and ordered him to give the guest a thousand dirhams every day, and serve him the same fine food enjoyed by the household, without setting a deadline.

The man stayed for days and then weeks, eating, resting and receiving the money regularly. Every morning a new thousand dirhams reached him, until he had gathered a sum he had never dreamed of holding.

After thirty days he held thirty thousand dirhams. He gazed at the big bag, and feared Yahya would check his account, see the sum had gone beyond what was proper, and demand some back.

The man did not want to wait until the matter became clear, so he quietly gathered his belongings and carried off all the money, and left the house secretly without saying goodbye. He thought that this way he would keep the gifts he had received.

When the servants learned of his departure, they told Yahya he had slipped away after collecting thirty thousand dirhams. Yahya did not grow angry, nor did he send anyone to bring him back, but marveled that the man had thought generosity could suddenly end.

Yahya said that if the guest had stayed in his house to the end of his life, he would never have stopped the gifts or meals. So the story ended with the man leaving with what he had gathered, while the door of hospitality stayed open in its owner’s heart.
