# AN-138 Yahya bin Khalid and the Poor Man: creative plan

Film length 172.27 s of narration + 3.5 s THE END = 175.77 s. All times are seconds on the final MP3. The machine-readable storyboard (17 scenes, 169 timed actions, every cue word) is `creative_plan.json`; this file explains the direction.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A poor man asks the vizier Yahya for help and is given a room, the family's own food and a thousand dirhams every day, with no end date. After thirty days he panics that it is too much, and sneaks away with his sack from a door that was never going to close.

**Conflict.** There is no villain. The conflict is inside the guest: he cannot believe in a kindness that keeps no account, so he invents an accountant.

**Emotional arc.** Need and shame (P1-P2), disbelief and delight (P2-P4), pride (P5 start), fear (P5), stealth and false relief (P6), then the film leaves him and ends on Yahya's calm wonder (P7-P8). The audience should laugh at the escape and then feel the loss when the open door is shown.

**Surprises.** (1) Yahya asks nothing. (2) No end date. (3) Yahya is not angry. Each is staged as an expectation that is visibly set up and then not met: question tags that are struck out, an empty box that stays empty, servants braced for a shout that never comes.

**Moral.** True generosity keeps no account; fear of losing a gift can cost more than the gift.

## 3. Character acting

- **Yahya** (person, costume vizier, age 0.55, s 1.3). Stillness is his character: slow blinks, level brow, small hand gestures (`PERSON_HANDS.chest`, `.chin`, `.palmsUp`, one `.pointFwd` for the order). His gaze is always on whoever speaks (`look()`), and he lifts the kneeling man by the elbow (`handRW`). He takes exactly one commanding pose (36.94) and one stopping palm (142.34). At 139.64 the acting is the absence of a reaction: hold the brow at 0 while everything around him expects anger.
- **The poor man** (person, costume villager, faded palette, bare feet, patches, s 1.2). Arc in posture: stooped and low eyed, `wet` eyes and `clasp` / `reachBoth` hands in P1-P2; saccades of disbelief in the room (`gazePlan(t, plan, 0.07)`); loose, lazy `lie` and one-eyed `wink` in P4; proud hands on hips at 87.16; then the fear set: smile drops through `o` to frown, `brow -0.8`, `tremble`, a sweat drop, arms round the sack. In P6 he is a hump on legs: `tip 0.3`, bent knees, the planned `sneak` gait, shoulders up, frozen for 4 frames when the servant snores. The half-raised hand at 119.58 that drops again is the "no farewell".
- **The treasurer** (person, costume merchant in green). The comic foil: precise entrance with swinging keys (`swing`), quill poised, eyes wide at 1000, a slow look to camera at EVERY DAY, a gulp, frantic writing. In S7 his raised brows and hovering quill ask the question the story never speaks. In the S9 repeats his bow shrinks and his smile grows.
- **Servants A and B** (person, costume servant; red and blue). Never in unison: B is two frames behind (`stagger`), bumps into A (`lurch`), and is the one who wants to chase. Their braced poses at 138.16 (`shield`, one eye shut) set up Yahya's calm.
- **Anticipation and follow-through.** Every rise, sit, heave and lunge has an `anticipate` dip and a `settleTo` overshoot; cushions and the sack squash (`sq`) when touched; keys, the bindle and the carried sack lag and swing; the horse snorts and swishes after the stop; coins bounce and one always rolls.
- **Listeners react.** In every two-shot the silent character has a planned reaction (head tilt, blink on a nod, eyes following the moth, eyes following the tags as they fly off).

## 4. Camera and composition

- World 1000 x 1230, floor y 900; faces stay between y 420 and y 720 world so nothing important nears the caption panel.
- Establishing wide with a pan (S1); two-shots built with `twoShot` for all dialogue (S2, S3, S6, S7); push-ins on the three decisions (Yahya's nod 31.5, the 1000 at 48.7, his non-anger 138.8); a close on the hands for "in his hand" (82.0).
- The guest room is always framed the same way wide (S5, S8, S10, S14, and inside the S16 vision) so that the empty couch at 127.24 and the aged guest at 155.72 read instantly.
- Direction of travel carries meaning: Yahya and the guest enter the house left to right; the guest flees right to left; the last pan goes left to right, back home to the open door.
- Thought clouds sit in the upper half; the thinker is low in frame with the tail to his head. Labels and tags live in sky or wall space, never over a face.
- The dismount is hidden by a close shot (11.2-13.0); the change to night and sack is hidden under the LATER card (camera keys 0.01 s apart at its midpoint); S14 whips from room to hall at 131.4.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour.** The moth from the purse; the treasurer's look to camera; the purse metronome; catching the coin on his foot; the sack stuck in the door; the white-bearded servant still carrying the tray in the vision.
- **Suspense.** The night exit: lamp turned down, the coin that almost clinks, the snore, the freeze.
- **Surprise.** Struck-out question tags; the empty UNTIL box; braced servants and a smiling master.
- **Dramatic irony and emotion.** The door drifting open behind the fleeing man (126.5) and the same open door at the end with a fresh tray on the step. The plant (NO END DATE on the empty box, 59.3) pays off in the vision (155-160) and in STILL OPEN (169.3).
- **Colour of thought.** His fear cloud is blue (cold); Yahya's clouds are yellow (warm).

## 8. Warnings and open questions

- The narration is almost continuous: only three pauses of 0.6 s or more, all before 23 s. Coins, doors and purses must be silent picture events.
- The English translation is condensed, so some English chunks are short and two do not translate the Arabic spoken at that moment (see `owner_questions`, first entry).
- About 30 pieces of lettering are planned; most are labels on key beats, but if the film feels wordy cut first: HOME, CALIPH'S PALACE, GOOD FOOD, THE BIG SACK, CLINK!, POOF!.
- The front-facing person has no profile: bows, turns and backward looks are acted with `tip`, `gaze`, `dir` flips and hands.
- The guest room is built on the merchant variant of `palaceHall`; check on a still that it reads as a bedroom, not a shop.
- Eight choices for the owner are listed in `owner_questions` (horse arrival, invented labels, the imagined angry Yahya, the family diagram, the dozing servant and open door, the aged guest vision, the LATER card over speech, and the condensed English).
