A ferryman carrying people across the Nile once transported an elderly hermit. He kindly gave the quiet, poor man some food.

The hermit thanked him and said he would die around noon the following day. He asked the ferryman to wash, shroud, and bury him.

He also asked the ferryman to keep his patched robe, water gourd, and staff for whoever came to claim them. The ferryman promised to comply.

The ferryman returned and found the hermit dead beneath a tree. The tale says a new burial shroud lay nearby, and he buried the man.

He saved the belongings. Next morning a young man reputed to enjoy frivolous pursuits came asking for the patched robe, gourd, and staff.

Asked how he knew, the young man said a dream had told him of the hermit’s death and his own succession. The ferryman handed over the items, bewildered.

The ferryman could not understand how such a young man might succeed a hermit. That night he dreamed of a reminder that divine grace cannot be judged by appearances.

The ferryman awoke reassured. The story warns against deciding someone’s spiritual worth or ultimate destiny from reputation alone.
