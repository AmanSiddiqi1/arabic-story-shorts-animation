A pair of wood pigeons lived high in a date palm, eating its fruit daily. Below them a hedgehog and his wife wanted the dates but could not climb the tree.

The hedgehog dug a burrow by the trunk and pretended to pray and live simply. Asked about his life, he claimed thirty years of devotion and eating only fallen dates.

Impressed, the pigeon asked for guidance. The hedgehog advised the couple to shake down the dates, save enough for a year, move near the ground and devote themselves to worship.

The pigeons worked until all the fruit fell. The hedgehog secretly stored it in his burrow, hoping hunger would bring them near enough for him to catch and eat.

Descending, the pigeons found no dates. The hedgehog claimed the wind had carried them away and lectured them about patience and trust while pretending to care little for food.

After long persuasion, the pigeons trusted him and entered his burrow. The hedgehog rushed to block the exit and bared his teeth. They realized it was a trap.

The male pigeon protested: “Yesterday you advised us; today you wish to kill us! Do you not fear your deceit returning upon you?” He mentioned two cheats who planned to betray a merchant and each other.

The hedgehog asked for the story. The pigeon told how each cheat poisoned the other’s food, both died and the merchant survived. The hedgehog listened at the burrow’s entrance while the pigeons remained trapped inside.
