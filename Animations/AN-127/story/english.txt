In Medina, Musab bin al-Zubayr met a clever woman called Izzah. Considering marriage to Ayishah daughter of Talhah, he asked Izzah to visit and describe her.

Izzah returned with a detailed report praising Ayishah’s face, eyes, and figure, including unusually frank physical descriptions. Her assignment had been to assess beauty.

Izzah noted Ayishah’s large feet and joked that Musab might struggle to match her passion after marriage. He did not abandon his plan.

Musab married Ayishah. The tale says their married life began with strong mutual desire. Afterward Izzah invited the bride and women of Quraysh to a gathering.

At the gathering, with Musab nearby, Ayishah recited verses about love and kisses, suggesting her husband was on her mind even among the guests.

The narrative is frank about their intimacy, saying they spent their wedding night together repeatedly. The following day a woman praised Musab’s vigor.

On another occasion a woman overheard Ayishah during an intimate encounter. Surprised by such openness from a woman of rank, she questioned her afterward.

Ayishah answered unapologetically that a wife should show her husband affection and desire. She did so by day and night, she said. The tale ends there.
