One night musician Isaac of Mosul noticed a large basket hanging from an upper window. Curious and affected by wine, he climbed in; servants raised him, mistaking him for an expected visitor.

Servants led him into a splendid hall, where a young woman asked how he had arrived. Isaac explained the basket but concealed his profession, claiming to be a Baghdad merchant.

Isaac returned on two more nights, becoming so absorbed that he once left al-Ma’mun’s gathering without permission. The angry caliph demanded an explanation.

In private, Isaac described the remarkable hostess. The caliph wished to meet her, so they hid their identities and rose to the house in two baskets.

The hostess entertained them with verse and song, until al-Ma’mun accidentally addressed Isaac by name and title. Realizing the other visitor was the caliph, she withdrew.

Al-Ma’mun learned the house belonged to Hasan bin Sahl and the woman was his daughter Khadijah. He asked to marry her and promised a substantial marriage settlement.

The caliph told Isaac to keep the affair secret. Isaac did so until al-Ma’mun’s death, then recounted how a strange basket led to extraordinary meetings and a royal marriage.
