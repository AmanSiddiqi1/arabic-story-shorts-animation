One sleepless night Harun al-Rashid walked through his palace. He saw an enslaved handmaid he favored, affected by wine, and asked to spend the night with her.

She said she was not prepared and asked him to come the following night. The next day, when he sent for her, she answered, “Day erases the promise of night.”

The reply astonished and annoyed Harun. He called three poets and ordered each to compose verses around the line about a nighttime promise erased by day.

Al-Rakashi recited the grief of a sleepless lover betrayed at dawn. Abu Musab followed, describing another heart burning with longing and repeating her words.

Abu Nuwas instead described a night in the palace: a handmaid drinking wine, displaced clothing, and a promise withdrawn with sunrise. His verses closely matched the event.

Harun awarded the first two poets ten thousand dirhams each. Then, accusing Abu Nuwas of secretly watching the encounter, he ordered his execution.

Abu Nuwas protested that he had stayed home. The caliph had supplied the theme, he argued, and poets imagine things they never witness; he quoted scripture on poets’ inventions.

Harun accepted that a poem was no proof of spying, pardoned Abu Nuwas, and awarded him twenty thousand dirhams—twice either other poet’s prize.
