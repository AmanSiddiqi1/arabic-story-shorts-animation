# AN-126 Harun al-Rashid and the Three Poets: creative plan

Film length 159.83 s of narration + 3.5 s held ending = 163.3 s. 17 scenes, 133 actions. All times are seconds on the final MP3. The machine-readable storyboard is `creative_plan.json` (every action with its cue word, acting and Library call); this file explains the direction.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A handmaid's witty excuse, "day erases the promise of night", becomes the caliph's poetry contest. Abu Nuwas imagines the night so exactly that he is nearly beheaded as a spy, then talks his way to a double prize.

**Conflict.** Harun's wounded pride against a poet's imagination. The audience has seen the night (S1-S3), so when Abu Nuwas's poem replays it, they understand Harun's suspicion before he speaks.

**Emotional arc.** Restless night, flirtation, a sting to the pride, playful contest, growing unease, fear (10 s), relief, generosity, a last laugh.

**Surprises.** (1) The sun erasing the moon inside the balloon. (2) The cloud of the third poem turning into the hall we already saw. (3) The servant's empty hands. (4) The double purse.

**Ending and moral.** Imagination is not evidence; a fair ruler listens before he punishes. THE END at 160.2 with the moon-knot badge that, this once, is not erased.

## 3. Character acting

- **Harun (caliph preset, s 0.9).** The film is on his face. Night: tired lids (0.25), saccades, a sigh. With the handmaid: softened lids, hopeful raised brows (brow -0.3), chest-and-offer hands. During the poems he is a still listener (cheek on fist) so that the change in S9 reads: cheek lifts (87.0), brows rise (88.4), tip forward and grip (90.0), looks over both shoulders (91.6), brows swing to a V (94.1). Anger is a slow rise from the throne with anticipation, a whip turn with settle, one pointing arm. Calm is a keyed breath (sq 1.03 to 0.98), brows to level over 1.4 s, sitting back with landSquash, a rueful hand to the neck.
- **Handmaid (lady preset, red robe).** Tipsy is small: a 2.4 s sway (tip 0.04), lid 0.45, a hop on the hiccup, mouthCover giggle. She tugs the shawl back up when she sees him. Her refusal is charming, not cold: palm up, a yawn, pointUp, a wink at 24.9. She is asleep the moment he leaves.
- **Three poets, three temperaments** (never in unison, `stagger(i)`): Al-Raqashi old and formal (stoop, beard stroke, raised arm, scroll); Abu Musab theatrical (hands on heart, head back, deep bows, fans himself); Abu Nuwas quick and amused (saccade gaze, quill twirl at 54.4 paid off at 159.7, recites with eyes shut so he misses Harun's reaction, opens one eye in the silence at 100.9).
- **Abu Nuwas in danger.** Turban hop, shield palms, hands to his throat, kneel with impactSquash and tremble. Then the tremble stops on the temple tap (130.2): his confidence returns as he argues. Relief at 150.3: sq 0.94, lid shut, checks his neck.
- **Servant and guard.** The servant is worried on his return (brow -0.7, eyes shut while delivering the answer) and comically quick to retreat at 108.9. The guard is expressionless (lid 0.4); only his pupils move; he reads his master's face and lowers the sword himself at 147.7.
- **Eye lines.** Every listener has `lookAt` on the speaker or the cloud. The one look to camera is Abu Nuwas's wink in S17.

## 4. Camera and composition

- World 1000 x 1230, floor y 900. One room in two lightings. Throne and Harun always on the right; the door arch (entrances, the guard) on the left; the reciting spot at x 500.
- Establishing wide for S1, two-shots with `twoShot` for the couple (S2-S3) and for accusation (S12), push-ins on decisions (37.0 the answer, 96.5-100.2 the suspicion, 139.8-145.5 the change of heart), static wide for the money gag (S11) so the counters read.
- Clouds live in the upper left (centre about (380, 340)), above the waiting poets and clear of every head; Harun must remain in frame at the right edge during S7-S9.
- The NEXT DAY... card at 29.5 hides the change from night to day (camera key 0.01 s apart at the card midpoint).
- One panel-only shake, 0.3 s, at 115.7. THE END in the upper third.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **The motif.** The moon with a knot (a promise) and the sun that erases it turns an abstract line of poetry into a picture the viewer can follow without reading. It appears eight times and becomes the THEME badge parked top right during the contest.
- **Setup and payoff.** The hiccup and slipped shawl at 11.5-12.3 are planted so that the same two moves inside Abu Nuwas's cloud (88.4, 90.0) make the audience gasp before Harun does. Use one tableau function for both.
- **Suspense.** S10 is almost still: two identical clouds, an equals sign, Harun's eyes sliding down to the bowing poet, who opens one eye.
- **Humour.** Abu Musab's overacting; the rivals' sidestep at 101.4; the empty-handed servant; "PHEW!"; the rivals comparing one purse with two; the purses moved out of reach in the ending.
- **Emotion.** The fear at 119 is real (kneel, tremble, wet eyes) so the pardon has weight; Harun's embarrassment at 146.9 makes him likeable.
- **Discretion.** Nothing immodest is drawn: a cup, a shawl, a polite gesture, a guard who never raises his sword.
