An old tale speaks of Labtayt, a city in al-Andalus with a sealed tower. Each new king added a lock until its door held twenty-four.

No one knew what lay inside. Earlier rulers had kept it sealed, until a new king from another family took power.

The new king decided to open the tower. His advisers warned that previous kings must have had a reason to keep it shut, but he insisted.

His nobles even offered him wealth to change his mind. He refused, removed the locks, and found figures of Arab riders bearing swords and spears.

A written warning said opening the tower would bring an army like the riders shown and lead to the country’s fall. The witnesses were frightened, but the door was already open.

The legend says that in that same year an Arab army led by Tariq ibn Ziyad reached al-Andalus, defeated the king and discovered immense treasure.

Among the treasures were crowns, old books, marvelous instruments, a mirror said to show distant lands, and a precious table attributed to Solomon.

The treasures were sent, the tale says, to Caliph al-Walid. The tower, sealed for generations, became remembered for a warning the new king heeded too late.
