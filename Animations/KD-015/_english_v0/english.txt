An ascetic lived in a small house and received a little ghee and honey each day. He ate what he needed and put the rest in a clay jar. As the days passed, the jar became almost full.

One evening, the ascetic sat beneath the jar hanging above his head. He looked at it and said, “There is so much ghee and honey in there! If I sold them at the market, I would get plenty of money.” He began calculating imaginary profits.

He thought, “With that money I will buy some goats. After a few months, they will have young, and the herd will grow. Then I will sell some and buy cows and a wide field.” He smiled as he imagined it.

Then he said, “I will plant the field and gather the harvest. I will have food and money. I will build a big house, buy beautiful furniture, and everyone will know I am a rich man.” His dream grew with every moment.

His imagination did not stop there. He pictured himself married, with a clever son. “I will teach my son reading and arithmetic,” he said. “When he grows up, he will work in the king’s palace, and people will be proud of him.”

Then he imagined that one day his son disobeyed him. The ascetic became angry at something that had never even happened, and grabbed a stick beside him. “And if he disobeys me,” he shouted, “I will punish him with this stick!”

He raised the stick and swung it as though striking his imaginary son. But the stick hit the real jar above his head! The jar broke, and the ghee and honey spilled onto his face and clothes.

The ascetic sat stunned as ghee and honey dripped from his hair. He looked at the broken pieces on the ground and then at the stick in his hand. There was no money, no goats, no field; even the honey he had saved was gone.
