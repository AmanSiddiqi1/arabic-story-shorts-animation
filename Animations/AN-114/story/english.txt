A wealthy man in Baghdad lost everything and had to work hard for daily food. One night a voice in a dream told him his fortune awaited him in Cairo.

He resolved to travel, crossed the long road to Egypt, and reached Cairo exhausted. Knowing nobody there, he entered a mosque and slept until morning.

That night thieves passed through the mosque toward a nearby house. Its occupants called for help, and the thieves fled before the police arrived, leaving the visitor asleep.

The police mistook the stranger for one of the thieves, beat him severely, and jailed him for three days. Then their chief questioned his origins and purpose.

He explained that a dream had promised him wealth in Cairo. The police chief laughed, saying a man should not leave home merely because of a dream.

The chief described his own recurring dream: a Baghdad house with a garden and fountain hiding buried money. He had never bothered to go there.

The traveler recognized every detail of his own home. Keeping the discovery secret, he accepted a little money for the journey and hurried back to Baghdad.

Back home he dug beneath his fountain and found a great treasure. The journey to Cairo had led him not to wealth there, but to wealth hidden in his own courtyard.
