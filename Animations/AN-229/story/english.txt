Ibrahim of Mosul, a celebrated musician, obtained Saturday leave from Harun al-Rashid to spend time with his household. He ordered the gates closed to visitors.

Despite the orders, a dignified old man in white with a silver-handled staff appeared. Ibrahim was annoyed but greeted him politely.

The visitor entertained Ibrahim with Arab stories and poems until his irritation faded. After being offered hospitality, the stranger asked to hear him sing.

Ibrahim played the lute. The stranger praised him and addressed him familiarly, irritating him, but Ibrahim sang again after hearing a promise of reward.

The old man then asked to perform. Ibrahim doubted his ability but handed him the lute, and the music surpassed anything he had heard.

The stranger sang of separation and longing; even the walls seemed to echo him. When Ibrahim requested a repetition, the man said he already knew the tune and vanished.

Ibrahim searched the house. The guards knew of no visitor, but everyone had heard the music. A hidden voice identified itself as Abu Murrah—the devil in the tale.

Ibrahim told Harun the story and reproduced the melodies from memory. Delighted, the caliph rewarded him and wished he could hear the mysterious visitor himself.
