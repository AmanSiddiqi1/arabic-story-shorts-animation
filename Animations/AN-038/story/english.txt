A fox entered a garden looking for food and found ripe grapes. While eating, it saw a falcon chasing a small partridge, which hid in its nest among the branches.

The falcon perched near the nest but could not reach the partridge. So it changed tactics, speaking gently: “Why fear me? I did not come to harm you but to help you find food.”

At first the partridge did not believe him. It had seen his sharp talons and knew he hunted birds. From inside the nest it asked, “If you wanted to help me, why were you chasing me?”

The falcon answered that the partridge looked hungry and he wished to guide it to plentiful grain. He spoke kindly and swore he meant no harm. The partridge listened, and its doubts began to fade.

At last the partridge left its nest and approached cautiously. The falcon did not wait: he seized it in his strong talons and lifted it from the ground. The bird understood too late that the promises were a trick.

Before dying, the partridge cried angrily, “You deceived me with words. You pretended friendship while planning to kill me. May my flesh become poison to you, and may you suffer for what you have done!”

The falcon ate the partridge, thinking it an easy meal. Soon his strength faded and feathers began falling from his body. He tried to fly, but his wings could no longer lift him.

The falcon fell to the ground and died on the spot. The fox had watched from afar how a hunt begun with deception and promises had ended. Then it left and continued on its way.
