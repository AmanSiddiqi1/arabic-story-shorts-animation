# AN-119 Abu Nuwas, the Three Guests and the Caliph: creative plan

Halftone Pop, 9:16, 171.42 s of narration + 3.4 s THE END hold (film ends 174.9 s). All times are seconds on the final MP3; every cue word and time is in `creative_plan.json` (17 scenes, 144 actions). The narration and subtitles are verbatim; the pictures are deliberately gentler than the words (section 8).

## 1. Narrative breakdown
- **Logline.** The poet answers the caliph's mockery with a cheeky joke, is sentenced to a donkey saddle, a parade and the sword, and jokes his way to a pardon and ten thousand dirhams.
- **Conflict.** A clown's tongue against a king's pride. Abu Nuwas cannot stop being funny, even when it is dangerous.
- **Beats.** Feast with empty cushions (2.42) > three guests invited (15.28) > a verse for each, nobody loses (37.66) > the knock (50.72) > the caliph (53.30) > mock judgeship (69.34) > the cheeky reply (76.62) > the caliph storms out (81.88) > NEXT DAY, the saddle and the order (100.74, 105.52) > the donkey act and the laughing ladies (116.44, 118.12) > the deadpan line to Jaafar (132.24 to 138.06) > the caliph understands (142.44) > laughter, pardon, 10,000 dirhams (149.68, 153.84, 159.42) > moral (169.70).
- **Surprises.** The knock is the caliph himself; the victim turns his own parade into a paid show; the punishment is called "his finest clothes".
- **Emotional arc.** Pride and fun > shock > cheek > dread (one honest gulp at 105.5) > defiance by clowning > relief. The caliph's arc mirrors it: mocking > stung > stone > a twitch of the mouth > helpless laughter.
- **Ending and moral.** Three tiles above Abu: a flexed arm (X), a crown (X), a speech balloon with HA! (tick). A wink, THE END.

## 3. Character acting
- **Abu Nuwas.** A performer: every pose is presented to an audience. Proud chest (tip -0.05), raised reciting hand, quick eyes (`gazePlan` saccades at 6.34 and 111.74). His turban is his mood meter: neat, crooked when merry (45.60), hopping off in shock (55.28), swallowed by the judge turban (68.52), gone under the saddle, back a little crooked at the end. The one honest fear beat is the gulp to camera at 105.52; after it he chooses to clown. The straight face (131.18 to 138.06) must be truly still: flat mouth, level lids, one slow blink, so that the saddle glint at 137.38 is the only "smile".
- **The caliph.** Slow, heavy, minimal. He never hurries except to leave (83.08). Mockery is a one-sided smile and a slow clap. In court he is stone (`noBlink`, folded arms) so that the single mouth twitch at 145.62 reads, then the laugh breaks in three stages: shoulders (148.14), burst (149.68), failed scowl with a tear (152.16).
- **The three guests.** Never in unison (`stagger(i)`): g1 confident, g2 the talker, g3 shy (hides a smile, last to stand, half hides behind g2). They watch the caliph and Abu like a tennis match in S7.
- **Masrur.** A wall with heavy lids. Heavy footfalls that make Abu hop (`kick`). His arc is tiny and funny: bored > one eyebrow (117.14) > a hidden smile, face turned away (119.42) > shaking shoulders (138.06) > an open smile at the end.
- **Jaafar.** Busy and proper; the double take (125.30) and the stifled laugh (138.06) are his two big moments.
- **The ladies.** Three different laughs: behind the hand, head back, knee slap.
- Anticipation and follow-through: 0.1 s dips before pointed gestures, `landSquash` on stops, `impactSquash` under the saddle, cape and ribbon lag, cups hopping on the slam, lamp `swing`.

## 4. Camera and composition
- Establish each set wide once (S1, S2, S6 reveal, S9, S11), then live in two-shots for the three duels: caliph and Abu (S6 to S8), Jaafar and Abu (S13, S14), Jaafar and caliph (S15). Use `twoShot` so neither face is cropped.
- Push-ins on decisions only: the invitation (18.76), the quip (75.70), the caliph's understanding (79.76 close; 142.44 close), Abu's fear (105.52), the moral (S17).
- One whip pan to the door on the knock (50.72) and one whip between the women's hall and the throne (138.88): the joke "travels".
- Faces and hands stay above world y 800; verse ribbons, balloons and labels live at y 380 to 560 and never cross a face. The order balloon is above the caliph at (700, 380); the thought cloud in S15 opens up left at (360, 400).
- Panel-only shake on the door slam (84.0) and the saddle drop (157.1).

## 5. Storytelling opportunities
- **Humour:** the turban as a mood meter; the giant judge turban; the tailor's presentation of the saddle; Masrur's hidden smile; the coin heap growing in the saddle while he is supposed to be shamed.
- **Suspense:** stillness after the knock (only pupils move); the caliph's unreadable stone face at court; the scimitar icon arriving as the third picture.
- **Surprise:** the door opening on the crown; Abu's idea (111.74) turning the walk of shame into a show.
- **Emotion:** the three empty cushions at the start (a host with no guests) and the gulp at 105.52 give the clown something to lose; the caliph's laugh "in spite of his anger" is played as a man losing a fight with his own face.
- **Running picture:** poetry is always the paper ribbon. It wins guests (S3), flatters them (S4), is "given to the caliph" (S14) and finally reaches his ear as a tiny roll (139.90).

## 8. Warnings and open questions
- **Mature source, discreet pictures.** The guests are adult gentlemen at a friendly dinner; the kiss is a no-contact courtly salute; cups are unlabelled; the "judge over procurers" insult is a mock turban and JUDGE OF MISCHIEF; the stripping is a change behind a screen into a long shirt; the execution is only an icon and a sheathed sword. All of these, the invented lettering and the added business are listed in `owner_questions` (11 entries, including three SCRIPT notes on English lines that are shorter than the Arabic).
- The words in the subtitles stay verbatim (wine, flirtation, kissed, procurers, strip, execute); the owner should confirm that is right for the audience.
- Many planned assets are not in `Library/` yet; build missing ones in the story under the planned names.
- The front-facing person cannot go on all fours; the donkey act is played upright.
- Large human cast (10) on four sets: no new sets, but staging effort is high.
