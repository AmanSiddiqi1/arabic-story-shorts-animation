Abu Nuwas prepared a luxurious feast and went looking for attractive company for food, wine, and flirtation. He saw three handsome youths and invited them in.

He recited verses praising the feast, and they accepted. Over food and wine they asked which was most beautiful. He praised and kissed each in turn.

As the drinking continued, someone knocked. Harun al-Rashid entered unexpectedly. Everyone rose respectfully, and the caliph asked Abu Nuwas what sort of gathering this was.

Harun mockingly appointed him judge over procurers. Abu Nuwas quipped, “Do you have a case to bring before me?” The caliph took offense and left angrily.

The next day Abu Nuwas attended court. Harun ordered Masrur to strip him, dress him in donkey gear, parade him before the palace women, and then execute him.

Masrur paraded him through the women’s chambers. Abu Nuwas kept joking, making them laugh and earning gifts. Jaafar the Barmecide then asked what had happened.

With a straight face, Abu Nuwas replied, “I gave the caliph my finest poetry; he gave me his finest clothes.” The joke reached Harun.

The caliph laughed despite his anger, pardoned Abu Nuwas before the execution, and awarded him ten thousand dirhams. One last joke saved the poet’s life.
