A court anecdote says Al-Amin, son of Harun al-Rashid, visited his uncle Ibrahim ibn al-Mahdi and admired an enslaved musician playing the lute.

Ibrahim noticed his nephew’s interest and sent the musician to him with luxurious clothes and jewels, expecting the gift to please him.

Al-Amin suspected his uncle had previously been intimate with her. Troubled by that belief, he sent her back despite the costly gifts.

A servant told Ibrahim the reason for the rejection. Realizing that a false suspicion was the obstacle, he planned a gentle explanation.

Ibrahim had two lines embroidered in gold on a silk garment, declaring that he had only seen and heard the musician and had not been intimate with her.

He dressed her in the embroidered silk, gave her the lute, and sent her back. Before Al-Amin, she sang verses gently reproaching his rejection.

Al-Amin listened to her song, read the embroidered message, understood his uncle’s meaning, and accepted the musician he had previously returned.

Al-Amin gave her quarters in his palace, thanked his uncle, and appointed Ibrahim governor of Rayy. The anecdote ends with a misunderstanding resolved.
