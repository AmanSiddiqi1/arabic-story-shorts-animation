# KD-017 The Hesitant Thief: creative plan

Film length 169.2 s (narration 166.21 s, last word ends 166.02 s, THE END held to 169.2). All times are seconds on the final MP3. The machine-readable storyboard with every action, cue word and subtitle chunk is `creative_plan.json`; this file explains the direction.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A servant and his friend are robbing the house when the owner knocks. The servant says "escape by the back door, by the well". The friend finds the door open, sees no well, and will not leave until someone explains the well. He is caught.

**Conflict.** Not thieves against owner: it is the servant against his friend's one fixed idea, with the knocking as a clock.

**Beats (cue word, time).**
1. Setup: the servant knows every door (باب 6.92). FRONT DOOR left, BACK DOOR right.
2. Setup: the deal with the friend (السرقة 11.40) and the routine: owners out, friend in, coins shared (ويقسمان 19.62).
3. LATER card "ONE NIGHT..." in the pause 21.34-22.36.
4. Rising: the family leaves, the two fill sacks (يجمعان 31.48); false calm, hourglass full (وقتًا 38.42).
5. Inciting: the knock (فجأة 43.08, طرقًا 46.06); OPEN UP! (افتحوا 52.44).
6. Turn: "back door, by the well" (البئر 61.90) and the pointing arm (وأشار 62.54).
7. Reveal and surprise: door open, way out clear (المفتوح 69.80); no well (بئرًا 73.36).
8. Comic: he runs back to ask (عاد 74.86, أين 80.68).
9. Comic escalation: the explanation (83.52-93.70), the friend's logic and planted feet (كيف 101.28).
10. Suspense: louder knocks, angry owner (يغضب 110.06); begging and pushing (توسل 111.30); still asking (البئر 121.42).
11. Climax: the door opens (فُتح 125.24), the owner sees the sacks (الأكياس 130.98), then the two arguing (يتجادلان 135.38).
12. Climax: he runs, too late (فات 141.52), caught on the threshold (فأمسك 142.02).
13. Comic payoff: "I found the door, but not the well!" (البئر 157.00).
14. Resolution: to the judge (الحاكم 162.78); the sacks stay (وبقيت 163.40).

**Emotional arc.** Smug ease, shock, hope (the open door), disbelief, mounting panic for the servant against total calm in the friend, the catch, and an ending of amused disbelief.

**Moral.** When the way out is in front of you, take it; fussing over a detail that does not matter costs you the chance. It is shown, not lettered: the back door stands open in the background from 68.3 to the last frame.

## 3. Character acting

- **The friend (thief)** is the comic engine. From 74.86 to 136.7 he is never afraid: calm lids (0.2), level brows, unhurried turns, polite gestures (shoulder tap, one raised finger, folded arms). He thinks he is the reasonable one. His eyes go up to his thought cloud whenever the well is mentioned. Panic arrives only at 137.2, in one burst (`runInPlace`, eyes wide).
- **The servant** carries all the panic, and it grows in steps: deadpan blink (82.1), head clamp (83.5), shove (91.6), triple look between friend and front door with `tremble` (99.2), silent scream (102.8), knees and clasped hands with `wet` eyes (111.3), flat on the floor beating it with a fist (121.4). Mask on, mask off in S2-S3: the bow and polite wave drop to a grin the moment the door shuts.
- **The owner**: anticipation before the shout (`anticipate`, chest fill at 49.16), follow-through on the turban (2-3 frames late), anger as `flush` plus steam, eyes before head when he hears the argument (133.6). After the catch his anger turns to plain curiosity (brow raised, palm open); his last look to the lens with a hand over his face is the audience's reaction.
- **The wife**: silent, never in step with her husband, leans away from his temper, peeks round the door frame.
- **Setup and payoff pose**: the thumb over the shoulder with a proud nod at 78.38 is repeated exactly at 153.60 (same `aR` arm angle).
- **Looks to camera** are used three times only: the pair at 55.38, the servant at 82.9, the owner at 160.36 (plus the frozen pair at 136.75).
- **Group rule**: the pair never move in unison; the friend reacts 3 frames after the servant (`stagger`), and flinches shrink with each knock.
- Blinks: `LIFE.t = t` for everyone; `noBlink` in the held shocks.

## 4. Camera and composition

- One cutaway set, 1800 wide: street (x 0..400), hall (x 420..1280), yard (x 1320..1800). FRONT DOOR in the left wall, BACK DOOR in the right wall. The hall fits one zoom 1.0 shot (camera x 850), used whenever both doors must be read together: 6.9, 62.5, 133.6, 161.3.
- Screen direction is fixed: danger comes from the left, escape is to the right. Every run to the right is toward freedom; every run to the left is the friend going the wrong way.
- Two-shots (zoom 1.5-1.8) for the arguments; static in S10 so the friend's stillness is felt; `twoShot()` in S15 so the open door stays in frame behind him.
- Whips: to the street side at 47.82, and after the friend at 76.08 (the camera first holds 0.7 s on the abandoned open door: the key image of the film).
- Inset circle (top left, r 120) in S12 keeps the bulging front door present while we stay at the back door. Hourglass meter top right from 36.16 to 146. The thought cloud sits top centre; nothing covers a face.
- Panel-only shake on the big knocks (4-5 px). Keep heads and action above panel y 1250; subtitles own the lower third.
- One LATER transition (21.34, ONE NIGHT...) with the day to night switch hidden under the card.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour**: the well icon that is small in the servant's balloon and huge in the friend's thought cloud; the exit left alone on screen; the friend walking out into the yard, free, and walking back in; the legs that finally run only when a hand already holds the collar; the friend not running even when the owner lets go.
- **Suspense**: the hourglass draining from PLENTY OF TIME to TOO LATE!; knocks that grow, then the silence at 123.08; the long shadow reaching the sacks before the owner does.
- **Surprise**: the first jolt of the door after the slow drift (43.08); the turn away from the open door (74.86).
- **Dramatic irony**: the servant wiping his brow in relief in the background at 71.2 while the friend freezes on the threshold.
- **Emotion**: the servant's kneeling plea with wet eyes; the quiet last image of the sacks and the unused open door.
- Lettering diet: 10 sound or speech words in 169 s, plus 5 labels.
