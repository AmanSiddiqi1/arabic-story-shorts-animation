# ALI-04 "The Merchant of Oil" (80s Cel Anime): Fable review, round 2 (v2)

Film reviewed: `Animations/ALI-04/renders/ALI-04_v2.mp4` (140.0 s). Sources: 9 of the 24 contact strips in `review/v2_pack/strips/` (18-24, 36-48, 72-78, 102-114, 120-138 s, 4 frames per second), 56 panel stills pulled from the MP4 at about 1.5 s spacing for the rest of the film, 8 larger panel stills at the moments cited below, and an ffmpeg `freezedetect` pass over the story panel.

Not checked: sound was not listened to. Stepped cel timing, landing squash and follow-through fall between sampled frames and cannot be judged from stills. The freeze scan (tolerance 0.004, 0.8 s minimum) flagged nothing, but the CRT noise in the picture could hide a still cel, so it only rules out a fully frozen panel. The v1 pack was not reopened; the comparison is against the round 1 notes.

## Verdict: PASS WITH NOTES

No BLOCKER. The three reasons for REVISE in round 1 are gone: the release is readable, the leader holds the cliffhanger to the last frame, and the signal has a pebble and an unobstructed "nobody answered". Every narrated event is on screen in order, subtitles are sharp in both languages, TO BE CONTINUED is present, no broken frames. The film is ready for the owner. The notes below are polish; none needs another review round.

## Notes (polish, most useful first)

### 1. 110.2-118.0 s: the guards are one block in front of the jars
Three guards and the captain stand shoulder to shoulder at the guest door (110.2-112.2), then at 114.5 about eight figures stand in one overlapped line across the jar row, covering the jars the scene is about. It still reads as "surrounded", so it does not hurt the story, but it is the weakest composition in the film and sits between two good shots. Fix: at 110.2-112.2 put one guard left of the door and two right of it with a body width between them, spears at different angles. At 113.2-118.0 use the note 1 capture layout early: guards in the gaps at the two ends of the row and neighbours left of jar 1, so the lids stay visible. Stagger their arrival by 2 to 4 frames each so they do not stop together.

### 2. 105.96-107.6 s: the pebble is still small
The arc, TIK! and the catch carry the beat, but the pebble itself is a few pixels on a phone and the first target lid is at the left edge behind a guard's helmet. Fix: pebble radius 12 to about 18 with a short streak behind it; move the guard at the left edge one jar further left (or pan 60 units left) so lid 5 and its hop are in clear view.

### 3. 72.6-77.7 s: the neighbours are a clump at one door
Baker, old woman, boy and Morgiana overlap in one shape and the boy who is sent for the guards is hard to find. Fix: spread them over about 260 units, boy in front and nearest the street, and let him leave on "ihdar" with an anticipation crouch and a dust puff while the others turn their heads after him.

### 4. 64.5-70.0 and 96.3-100.0 s: the kitchen is sparse
One table, two shelves and a floor of bricks filling the lower third. Against the owner's "full scenes" point it is the emptiest set in the film. Fix: add a foreground layer as in the yard (a stool, oil jugs, a basket of onions, a hanging lamp with a warm pool, steam from the pot on the hearth) and lower the camera a little so the floor takes less of the frame.

### 5. 135.5-140.0 s: the procession under the title
The thieves and guards walk as a tight row and TO BE CONTINUED sits across the paving just below their feet. The leader above them is what matters and he is clear, so this is taste only. Fix if cheap: keep the 80-unit gap but alternate two depths (y offset 8 to 10) so the heads do not form one line.

### 6. 118.4-121.5 s: the climb
The leader goes up the trellis as a sliding pose. Fix if budget allows: alternate hands and feet on twos, cape lagging by 3 to 4 frames, a small squash as he lands on the roof at 121.2.

### 7. 96.3-99.5 s: Morgiana at the kitchen window
She stands with a hand at her chin for the whole beat. A slow lean toward the window on "turaqib", a blink on threes and a head turn at the cat would keep the hold alive.

## Reusable techniques worth adding to the Library
- **Reveal row with a counter**: figures rise behind a row of props, then step out one at a time under a chrome counter and line up in front of their own prop (122-129). Works for any "they were hiding in N things" beat.
- **Foreground dressing pass** (`cyFore`, sliding faster than the set): the cheapest way found so far to meet the owner's "full scenes" point on a reused set.
- **Villain held over the outro**: the antagonist stays in the upper frame with a one-picture thought cloud while the resolution walks past below; a good default for the series cliffhangers.
- **Thrown-object beat**: dotted arc, small italic sound word at the hit, second arc ending in a catch with a glint.
