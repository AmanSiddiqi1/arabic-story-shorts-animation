A merchant owned a farm with a bull and a donkey. Every morning the bull went out to plow under the sun and returned exhausted. The donkey lived in the stable, ate good food, and rested most of the time.

One evening the bull looked at the donkey and said, “You are lucky. You sleep and eat while I work all day and come home aching.” The donkey pitied him and thought of a way for him to rest.

The donkey said, “Tomorrow pretend to be ill. If they put the plow on you, lie down and refuse to rise. When they give you food, do not eat. They will think you cannot work and let you rest.”

The bull followed the donkey’s advice. The worker saw him lying down and could not make him plow. He told the farmer that the bull was ill. But the merchant understood animal speech and had already heard their plan.

The merchant told the worker, “Leave the bull today and take the donkey instead.” The worker harnessed the donkey to the plow and made him work for hours. Unused to such labor, the donkey returned exhausted and sore that evening.

The bull greeted the donkey happily and said, “Thank you for your advice! I had a peaceful day.” At first the donkey said nothing, angry with himself. Then he thought about how to escape this situation.

At last the donkey said, “I heard our owner say that if the bull remains ill tomorrow, he will send him to the butcher.” The bull trembled with fear. He did not know that the donkey was saying this to get him back to work.

The next morning the bull stood up quickly and ate all his food. When the worker came, he went out to the field and began plowing again. The donkey stayed in the stable recovering, having tasted a day of his companion’s work.
