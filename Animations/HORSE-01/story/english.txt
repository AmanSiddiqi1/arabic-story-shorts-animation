A king held a palace celebration and invited inventors. A stranger arrived with a small cart, pushing a black wooden horse decorated with shining brass fittings.

Some guests laughed at the motionless horse, but the man said, “This is no ornament; it can fly faster than the wind.” The king asked him to prove his claim before everyone.

The inventor turned a small knob beside the horse’s neck. The wooden figure rose from the ground, flew over the garden, and returned. The crowd applauded, and Prince Firuz came closer to inspect it.

“Let me try it,” said Firuz. The inventor warned him to learn the ascent and descent controls first, but the prince quickly mounted and turned the knob he had seen.

The horse shot upward like an arrow, and the palace shrank beneath him until it looked like a toy. The prince called from the sky but heard no answer and did not know how to descend.

Firuz tried turning the knob the other way, but the horse rose higher. He watched clouds pass beneath his feet, felt the cold, and searched for a sign of how to descend.

He remembered the inventor pointing near the ear. Leaning forward, Firuz inspected the horse’s head and found a small lever beneath its carved mane.

Firuz looked at the earth and the lever. “Perhaps this is my way to safety,” he said, reaching for it as the sun set.
