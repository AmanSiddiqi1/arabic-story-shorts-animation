Verdict: REVISE

# KD-010 The Louse and the Flea: Fable review, round 1 (film v1, 169.83 s)

Reviewed from all 29 strips of `review/v1_pack/strips/` (4 fps) plus 25 extra frames pulled into `review/v1_pack/extra/` (sheets A to E). Narration, subtitle text and subtitle timing must stay exactly as they are; every fix below is staging, acting, camera or lettering only.

## What works (keep as is)

- The two scales and the macro lens bridge (0.6 to 13.7, dive at 13.7) are clear at once; the day-to-night window (8.2 to 10.5) reads well.
- S2: tiptoe, SHH, finger twitch, freeze, NOT A THING. Good setup of her rule.
- The warning thought cloud (49.2 to 53.5: closed eye, eye snaps open, OUCH on the arm) is the clearest piece of visual storytelling in the film.
- The SLEEP meter as a clock (drop and red flash at 75.5, NOT YET! at 85.4, AWAKE! at 95.7, full only at 146.5) works as suspense and as irony.
- Leap and bite 87.1 to 91.9: good whip, the louse's small "NO!" from the nook is a lovely touch.
- The lamp disc creeping to the flea's feet with the giant scanning eye (117.0 to 119.7), the fall and zigzag (120 to 123.7).
- SAFE HERE turning into SAFE HERE? under the lamp (128.5 to 130.2).
- Subtitles: Arabic large and clean in every sampled frame, English in blue below, one or two lines, never clipped, in step with the narration. The re-translated English is accurate against the Arabic in all 34 chunks; no change requested.
- Family-friendly: no squashing shown; the star twinkle is a gentle reading.

## Why REVISE

Three story events are not readable in the picture: the capture of the louse (she vanishes before the fingers arrive), the "got rid of her" flick (nobody is at the window), and the flea's final exit (a 20 px dark speck on a dark window for the last 7 seconds). On top of that the lamp glow is painted over the servants for about 40 s and makes them look like ghosts. These are the emotional climax and the ending, so they are worth a v2.

## Notes (most important first)

### 1. 159.94 to 166.9, MAJOR: the ending is invisible
What is wrong: after the cut to the room at 160.0 the flea is a few dark pixels climbing the wall (160.5 to 161.2) and then a roughly 20 px black silhouette on the dark-blue window pane (see `extra/E.jpg`). The planned "silhouette against the moon" does not happen: the moon is top centre of the window, the flea is at the bottom on the sill. The last head turn (164.68) and the hop out (165.58) cannot be seen at all. The viewer watches an empty room for 7 s.
Why it matters: the last sentence is about him; the film ends without its protagonist.
Fix:
- Bring back the macro lens, the same device as 22.38 (THE FLEA). Pop it at 161.76 `ليلته` over the bare wall right of the window, pointer to the sill, diameter as at 22.5 or larger, and keep it to 166.6.
- Inside the lens: flea on the sill in normal colours, lit (not a silhouette), the moon disc directly behind his head (move the moon in the lens view only). No bundle (it stays in the bed, which is the point).
- Acting in the lens: 161.76 lands on the sill with `landSquash`, facing out. 162.72 `مكان` slow head turn back toward the bed, `lid` 0.5, antennae low. 163.72 `يبدو` the bed sparkle fires (already there); his pupils flick to it and hold, `noBlink`, about 0.7 s. 164.68 `مريحًا` antennae sink further, one slow blink, head turns away. 165.58 `وآمنًا.` `anticipate` crouch 0.25 s, then `hop` out of the lens and out of the window; the lens holds on the empty sill 0.4 s and closes before THE END at 166.9.
- Keep the camera pull-out to the opening frame, but start it at 162.72 as planned and have the full room by 164.7, so the lens and the wide room coexist.

### 2. 135.34 to 138.1, MAJOR: the capture; the louse disappears before she is caught
What is wrong: she is visible under her thread from 135.5 to 136.5, then at 136.7 the nook is suddenly empty (thread only, `extra/C.jpg` frame 5) while two thin fingers descend; at 137.5 to 137.7 the fingers pinch a tiny grey speck about a fifth of her drawn size (`extra/D.jpg` frame 1). It reads as "she escaped and they picked up a crumb". The fingers are also narrower than the louse, so the giant scale built up all film collapses. Earlier (132.5 to 135.2) she is also completely invisible for almost 3 s while the narration says she tried to hide deeper and was seen.
Why it matters: this is the unfair moment the whole story is built to deliver. It must be unmistakable that she, the careful one, is caught.
Fix:
- 132.14 `الاختباء`: she pulls the thread over herself but stays partly visible: back, antennae tips and two eyes above the thread (do not hide her entirely by draw order). `tremble` 1.5 px, `sq` 0.9.
- 133.72 `لكن` tilt up to the giant eye as now; keep the nook in the bottom of frame with her eyes visible.
- 135.34 `رآها.` eye locks (as now), red "!" fully inside the panel (it is cut by the face disc at 135.5), push in on her; at 135.6 her pupils come to the lens, `noBlink`, hold to 136.5. This is in the plan and is not in v1: at 135.7 she looks sideways.
- 136.64 `أمسك`: a fingertip and a thumb tip enter from the top, each tip clearly wider than her whole body (use `bigHand` at the scale of the sleeping hand at 14 to 19 s; only the two tips need to be in frame). Their shadow (flat dark tone) slides over the nook; her eyes go up to them, `wide`, antennae flat. She stays in place, same size, to the end of the shot.
- Cut to the room at 138.14 `وتخلّص` with the tips about one body height above her: no contact, no shrunken speck. Remove the tiny pinched figure.

### 3. 138.14 to 140.5, MAJOR: "got rid of her" is staged away from the window
What is wrong: servant A stays in the cluster next to the master, lifts a fist beside the master's face, and FLICK! with a twinkle appears on the window a third of the room away (`extra/D.jpg` frame 2). Servant B is fully hidden behind A. Nothing connects the hand to the window.
Why it matters: without the walk and the arm out of the window the viewer cannot tell what happened to her.
Fix (as the plan describes):
- 138.14: A is already two steps from the group, walking left to the window with `walk`/`footfalls`, right arm held out straight in front at arm's length, pinched hand (`grip`), head leaning back in disgust (`tip` -0.1, `lid` 0.5). A small lens is optional: a 2 s lens on the pinched hand showing her eyes would make it certain that it is her.
- 139.0: he stops under the window, arm up and out over the sill (`anticipate` a short wind-back of the wrist).
- 139.24 `منها.`: the flick, a small grey dot arcs out and up, FLICK! beside his hand (not on top of the moon), star twinkle at 139.7 outside the window.
- The master stays at the bed with crossed arms, watching (`gaze` to the window); B stands apart at the foot of the bed holding his lamp, visible, turning his head 8 frames late.
- 140.2: A dusts his hands (two quick claps) and turns back; this buys the transition to the floor search at 140.94 `يجد`.

### 4. 105.5 to 146.0, MAJOR: the lamp light is painted over the servants
What is wrong: the `lampLight` disc is drawn on top of the actors, so servant A and often B are bleached to a pale, semi-transparent look for the whole of S11, S14 (`extra/B.jpg` frame 5, `extra/C.jpg` frames 1 and 2, `extra/D.jpg` frame 2). They read as ghosts or as a rendering fault, and their faces lose contrast exactly when they have to act (searching, shrugging).
Why it matters: about 40 s of the film, including the search and the capture aftermath.
Fix: draw the light disc after the set and before the actors (so it lights wall, bed and floor only), or keep it on top but at a much lower alpha with the actors' heads and bodies excluded (evenodd clip). Put the bright core at the lamp flame, not on the servant's chest. Check stills at 108.5, 113.3, 139.3 and 142.5. The macro-view lamp disc (117 to 137) is fine and should not change.

### 5. 107.9 to 116.7, MAJOR: the search hides the bed it is searching
What is wrong: master, B and A stand in a row facing the camera directly in front of the mattress. The pillow lift (112.3 to 113.0) is visible, but the blanket shake (113.26) and the mattress-edge lift (114.32) are small arm moves in front of bodies, and the HER HOME label at 115.4 points its arrow at servant A's belt (`extra/C.jpg` frame 2) because the nook is behind him.
Why it matters: the tension of P6 is that the light is working its way toward her home. The viewer has to see the bedding and where her home is.
Fix:
- By 107.9 the master steps back to far left (near the door) and points at the bed (`pointFwd`), so the bed is clear.
- A goes to the head of the bed (right), B to the foot (left), both turned three-quarter toward the bed, not toward the camera. Mattress edge and piping stay visible between them.
- 112.32 `الوسائد`: A lifts the pillow high and peers under (as now). 113.26 `والغطاء،`: B grabs the blanket hem with both hands and shakes it, two visible ripples running along the blanket; B is 8 frames late and looks under his own foot at 113.9. 114.32 `ويرفعون`: A puts the lamp down low and lifts the mattress corner 30 to 40 px; the piping bends.
- 115.36 `أطراف`: instead of the label on the floor, pop the macro lens on the nook (her two worried eyes, lamp glow arriving at the lens rim) with HER HOME attached to the lens. This reuses the film's own device and removes the misdirected arrow.

### 6. 123.5 to 124.9 and 147.4 to 159.9, MAJOR: the floor crack reads as a blue bucket on a pole
What is wrong: in the floor macro view the crack is a dark-blue trapezoid sitting on the floor line with a black vertical tile joint under it. With the flea diving in (123.7) it just about reads, but when he comes out and perches on its rim for 12 s (148 to 159, `extra/D.jpg` frame 3) he looks like he is sitting on a bucket on a stick. In the room view the same crack is a small zigzag in a tile.
Why it matters: "hid in a small crack" and "came out of hiding" are the script's words for two beats, and SAFE points at this object.
Fix: redraw the macro crack as a jagged fissure lying in the floor plane: an irregular black zigzag shape wholly below the wall line, wider in the middle, with two or three hairline branches, paper-coloured chipped edge. Move the vertical tile joint 80 px or more to one side so it does not look like a handle. Keep the evenodd clip for the dive. When he climbs out (147.44 `خرج`): antennae first, then head, then he pulls himself up and stands on the tile beside the crack, not on top of it.

### 7. 157.56 to 159.9, MAJOR: "then jumped away" is a shuffle
What is wrong: on `ثم قفز بعيدًا` he makes two or three very small moves to the right and ends half hidden behind the dark bed leg, still in frame at 159.7. There is no jump and he does not go away.
Why it matters: it is the action verb of the sentence and the hand-off to the final shot.
Fix: 157.56 `ثم` he turns away from the bed (left, toward the window side, matching where he appears at 160.0). 157.98 `قفز`: `anticipate` 0.2 s, then three real `hop` arcs with `landSquash`, each lower and heavier than his cocky hops in S3 (height about 60 percent, longer squash, antennae trailing low). One look back over the shoulder in the air or on the second landing at 158.62 (`gaze` up to the bed, 0.3 s). He leaves the frame by 159.6; the camera does not follow, holding the empty tile and the crack for the last 0.3 s.

### 8. 79.6 to 84.2 and 126.0 to 126.5, MINOR: the louse is too dark inside the nook
What is wrong: in the nook she is drawn dark blue on a dark blue interior; only her eyes read. The slow head shake at 81.10 `فبقيت` is not visible and at 81.5 to 82.2 the eyes barely move.
Why it matters: this is her last free choice (she stays), and it is the contrast shot to the flea's daydream.
Fix: lighten her shadow tint by about 35 percent (mid grey-blue with the normal black outline) or add a pale rim light on her head and back so the silhouette separates. Make the head shake two clear swings of about 12 px with the antennae following (`swing`), then `brow` -0.6 and a hold with `noBlink` under SAFE HERE.

### 9. 55.8 to 95.7 and 145.2 to 147.4, MINOR: SLEEP meter size and collisions
What is wrong: the DEEP tick label is about 24 px high at full size (below the 28 px label minimum) and hangs under the bar where it is easy to miss; during the bite close-up (88.0 to 91.9) the meter lies across the giant's closed eye and CHOMP! lands on top of the word DEEP (`extra/B.jpg` frames 2 and 3). The dinner-plate cloud at 77.2 to 79.5 also touches the meter and covers the giant's eyelid, the real sleep signal.
Fix: scale the whole meter by about 1.3 and letter DEEP at 30 px or more, placed at the right end of the bar. In the close-up 87.5 to 91.9 anchor the meter to the top-left corner over the dark background and offset the camera target (or the giant face) so the eyelid is clear of it; place CHOMP! right of and below the meter with at least 30 px of clearance. Move the daydream cloud to the upper left under the meter (the plan's own rule: clouds never on a face).

### 10. 105.5 to 107.5 and 143.1 to 144.7, MINOR: figures pile up
What is wrong: when the servants run in, A passes straight through the master's position and at 106.5 to 107.2 three heads are stacked in one clump; the planned bump (B into A at 107.02 `فجاؤوا`) cannot be read. At 143.2 to 144.5 the master walks back to bed through both servants.
Fix: at 105.2 (door bursting) the master turns and takes one step back toward the headboard. A stops with a `landSquash` skid about 90 px short of him; B arrives 8 frames later, bumps A's back (A lurches forward 15 px, lamp swings, small dust puff), both lamps `swing`. Keep at least 40 px of air between the three silhouettes at 107.5. At 143.14 `فعاد` the servants step apart first (A right, B left) and the master walks between them to the bed.

### 11. 28.5 to 36.0, MINOR: the flea's charm act is under-played
What is wrong: for 6 s the two-shot is static; the flea's spin on `أجمل` (30.76) is a small lean, and the pleading mask at 32.64 `اسمحي` with the one-eyed peek at 34.48 (the proof it is an act) does not register at 4 fps or in the full-size still at 33.0: his eyes look the same half-lidded as before. The louse keeps one neutral smile throughout.
Why it matters: this is where the audience should enjoy him and start to distrust him.
Fix: 30.04 arms-wide flourish and a full turn on the spot on 30.76 with antennae and bundle trailing (`swing`); 32.64 drop the bundle, deep bow (`tip` 0.3, `sq` 0.85), forelegs clasped, eyes large and `wet` with raised inner brows; 34.48 one eye pops open sideways at her for 0.4 s then shuts again; 35.38 `الليلة` one foreleg holds up a single "just one" digit. Louse: leans back 10 px on the flourish, `gaze` follows the spin, a blink at the bow, a small half-step out of the nook on 36.18 `رحّبت`. Add a slow push-in of about 8 percent from 32.6 to 36.0.

### 12. 154.4 to 157.2, MINOR: pale smear over the empty nook
What is wrong: a translucent pale ellipse lies across the nook and the label (`extra/D.jpg` frame 4). It lowers the contrast of the quietest shot and looks like a leftover lamp disc; the EMPTY label fades in through it and looks washed out until 155.7.
Fix: remove the ellipse. Let the shot be plain: dark nook, her thread, his bundle with its stick at the right. Bring EMPTY in crisply at 155.70 `القملة` (not earlier), or drop the label and let the image speak; a single slow 4 percent push-in is enough.

## Reusable techniques for the Library
- Macro lens with pointer as the bridge between room scale and insect scale, including "dive through the lens" as a change of scale (13.7). Worth documenting as the standard way to keep a tiny character present in wide shots, and it should be the default for any wide shot in which the protagonist is under about 40 px (note 1).
- Generic meter (`sleepMeter`) as an audience clock with a red flash on a drop.
- Label that changes under pressure (SAFE HERE to SAFE HERE?).
- Rule to add to the guide: light discs (`lampLight`) go under the actors, never over them (note 4); and giant body parts in macro shots must be wider than the insect they threaten (note 2).

Second review advice: Yes, a second Fable review of v2 is warranted. Notes 1 to 7 restage the climax and the ending (capture, flick, search, crack, exit and final shot), they change draw order for a long stretch (lamp light) and add new lens shots, so the result cannot be judged from the notes alone. A short comparison review limited to 105 to 170 s plus spot checks of notes 8, 9 and 11 would be enough.
