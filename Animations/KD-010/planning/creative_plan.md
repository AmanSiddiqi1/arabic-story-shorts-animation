# KD-010 The Louse and the Flea: creative plan

Film length 169.84 s (narration 166.84 s + 3.0 s THE END). 16 scenes, 181 timed actions. All times are seconds on the final MP3 and every cue is a word start from `planning/inputs/timeline.md`. The machine-readable storyboard is `creative_plan.json`; this file explains the direction behind it.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Idea in one line.** A careful louse lives unnoticed in a rich man's bed; she lets a cocky, hungry flea stay; he bites too soon, wakes the house and escapes, and the careful one pays for it.

**Conflict.** Patience against appetite. The louse has one rule (only when he sleeps, quietly). The flea agrees to it and breaks it within a minute.

**Spine (beats, with cue words).**
- Setup 0.62 `قملة`, 15.48 `بهدوء،`: her home, her rule, and proof that the rule works (one sip, nobody feels a thing).
- Inciting 22.38 `برغوث`: the flea hops in through the window.
- Turn 36.18 `رحّبت`: she welcomes him, and worries (40.02 `قلقة.`).
- Rising 54.36 `انتظر`: the warning; the SLEEP meter is set up. 65.90 `أعرف`: "I know what to do." 72.66 `جائعًا،`: hunger wins.
- Suspense 85.38 `ينام`: not fully asleep. Climax 90.10 `وعضّه`: the bite. Surprise 95.68 `ففتح`: the eyes snap open.
- Rising 107.92 `يحملون`: servants with lamps. Turn 123.68 `شقّ`: the guilty one is safe in a crack.
- Reveal 135.34 `رآها.`: the innocent one is seen. Emotion 139.24 `منها.`: she is got rid of.
- Emotion 155.70 `القملة`: the empty nook. Moral 164.68 `مريحًا`: he leaves the place that had looked comfortable and safe.

**Surprises.** The man stirring makes the flea hungrier although the meter drops; the eyes snapping open; the flea leaving without a glance at her; the lamp passing right over the flea's crack.

**Emotional arc.** Cosy and gentle (P1), charming and funny (P2 to P4), tense (P4 end, P5), slapstick panic (P5, P6), unfair and quietly sad (P7), still and empty (P8).

**Ending and moral.** The script gives no moral sentence, so the film adds none. The closing image repeats the opening wide shot at night: the same inviting bed, a dark nook, an empty window sill. THE END at 166.9, held to 169.84.

## 3. Character acting

- **Louse (careful, kind, anxious).** Low and slow: `walk()` with a small amplitude, `sq` about 0.92 when sneaking. Honest eyes that always look at whoever speaks; worry is a raised brow (`brow` -0.6 to -0.7), wrung forelegs, `tremble` 1 to 2 px, antennae down (`ant`). Her signature move is the tiptoe plus SHH (15.48), repeated as a lesson at 58.10 so the flea's leap at 87.12 reads as its exact opposite. Three held stillness beats with `noBlink`: 17.50, 82.36, 135.6 (the only time she looks into the lens in fear).
- **Flea (cocky, hungry, careless).** Everything springs: `anticipate` (crouch), `hop`, `landSquash`, with the bundle and antennae trailing on `swing`. Half-lidded (`lid` 0.35 to 0.6), smirk, chest out. His tell: the eyes keep sliding to the arm (`gazePlan` with 0.07 snaps) and the hind legs coil without him noticing (`crouch` creeping to 0.6 at 77.98). Pleading mask at 32.64 (wet eyes, `tip` 0.15) with a one-eyed peek at 34.48 to show it is an act. After the bite he never looks at her nook until 152.90; then antennae sink, `lid` 0.5, no tear.
- **Rich man.** Smug by day (`lid` 0.4, purse toss). Asleep: `breathe`, `lid` 1, Z. Waking is a chain with overshoot: twitch, scrunch, arm up (`settleTo`), eyes `wide` with a 2-frame flash, sink-and-spring out of bed (`anticipate`, `hop`, `landSquash`), then `flush`, fists, `tremble`, `cupMouth` shout and `pointDown` jabs.
- **Servants.** A tall, thin, eager leader; B short, stout, 8 frames late (`stagger`), smaller reactions, checks under his own foot. Lamps swing on their handles (`swing`). They never move in unison.
- **Giant parts in macro.** The sleeper is a hand that twitches (17.3), slides (75.0) and goes rigid (91.5), and a pale background face whose lid is the real sleep signal. The servant is a single huge scanning eye.
- **Listeners react.** In every dialogue shot both gazes are set; the comedy of P3 is that the listener's gaze is on the arm, not on the speaker.

## 4. Camera and composition

- **Two scales.** `bedchamber` (room, people readable) and `bedMacro` (insects 110 px or more on screen, the man a landscape). The **macro lens** inset bridges them in room shots, and the first change of scale (13.7) is a dive through the lens so the viewer understands where the macro world is.
- **Establishing and bookend.** S1 opens at zoom 1.0 on the whole room; S16 pulls out to the same frame at night.
- **Two-shots for the dialogue** (S4 to S7) with `twoShot`; louse always left facing right, flea right facing left, so screen direction never flips. The giant face is kept out of the two-shot until 38.38, when the widening camera reveals what she is worried about.
- **Push-ins on decisions:** her worry (40.02), his impatience (70.4), the nook (130.8), the eye lock (135.34).
- **Follow moves:** whip with the leap (87.12), crane with the fall off the bed (119.96 to 121.94), tilt along his eye line up to the bed (152.9).
- **Insert held still:** the empty nook, 154.4 to 157.2, the quietest shot of the film.
- **Layout safety.** Faces and key action stay inside the picture panel and above its bottom edge; the lens sits over bare wall (upper left), the SLEEP meter top left, thought clouds upper left or upper right, never on a face. Panel-only shake at 90.1 and 101.54.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Suspense: the SLEEP meter.** Set up as part of her warning (55.8), it becomes a clock the audience reads: creeping (71.1), dropping when the man stirs (75.5, which the flea misreads as dinner coming closer), short of DEEP at the leap (NOT YET!, 85.38), crashing to AWAKE! (95.8), and filling completely only after she is gone (145.34).
- **Humour:** the flea's bundle and trampoline entrance; the bow with a one-eyed peek; nodding without listening; the dinner-plate daydream and napkin; servant B bumping into A and checking under his own foot; two staggered shrugs beside the crack that hides the flea.
- **Irony:** the label SAFE HERE on her nook (82.36) comes back at 128.5 and shakes apart under the lamp; the flea's crack gets the plain label SAFE (124.1). The lamp sweeps over the guilty one and stops on the innocent one.
- **Surprise:** the 2-frame eye snap (95.68) after a long slow lid; the flea leaving without looking back (119.96).
- **Emotion:** her thread "blanket" that hides nothing (132.14), her one look to camera (135.6), then only the thread and his abandoned bundle in an empty frame (155.7). The capture itself is discreet: a pinch, a cut, a flick, a twinkle.
- **Payoff of the opening:** the sparkle that drew the flea to the bed (25.96) repeats at 163.72 as he leaves it.

- **OWNER: the SLEEP meter** is an added visual device, not in the script. Confirm it is welcome.
- **OWNER: the flea's remorse** (lowered antennae, one look back) is an interpretation; the script is neutral. Confirm, or play him simply relieved.
- The `insect` figure and the two sets are the main cost; nothing else is larger than S.
- Few pauses: the biggest physical beats have no sound effect, so anticipation, squash and lettering must carry them.
- The giant hand must use the same fill and outline as the `person` hands so room and macro views match.
- Lettering is at the upper limit (about 11 sound words plus labels). If a frame feels crowded, cut labels first: NOT A THING, HER HOME, NO FLEA.
- P8 chunk 1: the English begins with "When everything became quiet" while the Arabic ends with it; kept as one chunk so the pair still matches.
