A poor Iraqi man sought a way out of hardship. He heard of Abdullah ibn Malik, governor of Armenia, and his long-standing quarrel with Yahya al-Barmaki.

The man forged a letter supposedly from Yahya requesting help for its bearer. After a long journey, he presented it confidently to Governor Abdullah.

Abdullah doubted the letter because of his feud with Yahya. He promised a great reward if it was genuine, but severe punishment if it was false.

The man waited while Abdullah sent an envoy to Baghdad. Each day felt long: Yahya’s answer could bring him wealth or expose him to a humiliating beating.

Yahya immediately recognized the forgery. Yet he saw a chance for peace and told the envoy the letter was indeed his, urging Abdullah to honor its bearer.

Delighted, Abdullah gave the man two hundred thousand dirhams, ten Arabian horses, chests of clothes, servants and jewels. The estranged men began to reconcile.

Back in Baghdad, the astonished man admitted his trick to Yahya, thanked him for saving him from punishment, and offered him part of his gifts.

Yahya refused. The visitor had brought peace between two estranged men, he said. Yahya then matched the wealth and horses the man had received, doubling his reward.
