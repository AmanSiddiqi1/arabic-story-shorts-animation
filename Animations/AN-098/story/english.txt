A poor Iraqi man sought a way out of hardship. He heard of Abdullah ibn Malik, governor of Armenia, and of an old quarrel between him and the vizier Yahya ibn Khalid al-Barmaki.

The man forged a letter made to look as if from Yahya to the governor, asking him to honor and help its bearer. After a long journey, he presented it confidently to Governor Abdullah.

Abdullah read the letter and doubted it was genuine, because of his feud with Yahya. He told the man: if the letter is genuine, I will honor you, but if it is a lie, you will be severely punished.

The man stayed with the governor, awaiting the outcome, while Abdullah sent a messenger to Baghdad to ask Yahya himself. The days weighed heavily on the man, for the answer could bring him wealth, or expose him to beating and humiliation.

Yahya understood when he read the letter, and knew he had not written it. But he saw in it a chance to end the hostility, and told the envoy the letter was indeed his, urging Abdullah to honor its bearer.

Delighted, Abdullah gave the man two hundred thousand dirhams, ten Arabian horses, chests of clothes, servants and jewels. So Yahya’s name, once the cause of the quarrel, became the reason their friendship was renewed.

The man returned to Baghdad, hardly believing what had happened. He went to Yahya and confessed what he had done, thanked him for saving him from punishment, then offered him some of the many gifts he had received.

Yahya smiled and refused to take anything, saying the man had brought about peace between two friends who had fallen out. Then he gave him as much of his own wealth and horses as the man had received, so the poor man left with two rewards instead of one.
