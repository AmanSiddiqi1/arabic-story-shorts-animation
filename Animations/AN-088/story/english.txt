Searching the Yemeni desert for a missing she-camel, Abdullah bin Abi Kilabah saw a vast walled city. He approached hoping for help, but found it completely deserted.

Abdullah carried away pearls and fragrant treasures. When his report reached Caliph Mu’awiyah, he was summoned and questioned, but the caliph doubted the extraordinary description.

Abdullah produced objects he had brought back. Mu’awiyah consulted Ka’b al-Ahbar, who identified the place in the tale as Iram and began recounting King Shaddad’s ambition.

Shaddad, a mighty king, had read descriptions of Paradise. He ordered his rulers to collect precious metals and gems, even taking them from people, to build an earthly imitation.

Engineers found a spacious, watered plain and built magnificent palaces, columns, gardens, and canals. In the legend, the vast undertaking took centuries before completion.

Shaddad marched toward his completed city with his court. According to the legend, a terrible heavenly cry destroyed the procession one day before it arrived.

Ka’b said the road to Iram had vanished. Abdullah’s discovery thus framed a cautionary legend: a ruler spent a lifetime building splendor he never lived to see.
