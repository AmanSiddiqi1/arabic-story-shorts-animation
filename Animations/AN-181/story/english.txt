An old tale says Al-Hajjaj ordered a pious man imprisoned under heavy guard, with shackles on his limbs inside a locked enclosure.

The guard wondered how a powerless prisoner could withstand such a ruler. The man remained calm despite the locked doors and iron restraints.

The guard heard him repeatedly affirm that all creation and its dominion belonged to God, speaking confidently even in confinement.

He neither struggled with the jailer nor demanded that the locks be broken. He placed his trust in the One he believed could free him.

In the morning the jailer found the enclosure empty and the iron shackles on the floor. There was no trace of the prisoner.

Terrified of punishment, the jailer prepared a burial shroud and other funeral necessities before going to report to Al-Hajjaj.

The jailer described the empty prison and the captive’s words. Instead of executing him, Al-Hajjaj said the God he had invoked had freed him.

The tale ends with the discarded shackles as its sign of deliverance. It illustrates trust in God under oppression, not independently established history.
