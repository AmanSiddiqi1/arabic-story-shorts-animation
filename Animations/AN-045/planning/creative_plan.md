## 1. Narrative breakdown

**The idea in one line:** this is a film about where people look. The monkey pulls eyes one way while a hand works the other way; later a costly cloth pulls eyes to the outside of a bundle so nobody asks about the inside.

**Conflict:** appearance against contents. **Surprises:** the stolen bundle is worthless (71.52); the thief turns a bad theft into a better swindle (75.20). **Emotional arc:** amusement at the tricks, suspense at the steal, a laugh at the thief's disappointment, growing unease as the buyer walks into the trap, the wife's clear thinking, and a rueful, quiet ending. Nobody is punished on screen and nobody gloats. **Moral:** do not buy what you have not seen; a fine wrapping and a low price are the mark of a trick.

## 3. Character acting

- **Thief** (person, costume servant recoloured black, red cap, s 1.25). Default face: `lid 0.45`, `brow -0.4`, smirk. **Mask on / mask off:** a big friendly smile whenever a victim looks at him, dropped the instant they look away (50.70, 98.86). Thinks with his pupils: `gazePlan(t, plan, 0.07)` saccades at 9.74, 18.30-19.04, 55.78, 67.12. During the steal he does not blink (55.5-58.2) and only the arm moves (`handRW` eased over 1 s, `tip 0.1`). One freeze of 6 frames at the false alarm. Two looks to camera only: 2.82 and the wink at 83.38 (`wink: 'R'`).
- **Monkey** (Library monkey, s 0.7, red fez). The showman: every trick has `anticipate`, `hop`, `landSquash`; the fez and tail follow 2 frames late. It copies its master (finger twirl 5.52, finger wag 96.56, smug folded arms 83.38) and is the only character who shows a conscience of sorts: it covers its eyes at the rags (71.52) and shrugs at us (104.44).
- **Seller** (beanSeller, age 0.6). Three clear states: hopeful (`brow -0.5`, lean in), worn out (`lid 0.5 to 0.6`, sigh with `sq` breath, heavy `sit` with `settleTo`), delighted (laugh with `talk` pulses and shaking shoulders, `tip 0.12` toward the monkey, pupils on the monkey position every frame via `look`). He never learns he was robbed in this film: he is last seen waving the monkey goodbye.
- **Buyer** (merchant, green palette, s 1.3). Proud and springy: chest out (`sq 1.04`), rocks on his heels, rubs his hands with eyes shut. His smile closes step by step in S14 (nod slows 136.38, blinks 138.90, gulp 144.62) and ends in the shame pattern (looks up, eyes break away, head drops).
- **Wife** (villageWoman, s 1.25). Still and precise. Listens with her eyes (bundle, him, bundle), one brow up, then folded arms; speaks with pointing, a knuckle rap and palms up. Realisation at 145.84 is played as saccades then raised brows, not a light bulb. At the end her brow softens and she puts a hand on his shoulder.
- **Crowd**: the same five extras in both markets, each with its own seed, speed and delay (`stagger`); never in unison.
- Every character has a gaze target at all times, auto-blinks through `LIFE.t`, and breathes on holds (`breathe`).

## 4. Camera and composition

- **The staging rule of the theft (S5, S6):** thief on screen-left (x 1060), bundle (x 1220), seller on his stool (x 1400), monkey on screen-right (x 1590). Eyes go right, the hand comes from the left. Hold this geometry in every frame; the picture explains the trick without words.
- Establishing wide at the start (zoom 1.0), then push to the pair. A tracking shot for the routine in S2 and for the walk home in S12. Push-ins on the three decisions: the reach (54.4), the finished fake (79.8), the wife's questions (134.5-145.8).
- Inserts: the shirt (25.5-27.2) and the round cutaway on the bundle (80.58-83.2).
- Two-shots built with `twoShot` for thief and buyer (S11) and buyer and wife (S13-S15); the wife stays in frame during the ending.
- Lettering lives on the top band (panel y 80..230) or on the free side; never over a face. All faces and key action stay above panel y 1150.
- Transitions: whip to the seller (21.1), wipe to the alley (63.4), `LATER...` card at 84.0 hiding the cut to Market B, wipe into the house (125.3), cut to the dusk road and an iris out on the monkey (160.4).

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour:** the monkey copying its master; the seller hiding the hole behind his thumb; the thief looking through the hole in the shirt; the moth that flies out three times (26.38, 70.86, 163.86) so the audience recognises the same old clothes without a word.
- **Suspense:** stillness before the reach (only pupils move), the slow arm, the false alarm at 57.9, the tiptoe retreat. In S11 the buyer's finger on the knot at 117.30 is a last chance he lets go.
- **Surprise:** the held beat of hope at 70.14 before the rags; the cloth snapping open at 75.20.
- **Dramatic irony:** from 80.58 the audience has seen inside the bundle; every later smile of the buyer is watched with that knowledge. The crowd's treasure cloud (S10) and the buyer's profit clouds (S11, S12) show beliefs we know are false.
- **Setup and payoff:** two-finger signal (14.66 / 45.02); holed shirt held in two fingers (70.86 thief / 164.50 buyer); the sad zero (SOLD: 0 at 34.14 / WORTH: 0 at 165.80); the lazy stroll (1.18 / 156.52).
- **Emotion:** the tired seller's sigh; the wife's hand on the shoulder at the end.

- Cue every action with the word times in `creative_plan.json` (`cue.t`), which equal `timing.json` word starts. Anticipation may start up to 1.5 s earlier as listed.
- The person figure is front-facing only. Show direction with `gaze` (`lookAt`, `look`, `gazePlan`), `tip`, `lean` and hand targets (`handLW/handRW` for props, `PERSON_HANDS` for gestures). Use arm angles `aL/aR` for the big poses that must read at phone size (arms wide at 132.56, showman sweep at 87.64).
- Walks: a position function with `walk()` and `footfalls()`; the tiptoe retreat uses four eased segments with short holds.
- Monkey flips: rotate about (x, y - 90 s) and add `hop()`; keep `anticipate` and `landSquash` on every trick. Juggling: three `fig()` on phase-shifted arcs, pure functions of `t`.
- Thought clouds: `thoughtCloud(ctx, x, y, w, h, t, {tail, inside, scale, at})`, three uses only; figures inside at least 110 px tall on screen.
- Counters and stamps use the style primitives (counter, box, emphasis, label, arrow, transition, title); call `finish` last every frame.
