In a city lived a thief with a trained monkey that performed amusing tricks. He used it to distract marketgoers while looking for a chance to steal.

One day he saw a seller offering old, worn clothes. No one wanted them, and the weary seller wrapped the clothes in a bundle and sat to rest.

The thief made his monkey leap and play before the seller. While the seller watched, amused, the thief quietly stole the bundle.

The thief carried the monkey and bundle to a secluded spot. He rewrapped the rags in costly, beautiful fabric to make the contents seem valuable.

In another market he offered the bundle cheaply, with one strange condition: the buyer must not open it before paying. He wanted people to imagine valuable contents.

A buyer admired the elegant wrapping, thought it a bargain, and purchased it without inspecting it, hoping to resell at a profit.

His wife asked about the purchase. Hearing of the low price and hoped-for profit, she asked why he bought an unopened parcel and why anything valuable would be sold so cheaply.

She recognized the trick and warned him against stolen or unknown goods. The thief left with the money and monkey, while the buyer remained with old clothes worth less than he had paid.
