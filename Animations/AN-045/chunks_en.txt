In a city lived a thief|with a trained monkey that performed amusing tricks.|The man used to go to the markets,|and he used the monkey to distract people while he looked for a chance to steal.
One day he saw a seller carrying old, used clothes.|The seller called out his wares for a long time, but no one wanted to buy them.|At last he grew tired, so he gathered the clothes into a bundle and sat down to rest.
The thief came up to the seller and made the monkey jump and play in front of him.|The seller laughed and watched the animal's tricks closely.|At that moment the thief reached out and took the bundle of clothes without the man noticing.
The thief carried the monkey and bundle to a secluded spot.|He opened it and saw the worn out clothes,|then wrapped them again in a beautiful, expensive piece of cloth,|so that what was inside would look more valuable than it really was.
He went to another market and offered the bundle for sale at a low price.|with one strange condition: the buyer must not open it before paying.|He wanted people to think something very valuable was inside.
A man saw the beautiful cloth, liked it, and thought he had found a great bargain.|He bought the bundle without checking what was in it,|and took it home, expecting to sell it for a big profit.
His wife asked him what he had bought,|and he told her about the low price and the profit he was expecting.|She said, "How can you buy something without knowing what is inside?|And why would its owner sell it so cheaply if it were worth anything?"
The wife understood that there was a trick behind the deal,|and warned him against buying stolen or unknown goods.|As for the thief, he had taken the money and left with his monkey,|and the buyer was left with a bundle of old clothes not worth what he had paid.
