A once-rich man lost his fortune|and could no longer support his household.|His wife advised him to seek help from an old friend he knew and trusted,|so he went to him and told him of the poverty and hardship he had suffered.
The friend listened and gave him five hundred dinars to start new work.|Having worked as a jeweler in his youth,|so he rented a shop in the jewel market,|bought some goods and sat waiting for customers.
Three men came to the shop and asked him about his father.|He told them his father had died, and they asked whether he was really his son.|He gathered some market people, who testified before them that he was that late merchant’s son.
They handed him two bags|holding thirty thousand dinars, ornaments and precious stones.|They said his father had left these with them in trust before he died,|and that they had come to return them to his rightful heir.
Soon after, a woman came to the shop|and chose jewels priced at five hundred dinars, but paid three thousand dinars.|The man was amazed at these events|and felt the doors of fortune had suddenly opened to him.
He took five hundred dinars to his friend and said he wanted to repay what he had borrowed.|But the friend refused, telling him it had been a gift from the start,|then gave him a paper and asked him not to open it until he was home.
The man opened the paper and found in it verses revealing the truth.|The three men who had come to the market were his friend’s relatives,|and the woman who bought the jewels was his friend’s mother,|while the money and ornaments had been sent secretly by his friend.
The friend wrote that he had arranged it all|to help him without the embarrassment of asking for money face to face.|The man then understood that his new wealth was no unknown inheritance,|but the fruit of a loyal friend who had protected his dignity.
