A once-rich man lost his fortune and could no longer support his household. His wife urged him to ask an old trusted friend for help.

The friend gave him five hundred dinars. Having worked as a jeweler in his youth, the man rented a shop in the jewel market and began trading again.

Three men came asking about his father. Told the father had died, they asked witnesses from the market to confirm the jeweler was truly his son.

They handed him two bags holding thirty thousand dinars and precious jewels, saying his late father had entrusted the goods to them for his heir.

Soon a woman arrived and bought jewels worth five hundred dinars for three thousand. The astonished jeweler believed fortune had unexpectedly returned.

He brought five hundred dinars to repay his friend. The friend refused, saying it had been a gift, and handed him a sealed note to read at home.

At home he read verses revealing the truth. The three men were his friend’s relatives, the buyer was his friend’s mother, and all the money and jewels came from his friend.

The note explained that the friend had arranged everything to help without embarrassing him. The new fortune was not a forgotten inheritance, but an act of loyalty that preserved his dignity.
