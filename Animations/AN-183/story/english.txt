A tale describes a devotee accompanied by a cloud that shaded him and provided water as he traveled, a gift he attributed to God.

When his devotion weakened, the cloud vanished. Grieving its loss, he dreamed of a way to seek its return.

In his dream he was told to ask a righteous king to pray for him. He traveled to the king’s city and learned of a weekly public audience.

Seeing the king’s splendid procession, the devotee doubted his holiness. Nevertheless he presented his request at the public audience, and the king welcomed him.

The king led him to plain private quarters. There the devotee saw that the king and his wife lived simply and devoted themselves to worship.

The king explained that he had accepted the throne as a duty to prevent disorder, not from love of power; his public splendor differed from his private life.

The devotee asked them to pray. They spent the night in worship, and at dawn, the tale says, the cloud reappeared above him.

He thanked the couple and resumed traveling beneath the restored cloud. The lesson is that outward ceremony does not reveal a person’s private sincerity.
