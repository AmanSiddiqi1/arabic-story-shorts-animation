A stranger burdened by debt had neither work nor enough food. He followed a group entering a grand mansion, hoping he might find something to eat.

He slipped inside unnoticed and sat in a corner. Four large dogs wore golden collars and silver chains, with solid gold dishes set before them.

Servants brought food for the dogs, but the man was hungrier than they were. One dog watched him, then stepped aside as if inviting him to eat.

He hesitated, then ate until satisfied. The dog did not stop him; it pushed its golden dish toward him with a paw, as though offering that too.

He took the dish, sold it for a great price, paid debts, and invested in trade. Over the years his business prospered, and he became wealthy.

He never forgot the dog or its owner. Years later he returned with repayment and gifts, only to find the mansion ruined and its former splendor gone.

He found the former owner living in poverty. The visitor explained how the dog’s dish had saved him from hunger and debt, then offered the money with thanks.

The impoverished owner refused: His dog had given the dish, and he would not charge for another’s gift. The visitor left amazed by dignity that poverty could not diminish.
