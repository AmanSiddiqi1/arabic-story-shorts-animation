# AN-104 The Man Who Stole the Dish of Gold Wherein the Dog Ate: creative plan

Film length 168.59 s of narration + 3.5 s THE END hold (to 172.09 s). 16 scenes, 153 actions. All times are seconds on the final MP3; every cue word and time is in `creative_plan.json` (the machine-readable storyboard, which is authoritative for timings).

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Spine.** A man with nothing is fed by a dog that has everything. The dog then gives him its gold dish. The dish makes his fortune. Years later he comes to repay it and finds the rich master ruined, and the ruined master will not take a coin: "my dog gave it to you, and I do not take the price of a gift someone else gave."

**Conflict.** Not man against man. First hunger against shame (may I eat a dog's food?), then gratitude against dignity (the visitor needs to repay, the owner needs to stay the kind of man who does not sell gifts).

**Beats (16, see JSON `beats`).** Setup 2.16 (debts) · inciting 15.44 (follows the guests) · reveal 30.08 (four dogs richer than he is) · emotion 47.88 (hungrier than the dogs) · suspense 52.04 (the long look) · turn 54.62 (the dog steps back) · 66.70 (he eats) · surprise 72.34 (the paw pushes the dish) · 88.78 (sold) · 99.84 (trade prospers) · 112.66 (returns) · reveal 122.84 (ruin) · emotion 136.60 (it is the owner himself) · climax 147.96 (refusal) · surprise 157.26 ("my dog") · moral 165.23 (dignity).

**Surprises.** (1) The dogs wear gold while a man starves. (2) The dog, not a person, is the generous one, twice. (3) The mansion is a ruin. (4) The poor man turns down money he badly needs.

**Emotional arc.** Weighed down and ashamed (S1 to S3) → wonder and envy (S4) → hunger, then the tender stillness of the look (S5) → relief and disbelief (S6) → rising energy and comedy of success (S7 to S9) → gratitude (S10, S11) → shock and sadness (S12) → recognition (S13, S14) → the quiet, upright refusal (S15) → awe (S16).

**Ending and moral.** The old man goes back to mending his sandal with a straight back, the old dog against his knee, daylight on both. Dignity is not a function of money.

**Three visual threads to keep consistent.**
- *The debt scrolls*: 3 on his back from 2.16; 2 struck at 91.42 and 92.18 ("some of his debts"); the last struck near 100.5.
- *The paw*: paw on the gold dish (74.56) = paw on the visitor's knee (157.26). Same pose, same dog.
- *The straight back*: the stranger is bent in S1 and upright when rich; the owner is bent in the hut until he speaks (152.12), then sits like a king on his mat.

## 3. Character acting

**Stranger / merchant.** Poor: `tip` 0.08 growing to 0.13 under the scrolls, gaze on the ground, `lid` 0.35, short steps (`walk` amp 0.7). Hunger is acted with the eyes: gaze stays on food while the body moves on; `wet`; hands to belly. In the court he is all saccades (`gazePlan` ease 0.07) and freezes. Eating: careful pinch, then faster; eyes shut on the second bite. After the dish: upright, amp 1. Rich: `hipsHands`, relaxed `lid` 0.4. In the hut he kneels (`kneel` 1), offers with `reachBoth`, bows with `tip` up to 0.25; at "my dog" he laughs through wet eyes.

**Dog1.** Never cartoon-frantic. Calm half lids (`lid` 0.25 to 0.3), ears lead every head move, `headTilt` for curiosity, slow `tailWag`. Invitation = back off two steps, sit, two muzzle nods, paw `raise`. The push = stand, step in, weight back, paw forward twice. After giving: lies down, chin on paws. Old: grey muzzle, wakes ear first then one eye, same paw gesture.

**Other dogs.** Background life only: eat at different chew rates, one looks up at 70 s and goes back to eating. They never notice the man; that is what makes dog1 special.

**Lord / poor owner.** Rich: seated, generous wave, never looks at the door. Poor: absorbed in mending, mild; when he refuses there is no anger and no pride on show: open palm (`shield`), small smile, then both hands closing the visitor's fingers over the purse. From 152.12 the stoop eases off and stays off. He never looks at the money.

**Listeners react.** Servant bows with shut eyes; porter boy peeks in the chest and looks up worried at the ruin; old woman sighs before pointing; the owner's eyes follow the balloon frames.

**Anticipation and follow-through.** Scroll landings press him down (`landSquash`); hop over the step (`hop`); sit and kneel with `settleTo`; chain and purse on `swing`; scrolls bob a beat late.

## 4. Camera and composition

- Establishing track on the 2400 strip (S1), pan with the eye line to the palace (S2). The stranger always travels left to right toward the mansion, in S2 and again in S11/S12, so the return rhymes with the first visit; S12 repeats the S2 framing.
- Court: wide for the sneak; eye-line pan for the dog reveal with three eased inserts (collar, chain, dish); from 45.14 a `twoShot` of man and dog1 with the dish between them. The long look gets the only slow creep-in of the first half.
- Dish push: insert that still keeps both faces at the top of the frame.
- S9 locked wide for the time-lapse; S10 push-in under the thought cloud.
- S12: start close on the smile, reveal the ruin by pan and pull-out on وجد.
- Hut: two-shot with the dog low between the two men; push to the owner for his speech, tilt down to the dog on كلبي, back up; final slow pull-out to the whole room.
- Keep faces and the purse exchange in the upper and middle panel; nothing important near the panel's bottom edge. Lettering on wall or sky space only.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour:** debts as a load of scrolls; the servant bowing with shut eyes while the stranger slides past; "GRRR"; the man comparing a gold dish to his empty palms; "me?"; the goldsmith's coin eyes; the boy peeking in the chest; the stallkeeper who refused him now bowing.
- **Suspense:** the near miss at 23.4; the long look (2.3 s of stillness); will the dog bite when he reaches for the dish.
- **Surprise:** two pushes of the dish, the second bumping his knee; the ruin revealed on the word; the old dog alive in the hut and its paw on his knee.
- **Emotion:** hands closing round the rim; stopping mid-bite when rich; hand on the planks; the purse pushed back; the straightened back; the final light.
