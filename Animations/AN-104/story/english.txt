A stranger burdened by debt had neither work nor enough food. Walking through a big city, he saw a group entering a large palace, and followed them, hoping to find something to eat.

He slipped inside unnoticed then sat in a corner of the courtyard. He saw four large dogs wearing gold collars, tied with silver chains, with gold dishes in front of them.

Servants brought food for the dogs, but the man was hungrier than they were. One of the dogs noticed him and looked at him for a long time, then stepped aside as if inviting him to eat.

He hesitated, then ate until satisfied. The dog did not stop him; it pushed its golden dish toward him with a paw, as if saying: take this too, perhaps you need it more than I do.

The man took the dish, left the palace, and sold it for a high price. He paid off some of his debts and bought goods to trade with. Over the years his business prospered, and he was able to live in comfort.

He never forgot the dog or its owner. Years later he returned with money equal to the dish’s value and other gifts, to repay the kindness. but he found the place in ruins, and all its wealth gone.

He asked about the owner of the palace, and was directed to a poor man living in hardship. He recognized him as the owner himself, and told him how the dog’s dish had saved him from hunger and debt, and offered him the money with thanks and apologies.

The poor man refused to take anything, and said: I did not give you the dish, my dog gave it to you, and I will not take payment for a gift someone else gave. The visitor left amazed by dignity that poverty could not diminish.
