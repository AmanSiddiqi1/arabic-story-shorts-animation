A money-changer passed through the market carrying gold coins. A thief boasted to his companions that he could steal the purse by himself, and they challenged him to prove it.

The thief followed him home. The money-changer put the purse on a high shelf, then went to the lavatory, asking his maid to bring water. She left the door open.

The thief slipped inside, took the purse and escaped unseen. He returned proudly to his companions, expecting them to praise his daring and speed.

Instead, his companions warned that the money-changer would accuse and beat the innocent maid. They said his trick was incomplete unless he saved her from punishment.

He returned and found the maid being punished. Knocking, he pretended to be a messenger from a neighboring shopkeeper who had found and safeguarded the missing purse.

The thief showed the familiar purse. When the money-changer reached for it, he demanded a signed, sealed receipt to prove to his supposed employer that he had delivered it.

The money-changer went inside to write the receipt, believing his money restored. As soon as he turned away, the thief ran off with the very same purse.

The money-changer did not recover his gold, but the maid escaped further punishment. The thief kept the purse and saved her from the blame his first trick had caused.
