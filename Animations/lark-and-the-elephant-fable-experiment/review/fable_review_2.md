# The Lark and the Elephant: director's review 2 (v1 of 20.2-46.5 s)

Judged from the two v1 contact sheets, 30 full-resolution stills pulled from the v1 clip, and `seq_tragedy.js`. I did not play the clip in motion, so the weight and timing judgements rest on stills at 0.15-0.5 s spacing plus the code.

## Verdict

**v1 is clearly better than the original, and one more short round is worth doing.** Shots A to C (arrival, "saw / did not care", the step) now tell the story without sound. Shot D (the lark's grief) is much improved up to the mid-air shock, then weakens: the poses from 41.6 to 45.7 are too alike, the tears read as a blue shard, and the elephant's rump fills nearly half the frame.

On Opus's deviations:
- Re-timing to the real word times: accepted, it is better than my estimates.
- Elephant stop at x 165: accepted.
- Tighter camera: right for B and C, too tight for D (see fix 1).
- Skipped nuzzle and the persistent shells, feathers and straw: accepted. The persistent worm needs moving (fix 6).

## Fixes for one more round (in priority order)

1. **Re-stage the grief so the elephant is a strip, not half the frame (40.2-45.8).**
   - Lark lands at x about 300 and hops to x = 350 (not 385). She stays at 350 until 45.8.
   - Camera for 40.5-45.4: centre x about 300, y about 800, zoom no more than 2.8. That shows x 121..479: frogs fully in at left, lark at about two-thirds across, his rear leg only at the right edge.
   - Land the last drifting feather at x about 375 and put the shell she nudges at about 372, so her actions still play toward the ruin.
   - She hops to (385, 878) during 45.8-46.4 to meet the unchanged take-off.

2. **Push the three grief poses apart (43.35-45.8).** Each must differ in silhouette.
   - Collapse, 43.35-43.9: eyes shut by 43.5, squash to 0.85, head drop about 20, crest fully flat, tail drops.
   - Wail, 43.9-44.7: head rotated back about -0.6 rad so the beak points 45 degrees up, beak fully open, body stretched to 1.06, both wings out and back. Hold 0.6 s with a slight tremble.
   - Sobs, 44.8-45.4: head below the shoulder line (drop about 24, head rotation +0.35), wings hanging, 3 px shake in two bursts.

3. **Redraw the tears (43.9-45.8).** Replace the angular blue shard at her chest with round drops: teardrop shape about 6 px radius with a white highlight, emitted from the eye at about 3 per second, falling under gravity with a tiny splash on the grass. No tears before 43.9.

4. **Redraw the raised leg (33.4-34.4).**
   - Build it as a bent leg: thigh from the shoulder forward and down, a knee, shin hanging vertically, the pad under the shin tilted about 20 degrees so the sole shows.
   - Remove the sideways wedge.
   - Hang the pad directly above the nest with a visible gap of about 20 px over the chicks' heads during the hold.
   - Keep the 0.5 s hold and the 34.40 impact; add one frame of vertical stretch on the drop.

5. **Make the impact dust a burst (34.4-35.0).**
   - 8-10 puffs of mixed radius (10-34 px) leaving the foot radially, moving out and up, slowing, gone within 0.6 s.
   - The planted foot stays visible in the middle from 34.5.
   - No even row along the ground. Apply the same shape, smaller, to the walking footfalls.

6. **Fix the ending turn and the worm (39.9-46.4).**
   - Start the resolve at 45.4, not 45.8: eyes open, brow fully down, crest back up, head lifted and tilted **up** toward where his head is (pupil up-right), not level at the leg.
   - Ease the camera pull-out over 45.6-46.4 instead of a 0.3 s snap. It must still equal the existing camera at 46.5.
   - The dropped worm currently lies in red right under her feet for the whole grief and reads as a wound. Let it fall and come to rest at x about 300, clear of her, where she landed.

## What not to touch
Shots A and B, the chick acting, the frogs, the mid-air shock at 39.9, the HMPH and CRUNCH placement, and all word-timed cues.
