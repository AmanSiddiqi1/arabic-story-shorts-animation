One day, an ascetic went to the market and bought a fat goat.|He paid for it, tied a rope around its neck,|and started walking home with it.|He was pleased with his purchase and suspected nothing.
Three thieves saw the goat and wanted to take it without paying.|They met behind a tree and agreed on a strange trick.|One said, “We will not steal the goat by force.|We will make the man abandon it himself.”
The first thief stood by the road.|When the ascetic passed, he asked in surprise, “Why are you leading a dog home?”|The ascetic laughed. “This is a goat, not a dog!|Can you not see properly?” Then he continued on his way.
A little later, the second thief appeared and stared at the goat in astonishment.|“A man like you leading a dog through the street? How strange!” he said.|The ascetic stopped and wondered, “Why does everyone call it a dog?”
Then the third thief came and said confidently,|“My good man, that is certainly a dog. Who sold it to you?”|The ascetic looked at the animal again|and began to feel worried and confused.
The ascetic thought, “I saw a goat at the market,|but three men say it is a dog.|Did the seller deceive me? Or can I no longer see the truth?”|He did not know whom to believe.
At last, he convinced himself that perhaps the seller had cast a spell on his eyes.|He untied the rope, left the goat beside the road,|and returned angrily to the market to look for the seller.
The three thieves emerged from their hiding places, took the goat, and went away.|They laughed because their plan had worked without a struggle.|The ascetic, meanwhile, was looking for the seller,|unaware that his goat had been a goat all along.
