Each year a Bedouin poet praised Ja’far the Barmecide and received a thousand dinars to support his family. One year he returned to find Ja’far executed.

The poet mourned and fell asleep. In a dream, Ja’far directed him to a merchant in Basra and told him to mention a single bean.

He went to Basra and repeated the message. The merchant wept, welcomed him for three days, and gave him fifteen hundred dinars.

The merchant promised another thousand each year. Asked why, he explained that he had once sold cooked beans on Baghdad’s streets in poverty.

One cold, rainy day Ja’far saw the shivering vendor and called him inside. Each guest bought a measure of beans and filled the seller’s container with gold.

Only one bean remained. Ja’far split it, and a woman in his household bought one half for twice the collected gold; Ja’far matched the generosity for the other half.

The seller left with unexpected riches, moved to Basra, and prospered in trade. He told the poet that he could never forget Ja’far’s kindness.
