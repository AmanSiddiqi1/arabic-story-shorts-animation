A Muslim army besieged a fortress near Damascus, and among its soldiers were two brave brothers. One was killed and the other was captured. The commander of the fortress wanted the prisoner to change his faith.

One of the commander's men suggested that his daughter might persuade the prisoner to change his faith. But she found him occupied with prayer and recitation of the Quran.

For seven days she heard his recitation, grew fond of him, and asked about Islam. After he explained his faith, she chose to embrace it.

The prisoner said that marriage required witnesses outside the fortress. The young woman deceived her family, so they allowed the two to visit a nearby village.

That night they fled on horseback. At dawn, exhausted, they heard pursuers approaching as their horse struggled to continue.

The woman encouraged him to pray. The tale says they heard his dead brother’s voice promising angelic help and safe passage toward Medina.

They reached Medina, where the tale says Umar ibn al-Khattab came out to meet them, astonishing others with his foreknowledge.

Umar arranged a wedding feast. The pair married and began a family. Angelic voices and extraordinary travel belong to the story’s legendary frame.
