# The Lark and the Elephant: comparison review (v1 against the original)

Reviewed: `renders/The Lark and the Elephant - v1.mp4` (195 s) against the original. I read the 27 v1 strips outside 20.2-46.5 at 4 fps in order (0-24 s and 42-195 s) and 24 larger stills (51.6, 53.4, 61.6, 74.4, 90.5, 91.4, 92.9, 101.2, 106.6, 114.6, 120.9, 136.9, 141.9, 145.4, 162.5, 165.4, 165.7, 166.9, 170.6, 183.0, 184.4, 190.4, 193.6, 194.5). I did not play the film or listen to it, so motion, weight and sound are judged from stills at 0.25 s spacing. The QC figures (duration, loudness, lag, frame-identical 20.2-46.5) are Opus's; I did not re-measure them. 20.2-46.5 was not reviewed.

Positions are world units of `scene_d.js` (ground `GR` 900, pond x 123..307, pit x 580..880).

## Verdict

**v1 is a different and much better film than the original, and it needs one short correction round before release.** The story now plays in pictures: the appeal is a scene between two faces, the attack is readable crow by crow, the trap makes sense (he walks past the silent pond to the croaking pit), the fall has a teeter, real speed and an impact, and about 45 pieces of lettering have become 9. The film no longer changes style at 20.2 and 46.5.

Four things hold it back, and all four are quick:
1. **The elephant in the pit cannot act.** His trunk is hidden inside the pit wall from 163.6 to the end, the two "climbs" are a detached leg drawing over his ear, and his reactions in the ending are too small to see.
2. **The blind, thirsty elephant has his trunk in the pond** (105.7-108.7 and 114-116) and stands behind it for about 9 s (113.3-122.5). Part of this came from my own route table.
3. **A red tongue hangs under his face right after the eye strikes** (101.0-101.7) and reads as blood.
4. **Birds overlap each other** in three scenes, and two pieces of lettering sit on characters.

## New defects (not in the original)

1. **163.6-195: the trunk is hidden in the pit wall.** At rest x about 715 his head touches the right wall and the ground layer covers the trunk. Every trunk action I asked for in the pit is therefore invisible.
2. **165.0-166.5: the climb drawing.** The `lift` leg (bent leg with sole) is drawn detached over his ear and cheek as an outline (165.4, 165.7). It looks like a rendering fault.
3. **101.0-101.7: red tongue under the face** immediately after the flashes at the eye, with the X eye at 101.5. It reads as blood.
4. **105.7-108.7 and 114.0-116.0: trunk tip on the frogs and in the pond.** The frogs stay fully up and unreacting until 109.7.
5. **114-128: footprints drawn on the pond surface** (brown marks on the water, 114.6 and 120.9).
6. **Overlaps:**
   - lark and blue bird beak to beak, 73.2-83.7;
   - lark and lead crow, 85.5-96.5;
   - lark drifting into the crows, 68.5-71.2;
   - lark flying through the crows, 47.5-48.0 and 168.5;
   - lark in the frogs' reeds, 7.7-8.0, 9.2-9.5, 18.0.
7. **163.7-164.2: CRASH! on the crows. 155.0-157.2: the cloud's tail bubbles on the lark.**
8. **50-56: the lark's crest touches the underside of the crows' branch** (my mark (770, 712) was too high).

## Corrections for the final, in priority order

**Needed before release**

1. **Give the elephant a pose in the pit that can act (163.9-195).**
   - From 163.9 he sits back: pivot at the rear feet, `rot` about −0.2, rest x about 695, so the head comes up and left and the **whole trunk is inside x < 870**. Check with a still at 170.6.
   - Climbs (165.0-165.9 and 165.95-166.6): rotate the whole body to `rot` −0.35 about the rear feet, both ordinary front legs against the right wall, trunk stretched up to stop about 60 units below the rim (tip y about 960), then slide back with a small squash. Do not use the `lift` leg drawing here.
   - Ending: head lifted toward her from 170.2 (`drop` −10) with the trunk half raised; trunk recoil on "احتقرتني" (173.3); near ear `earUp` 0.8 toward each sound at 179.5, 179.75, 180.0 with a head turn of at least 10 units; on "غرورك" (183.92) `drop` to 22, ears fully down, trunk curled under the chin. The difference between 183.0 and 184.6 must be obvious in two stills.
2. **Keep the trunk out of the pond (105.5-122.5).**
   - Frogs duck to their eyes at 105.7, as he turns, not at 109.6.
   - While the trunk tip is left of x 325 it sweeps the air at about y `GR − 50`; ground taps only at x ≥ 325. The pass over the reeds on "والماء" (109.6) stays.
   - After the wipe: x 380 facing **right**, trudging to 420 by 116.0 (head away from the pond). Caw at 117.2 from ahead and above; chirp at 118.4 from behind; `turnFlip` 118.9-119.5; walk left to 404 by 120.5; branch pat 120.6-121.8 as now; exit from 122.5.
   - Draw footprints only for x 320..560.
   - Ears: `earOff`/`earUp` changes of at least 0.6 at 117.2 and 118.4, each held 0.5 s. Bring the cawing crow into frame (camera x about 440 for 116.7-119.0).
3. **Remove the tongue from 98.6 to 103.5.** Show the bellow with the raised trunk, the flared ears and the head thrown back.
4. **Separate the birds.**
