An old tale tells of the eloquent young Adi ibn Zayd and Hind, daughter of King al-Numan. They met at a place of worship with Hind’s attendant Mariyah.

Mariyah pointed Adi out to Hind, praising his appearance. The princess approached, admired his manners and spoke briefly before departing.

Adi sent a friend to learn her identity. Hearing that she was the princess, he spent a sleepless night longing to see her again.

Mariyah also loved Adi. She sought him out, became privately involved with him, and nevertheless agreed to help him approach Hind.

Mariyah arranged for Hind to see Adi from the palace. When Hind’s longing became overwhelming, Mariyah told her father and proposed marriage.

The king hesitated to raise the subject openly. Mariyah arranged a banquet at Adi’s house, where Adi asked for Hind’s hand; the king agreed, and they married three days later.

The couple lived happily at court for three years. But the tale does not end there: the king later grew angry with Adi and had him killed, without explaining why.

Hind mourned deeply, left the palace, and withdrew to a hermitage near al-Hira. She remained there grieving for Adi until her death.
