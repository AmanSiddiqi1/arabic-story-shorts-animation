An ascetic was sitting beside a river when he saw a bird carrying a little mouse in its claws.|The mouse fell in front of him, and he picked her up gently.|Wanting to protect her, he prayed that she might become a girl and live in his home.
The mouse turned into a beautiful little girl, and the ascetic took her to his wife.|They raised her as their own daughter. Many years passed, and she grew into a young woman.|One day, her father asked whom she wanted to marry.
“I want a husband stronger than everyone,” the young woman said.|Her father thought for a moment. “Then ask the sun,” he said.|“It lights the whole world, and we know nothing stronger.”|So they went to the sun.
But the sun said, “The cloud is stronger than I am, because it can block my light.”|They went to the cloud, which said, “The wind is stronger than I am.|It pushes me from one place to another, and I cannot resist it.”
They asked the wind, which said, “The mountain is stronger than I am.|I can move clouds and trees, but the mountain stays in place however hard I blow.”|The young woman was pleased, and she and her father went to the great mountain.
The mountain said, “There is a little creature stronger than I am.|It is the mouse, who digs inside the rocks and makes himself a home in my body.|I cannot stop him.”|The young woman looked at her father in surprise.
They found a mouse near the mountain,|and the ascetic asked, “Will you marry my daughter?”|The mouse replied, “But she is human, and my home is very small.|I can marry a mouse like myself, not a human.”
The young woman smiled and told her father,|“I want to become a mouse again, as I once was, and marry this mouse.”|Her father agreed, and she turned back into a mouse.|She entered the mouse’s home, and her long journey ended beside the mountain.
