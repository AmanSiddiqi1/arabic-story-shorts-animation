The inventor flew the horse far away to the palace of a king neither knew. | He landed in a broad garden | and claimed to be bringing a marvelous gift and a princess who had chosen to travel with him.
The princess calmly told the king, “This man deceived me, | and I wish to return to my family.” | Amazed by the horse, the king ordered his men to wait until they learned the truth.
Fearing the inventor might escape again, the princess asked the guards to secure the horse in a closed courtyard. | She told them she knew the descent lever, | but would not use it while he was nearby.
Meanwhile Firuz followed reports of the strange horse from caravan to caravan. | He found an ornament fallen from the saddle along the route | and then heard of a stranger who had flown into a distant palace.
The prince arrived disguised as a skilled craftsman, carrying a small box of repair tools. | He told the guards that he understood wood and brass controls and could inspect the mysterious horse.
The king allowed him to inspect the horse under guard. | Firuz examined the controls and found them intact. | Then he asked to test the machine in an open courtyard the following morning.
From behind a screen, the princess heard Firuz say, “The moon knows the river’s path.” | It was the secret phrase she had chosen, so she knew he had come to rescue her.
They agreed on a plan with a signal. | In the morning, the king, guards, and inventor gathered to see what the craftsman would do.
