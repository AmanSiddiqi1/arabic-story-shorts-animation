# HORSE-04 "The Stolen Horse" (80s Cel Anime): Fable review, round 1 (v1)

Film reviewed: `Animations/HORSE-04/renders/HORSE-04_v1.mp4` (145.5 s). Sources: all 25 contact strips in `review/v1_pack/strips/` (4 frames per second), 34 full-resolution panel stills and 3 enlarged crops pulled from the MP4 at the moments cited, and a look at the scene sources for three causes named below. Times are seconds on the film.

## Verdict: REVISE

**No BLOCKER.** The story is in order, every narrated paragraph has a picture, subtitles are sharp and complete in both languages, MEANWHILE... and NEXT MORNING... are in place, TO BE CONTINUED is present and the end hold keeps moving.

REVISE because the film's peak and its ending do not read, and two of the owner's points are missed:
- **The princess behind the screen is not visible.** For 16 of the 20 seconds of S14-S16 the lattice shows a few coloured slivers in three cells. A viewer who does not know the plan sees a decorated board (note 1). The close-up breaks her face into about fifteen pieces (note 2).
- **"They agreed by a signal" has no readable signal** (note 3), and **the last frame does not show the hand at the knob or what the inventor sees** (note 4).
- **Both of the inventor's balloons cover the princess's face**, including her head shake, the one beat where she contradicts him (note 5).
- **The riders are washed out in the sky again**, the same fault and the same cause as HORSE-03 (note 6).

Keep as they are: the far palace growing under the horse (4.5-7.7) and the cut to the full garden (8.0); the king's raised palm and the guards arriving (32-35); the fear cloud with the inventor flying off (41.2-42.3); the cart rolling into the court and CLANK! (46.6-48); the lever callout with the DOWN tag (51.4-54.2), which is the best piece of staging in the film; the desert road, route strip and the driver's pointing arm (57.5-64.5); the rosette insert with the dotted fall and the gap in the saddle fringe (65.9-68.6); the old man and the two children (69.6-75); the swap behind the palm trunk and the crown in the open box (78.3-82.2); the knob and lever inserts (100.7-102.0); the dusk plate, which is clearly dusk and clearly not a tint; the memory card; the widest view of the square (137-138.5).

Not checked: sound was not listened to; stepped cel timing and foot sliding cannot be judged from stills.

## Notes (most important first)

### 1. 112.8-120.4 and 124.5-132.4 s (S14-S16 wide): show that she is behind the screen
- **What is wrong.** The bay is about 270 px wide with 6 x 5 openings of about 40 px. The bust at s 0.8 is cut into slivers: at 114.6, 118.6, 126.9 and 129.9 the only signs of her are a beige fleck in one star, a teal V in one cross and a pink patch in one star. No eye, no head shape. The fingers and eyes planned for 113.94, the ear at 114.66 and the wide eyes at 119.88 cannot be found even on an enlarged still. The 25 bright yellow openings around them are louder than she is.
- **Why it matters.** The narration says "from behind a screen the princess heard". The whole peak depends on the viewer seeing listener and speaker in one frame; here there is only a speaker.
- **Fix.**
  1. Behind the lattice draw her as one flat shape first: a head-veil-shoulders silhouette in deep rose (`#8a3a5e` or similar), bust at s 1.15 so the head is about 85 px wide, against the warm glow. A silhouette survives being cut by a grid; a detailed figure does not. Let it rise into view at 113.56 (خلف) over 0.4 s with a small settle.
  2. Give her one real opening. Replace the two centre cells of the eye row with a single arched listening window (about 110 x 60 px, one thin centre bar at most), or a small hinged leaf she pushes open 20 degrees at 113.94 (ستار). In it: both eyes drawn whole, whites and pupils at least 14 px each, lids moving, gaze going down-left to the craftsman at 116.06 (فيروز) and widening at 119.88 (النهر).
  3. Dim the empty openings to a soft amber (about half the present brightness) so the window and the silhouette are the brightest things in the bay.
  4. Name her once: `S.label` tag THE PRINCESS with a short `S.arrow` to the window, 115.44 (الأميرة) to 116.9, placed above the bay roof in the sky.
  5. Fingers: two fingertips hooked over the lower edge of the window from 113.94, lifting off at 119.88.
  Check on a 360 px wide still, as the plan asked (stills 114.0, 118.6, 127.0).

### 2. 120.44-124.5 s (S15): the eye close-up is a puzzle, not a face
- **What is wrong.** The lattice fills the panel at one pitch. From 120.5 to 123.2 her eyes are two white stars with a black dot each, with no lid, lash or iris; they read as two flowers. The anime eye (iris, highlight, lashes, tears) only arrives at 123.5, with one second left. Around the eyes, about fifteen other cells show pieces of circlet, veil, cheek, mouth, collar, sleeve and sash, all in flat bright colours, and every other cell is bright yellow. The eyes are the least contrasted thing in the picture. The memory card lands on the circlet row and touches the eye row. At 120.5-121.5 a dark bar crosses the top of the panel.
- **Why it matters.** This is the one face close-up of the film and its emotional peak: she recognises her own secret phrase.
- **Fix.**
  1. Frame closer and higher: bust at about s 3.0 so the two eye openings are each about 230 px across and sit in the middle third of the panel; nothing below the cheeks is in frame (no collar, sleeves or sash).
  2. Use the 123.6 eye drawing from the first frame (120.44, كانت): iris, two highlights, upper lashes, brow just visible at the top of each opening. Act with the lid: half closed listening at 120.44, snapping wide at 121.82 (العبارة) with a 2-drawing pupil shrink, shine building at 122.54 (السرية), the tear welling at 123.60 (اختارتها) and held, not falling until 126.72 in the wide.
  3. All other openings: dark warm interior (plum to brown), with only a soft skin tone where her face is behind them. No yellow glow in the close-up; the light comes from outside. Sweep one pale moonlight band left to right across the screen at 121.82-122.6.
  4. Memory card: lower left corner, clear of the eye row by a full cell, same timing (121.82-124.4).
  5. Remove the dark bar at the top (the lattice should run off the panel on all four sides).

### 3. 128.5-131.4 s (S16): the signal cannot be seen
- **What is wrong.** At 128.9 and 129.9 the craftsman stands in front of the horse's forelegs with both hands at his own chest; the knob is 90 px above and behind his head and nothing touches it. The ribbon is a pink fleck about 12 px long under one cross at 131.0. The plan picture (130.7-131.3) is the only readable event in these 3 s, and it is on for 0.6 s.
- **Why it matters.** "They agreed on a plan by a signal" is the paragraph. Without a visible signal and answer the picture balloon comes from nowhere.
- **Fix.** Use the callout that works so well in S6 (`ehCallout`, r 150):
  1. 128.50 (اتفقا): he turns to the horse, right arm up, and taps the knob twice (128.6 and 128.95). Callout over the left sky showing hand and knob, with a small ring flash on each tap and the lettering TAP TAP beside it. Hold to 129.6.
  2. 129.74 (بإشارة): the rose ribbon comes out through the listening window, at least 70 px long and 10 px wide, and flicks twice (129.8, 130.15) with a `swing` settle. Second callout beside the bay, or a 12 percent push toward the bay, 129.7-130.7.
  3. 130.78 (على): the plan picture, held to 131.5 (0.75 s minimum), tail to him.
  4. Guard D's half turn (the planned suspense) at 130.2, both of them freezing for two drawings, then 131.22 (خطة) the innocent whistle.
  Keep the gold path on the rill bright through all of it; at the moment it has faded to a pale line by 126.9.

### 4. 138.4-145.5 s (S17 end): the cliffhanger picture is missing
- **What is wrong.** In the last frame (140.7, 143.0) the craftsman stands dead centre in front of the horse's barrel with his hands on his chest straps. The knob is beside his ear and he is not reaching for it. The crimson corner is a red tick about 12 px high on a brown cloak, half behind a strap. The inventor's eyes go wide at 141.2, which is good, but he looks at the craftsman's shoulder and nothing shows what he has seen. Around them about twenty figures stand in one overlapping row with fifteen spears and helmets, covering the horse's tail, legs and head; the king is three quarters hidden behind the inventor and a guard.
- **Why it matters.** The episode ends on two things: the hand a finger's width from the knob, and the inventor recognising the sash. Neither is on screen.
- **Fix.**
  1. Clear a stage: nobody within 150 units either side of the horse except the craftsman. Front row is five figures only (two guards left, inventor with his two guards right, 70 units between bodies). Everyone else goes in a back row at s 0.85, raised 30 px, lighter line, in front of the arcade. King seated under the pavilion, whole upper body clear, chin on fist, leaning in through the hold.
  2. 138.44 (ليروا): the craftsman walks in from the left with `walk` + `footfalls` and stops at the horse's shoulder on the tail side of the knob, body turned to it. 139.58 (سيفعله): the small nod up and the ribbon answer (a short pan up or a callout, since the bay is not in this set). 140.52 (الصانع): right arm rises on `flow`; the hand stops 12 px from the knob and hovers with a 2 px tremble on threes; the knob glints once.
  3. The corner: make the crimson sash corner with the saffron knot about 40 x 50 px on screen, on the side of the hip that faces the inventor, clear of straps. At 141.0 a light band crosses it, then a round callout (r 130) of crimson and saffron appears next to the inventor's head with a short `S.arrow` from his eyes, or simply a dotted eye line from his eyes to the hip. His hand goes to the torn turban tail at 141.4.
  4. Hold: his pupils shrink, the guards beside him glance at each other out of step, pennants and the pavilion fringe keep moving, the hand keeps hovering. TO BE CONTINUED... stays in the sky as now.
  The same enlarged corner should be used in every craftsman shot from 78.9 (the plan's own warning).

### 5. 13.5-18.3 s (S2): the balloons sit on the princess, and her answer is lost
- **What is wrong.** A GIFT FOR YOU, GREAT KING! (13.5-15.3) and SHE CHOSE TO COME! (16.5-18.3) are both placed at the height of the mounted princess. At 13.6 and 17.4 her head is entirely behind the balloon; only her crossed arms show. Her head shake on 17.16 (اختارت) happens behind the second balloon, and the wedge tails cross her body to reach him.
- **Why it matters.** She is "the princess who chose to come". Her silent no, while he says it, is the joke and the conflict of the scene.
- **Fix.** Put both balloons to the left of the horse, over the hedge and wall above the inventor (about panel x 90..420, y 330..420), tail straight down to his turban. Or have her dismount at 15.10 (وأن), so that she stands between him and the king when he says it. Either way her face stays clear, the shake is three clear drawings at 17.16, her eyes go to the king at 17.6, and his mask slips at 18.18 with his eyes flicking to her.

### 6. 0-7.7 s (S1): riders pale in the sky
- **What is wrong.** At 1.5 and 5.0 the inventor's robe is lavender grey, the horse's hindquarters fade into haze and both faces are milky. Cause: `ehSkyFront(..., a: 0.45)` in `h04_sky.js` line 36 is drawn over the riders, the same cause as HORSE-03 note 3.
- **Why it matters.** Owner point: no wash over the characters. It is also the first picture of the film.
- **Fix.** Draw the near cloud layer before the riders, or keep it in front only below and beside them (clip a clear window around the rig, or use a few separate wisps that pass under the hooves). Riders at full colour in every frame. Check stills 1.5, 5.0 and 7.2.

### 7. 20.3-46.6 s (S3-S5): 26 seconds in one clump
- **What is wrong.** From 12.0 to 45.0 the camera holds one framing. Inside it:
  - The peacock's fan covers the king from the waist down from 12.5 to 38.5 (26 s), including his hands in the weighing gesture at 35.14.
  - The king never goes to the horse. On 29.94-31.46 (الملك إلى الحصان بدهشة) he stays at the right edge behind the princess and leans; there is no walk, no rap on the wood, no hop back. His wonder is one raised finger at 31.7.
  - The guards who freeze on one foot (33.1-34.2) are cut by the right edge of the panel; two of the four are half out of frame.
  - 36.2-38.7: the captain stands on top of the inventor (bodies overlap by half) so the inventor's reaction is hidden.
  - 45.0-46.2: seven figures overlap around the horse; lifting it onto the cart cannot be seen.
- **Why it matters.** This is the argument of the film: her truth, the king's curiosity, his order. The king's curiosity is the comic engine and it stays offstage.
- **Fix.**
  1. Peacock: by 20.3 it has strolled to the lawn in the lower left foreground (below the pool), fan folded; it may tilt its head with the king at 30.8. It never stands in front of a speaking character.
  2. 28.56 (نظر): the king's eyes go to the horse, then the head. 29.94 (الملك): he walks past the princess (she steps back half a pace and watches him) to the horse's chest, camera panning right about 120 units with him so the horse's head and the arcade are centred and the inventor is at the left edge. 30.78 (الحصان): two knuckle raps on the chest with a small ring each. 31.46 (بدهشة): hop back with `landSquash`, then the finger creeping to the knob and both riders jumping.
  3. 32.22 (وأمر): he turns to the right, palm up; guards come in from the arcade with 90 units between them, all inside the panel by 33.1.
  4. The captain plants his spear in the gap between inventor and horse with his body behind the spear and to the right of the inventor; the inventor flinches half a pace left. No overlap of the two bodies.
  5. 45.34 (حفظ): two guards only at the horse (one at the head, one at the tail), the others stay back; princess and inventor step aside to the left. The lift is one clear up-and-down of the horse with the cart sliding under.

### 8. 55.1-56.4 and 111.5-112.3 s: the inventor at the grille is not there
- **What is wrong.** At 55.7 the gate is at the left edge and the "turban at the grille" is a 10 px strip of yellow and a sliver of face behind bars; at 56.1 it has already gone. At 111.6 the captain stands in front of the grille, so the shutter closes on something the viewer never saw.
- **Why it matters.** "She would not use it while he was near" needs him near. The shutter is the scene's laugh and the plan's way of keeping the villain in the film.
- **Fix.** 48.26 onward: the grille is in frame whenever she speaks of him. Widen the bar gaps for this drawing or let him grip two bars: both eyes, the turban front and eight fingertips, at least 70 px wide in total, lit from the court side. 55.08 (ما): she points, the camera pans left so the gate is in the left third (not at the edge), he is seen for a full second, then ducks at 55.68 (قريبًا) leaving the turban tail a drawing behind. 111.52 (التالي): the captain stands beside the gate, not in front of it; the inventor's eyes are seen for 0.4 s, the shutter slides across, CLACK, one eye reappears at a knot hole.

### 9. 78.9-94.5 s (S10-S11): the disguised hero disappears into the gate

From 83.5 to 94.5 he stands in front of the brown gate in a brown cloak with a brown box, between two guards whose crossed spears and sleeves overlap him. The rap on the wood (87.76) and the quarter turn of the knocker (88.44) are not visible; the hammer at 93.0 is.

Guards at least 110 units either side of him; spears cross above his head, not through his body, and uncross at 87.18. Guard A stands clear of the palm (left of the gate post, not behind the trunk). 87.76 (الخشب): he steps to the gate leaf, one arm out, two raps with ring marks and the lettering KNOCK KNOCK. 88.44 (والمقابض): his hand on the brass knocker, a visible quarter turn, a glint, both guards leaning in out of step. Put I KNOW WOOD AND BRASS. in the sky above the wall, with a thin tail.

### 10. Balloon faults (24-28, 72-73.7, 104.2-105.5, 117.4-120.2 s)
- 26.5-28.4: the picture balloon's tail runs straight through I WISH TO GO HOME. (it reads "HOM .") and points at the king's turban, not at her. Move the home picture to the left of the text balloon or stack it above with one shared tail to the princess.
- 72.0-73.7: picture balloon and IT FLEW THAT WAY! are both up, the picture's tail passing behind the text. Show the picture 71.0-72.3, then the text alone. The route strip also sits on the sun; drop the sun 80 px or shorten the strip.
- 104.2-105.5: BOTH WORK! and its wedge cover the knob, the horse's neck and one of the two ticks. Balloon into the wall band above the frieze; both ticks land on clear wood beside knob and lever.
- 117.4-120.2: THE MOON KNOWS THE RIVER'S PATH is fine in place, but its wedge covers the horse's head. This is the password, so give it a thin tail to his mouth and hold the text until the cut at 120.44.
- General: the tails are still wide dark wedges about 90 px across at the base (87.5, 104.5, 118.6). Narrow them to 20 to 30 px or use a short pointer.

### 11. 57.5-75.6 and 46.6-57 / 94.8-132 s: the lower quarter of the panel is empty
- **What is wrong.** Below the feet the desert is a flat salmon band with a few 10 px marks (62.0, 72.5), and the court is plain paving (49.5, 99.5, 114.6). The garden's band has grass and flowers and looks finished; these two do not.
- **Why it matters.** Owner point: full scenes with things to look at on the ground.
- **Fix.** Desert: a nearer parallax layer with two or three rocks, dry bushes, a half buried amphora, hoof and camel tracks along the road, moving faster than the road when the camera tracks. Court: two potted orange trees and a low bench in the foreground corners, a carpet under the star, lantern light pools on the paving at dusk, and the rill's reflection. Nothing taller than knee height where figures stand.

## Reusable techniques worth adding to the Library
- **Round callout inset** (`ehCallout`, 51.4-54.2): shows a small control or signal without leaving the wide shot. Use it for any hand-on-object beat in a held frame.
- **Swap behind a foreground trunk** (78.3-79.0): one character goes behind, another drawing comes out, the trunk drawn as a front pass.
- **Route strip** with stops filling in (58-75): a clear, small travel counter.
- **Rule for figures behind a screen or grille:** silhouette first, one whole opening for the eyes, dim empty cells, and a name tag once; test at 360 px width.
- **Rule for mounted characters:** before placing a balloon in the wall band, check that no rider's head is in it.
