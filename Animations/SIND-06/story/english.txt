On another voyage, Sinbad boarded a trading ship traveling among distant islands. At first the sea was calm, but then a powerful current drove the ship toward tall rocky mountains.

The captain tried to change course, but the ship struck rocks and broke apart. Sinbad and several sailors reached a narrow beach surrounded by mountains, with no way out.

Sinbad searched for food and water and found a strange river running among the stones into a dark opening beneath the mountain. Sparkling pebbles resembling gems lined its banks.

“Perhaps this river reaches inhabited land,” he thought. He made a small raft from the wreckage and loaded food and bright pebbles onto it. The sailors stayed near shore, hoping for rescue.

Sinbad boarded the raft, and the current carried it into the cave. It was dark with a low roof, so he ducked and gripped the ropes.

Sinbad heard water striking the walls and lost track of time. After long hours he grew exhausted, lay upon the planks, and closed his eyes, hoping to survive.

He opened his eyes to sunlight and men beside the raft. They were farmers who had found him at the river’s end and taken him to their village.

He told the king his story, and the king honored him. Sinbad asked him to send a ship to the mountain; it brought back the sailors who had waited there. Days later, they all returned to Baghdad, thanking the farmers before they all left.
