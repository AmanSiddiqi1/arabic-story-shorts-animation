A fox lived in a cave on a mountain and often suffered from hunger. A crow nested high among the rocks. The fox thought the bird could reach food he could not reach.

The fox went to the crow and greeted him kindly, then said, “We have been neighbors for a long time. I hope we become friends and help each other.” But food, not friendship, was foremost in his mind.

The crow looked at him warily and said, “You hunt birds, and I could become your prey. How can I trust you? Pleasant words cannot prove your intentions.”

The fox did not back down. He said friendship benefits everyone, and promised to be a loyal neighbor. Then he told the story of a flea that a mouse had helped, who repaid the favor by helping her take money from a merchant’s house.

The crow listened but was unconvinced. “Friends may help one another,” he said, “but you betrayed a wolf who lived with you. If you did not keep faith with him, how can I keep myself safe near you?”

Then the crow told of a falcon who used his strength to oppress the birds. When he grew old and weak, he began tricking them to eat their food. The crow said, “I fear your friendship is like that falcon’s trick.”

The crow also told of a sparrow who tried to imitate a mighty eagle and got into great trouble. Then he told the fox, “It is better to know the limits of your strength, and to keep away from my nest. I will not drop my guard because of your promises.”

The fox gave up hope of winning the crow over and walked away, showing sadness and anger. When the crow asked why, the fox replied that the crow was craftier than himself. He returned alone to his cave.
