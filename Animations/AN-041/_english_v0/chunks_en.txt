A hungry fox lived in a cave on a mountain.|A crow nested high among the rocks.|The fox thought the bird could reach food he could not reach.
The fox greeted the crow warmly|and proposed friendship|and mutual help.|But food, not friendship, was foremost in his mind.
The crow answered cautiously:|“You hunt birds, and I could become your prey.|How can I trust you?|Pleasant words cannot prove your intentions.”
The fox insisted|that friendship benefits everyone.|He told of a flea sheltered by a mouse|who later repaid the favor by helping her take a merchant’s coins.
The crow listened but was unconvinced.|“Friends may help one another,” he said,|“but you betrayed a wolf who lived with you.|How could I be safe near you?”
The crow told of a powerful falcon who oppressed other birds,|then when old and weak resorted to trickery to take their food.|“Your offer reminds me of him,” he said.
The crow also recalled a sparrow ruined by trying to imitate an eagle.|He warned the fox to respect his limits|and stay away from the nest.
The fox lost hope and left, looking upset.|When the crow asked why,|the fox replied that the crow was craftier than himself.|He returned alone to his cave.
