# AN-041 The Fox and the Crow: creative plan

Film length 167.81 s (narration 164.81 s + 3.0 s THE END). All times are seconds on the final MP3. The machine-readable storyboard (12 scenes, 157 timed actions, subtitle chunks, assets) is `creative_plan.json`; this file explains the direction behind it.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A hungry fox climbs to a crow's nest with sweet words of friendship, hoping she will fetch him food. The wary crow answers every promise with a reason not to trust him, and he goes home alone, beaten by a better mind.

**Conflict.** Charm against caution. Nothing physical happens between them: the fox can only talk, the crow can only refuse. The picture of that conflict is a **chasm** between his terrace and her nest rock. He leans over it, she stays behind it, and it is in every two-shot.

**Emotional arc.** Fox: hungry, hopeful, performing, caught (three times, each a little worse), deflated, angry, then honest for the first time. Crow: alarmed, cool, firm, finally gracious. The only honest thing the fox says in the film is his last line, and it is the first time she bows to him.

**Ending and moral.** No punishment and no gloating. He walks into the dark cave, two eyes, one more belly growl, THE END. Moral: judge a friend by his record, not his words; know your limits.

## 3. Character acting

**Fox (Library `fox`, s 0.8).** Three faces, kept strictly apart:
- *Performing* (whenever the crow can see his face): `mouth: 'smile'`, `brow` −0.4, `lid` 0.3, tail wag (`tailUp` 0.4..0.9), paw gestures with `raise` and `reach`, polite `sit` 0.4 with `plant: true`.
- *Mask off* (36.70-41.2, the peek at 70.30): head turned from her, `mouth: 'closed'`, `brow` 0.4, `lid` 0.5, gaze away.
- *Caught* (41.3, 52.44, 97.08, 121.76): eyes wide, saccades with `gazePlan(t, plan, 0.07)`, ears −1, stiff grin with raised brows, `sq` 0.95. Each time is a little worse than the last.
His body betrays him before his face does: the belly (3.6, 41.4, 164.7), the tongue (38.84, 52.44), the shadow (47.78), the ear that turns before the head (6.80, 154.84). At the end (143.94 on) everything drops: ears −1, `tailUp` 0, `tip` 0.08, `wet` eyes, slow `walk` amp 0.6; the anger is one stamp only, then it drains (flush fades from 155.94).

**Crow (Library `bird` kind `'crow'`, s 1.2).** Economy. She almost never moves her feet. Head angle (`headRot`), a one-eyed side look, `lid` 0.35-0.55, `crest` for alarm, and one big gesture kept for accusations: the pointing wing (`sag` −0.8) at 46.04, 92.60, 119.14, 138.98. Stillness is her strength (85.76 and 147.76: only the pupils move). A wing stays over the eggs whenever he comes closer. She grows through the film: low on the nest in S3, level in S7, standing at full height in S10 (`sq` 1.08, `crest` 1).

**Anticipation and follow-through.** The peck (57.54): head back 0.2 s, jab, the heart puckers before it bursts, the fox flinches 3 frames late. The wing swipe (87.84): back, swipe, follow-through. The edge (137.28): reach, crumble, startle stretch, `hop` back, `landSquash`. Every fox turn uses `turnFlip`.

**Secondary motion.** Tail wag that slows and stops when she stares (85.76-87.4); nest twigs hop on her landing; pebbles into the chasm at each approach; the heart bobbing (`breathe`), sagging when the vow fails (71.1), shivering when pointed at (119.14).

**Inside the clouds.** Groups never move together: the three small birds react with `stagger(i)`, except once on purpose, when the falcon's trick makes all three look away (113.92). The wolf is slow and trusting (long yawn, wet eyes when he wakes alone).

## 4. Camera and composition

- **One tall world**, 1000 x 2300: cave at the foot (ground y 2100), terrace (y 900), chasm (x 600..690), nest pinnacle (top y 610, nest at 790, 610). The 9:16 picture suits a mountain: S1 cranes up 1060 units from fox to crow (7.0-9.4), and S12 looks back up from the bottom.
- **Standing two-shot** for the dialogue: centre about (520, 700), zoom 1.2. Fox lower left looking up, crow upper right looking down, the gap between them. Her head is always about 200 units above his: he asks, she judges.
- **Push-ins on decisions**: 18.6 (the idea), 36.4 (mask off, zoom 1.7 on the fox alone), 138.2 (the eggs), 120.2 (the two smiles), 157.4 (the admission).
- **Cloud framing**: centre (520, 450), zoom 1.3. Cloud upper left (centre about 390-400, 300; 540-560 x 370-380), teller and listener kept at the edges. Tail to the teller.
- **Tracking** the walk-away (148.6-152.8), the crow sliding to the edge of the panel but never leaving it, then a wider two-shot (zoom 1.1) because they are now far apart.
- **Transitions**: cuts; a wipe from the cave to the summit (21.54); one LATER... card (160.75-161.55) with the cut to the foot of the mountain hidden under it.
- Faces and key action stay in the panel (top two-thirds); lettering never covers a face.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour.** The belly that growls at the worst moment and again as the last sound of the film. The tongue that licks by itself on "your prey". The vow with a peek. The story cloud swept away with one wing. SAME TRICK: the old falcon's smile parked beside the fox's identical smile. The sparrow heaving at a sheep that does not notice.
- **Suspense.** How close will he get? Each approach costs a pebble into the chasm; at 137.28 the edge itself goes. The crow's unblinking looks.
- **Surprise.** She already knows about the wolf (94.52): the cloud turns blue and the past appears. The heart is pecked and bursts (57.54).
- **Emotion.** The second heart shrinking and sinking (142.44); the tear and the one honest sentence; her small bow; the lonely walk into the cave at dusk.
- **Visual motifs that carry meaning:** the gap (her caution), the heart (his words), the shadow (his nature), the cloud colours (yellow = his private thought, paper = a told tale, blue = her memory).

## 8. Warnings and open questions

**Warnings**
- A talking story: two characters stand still for two minutes. The clouds, the heart, the shadow and the gap are what keep something changing every 1-2 s; do not drop them without a replacement.
- S6 tells a whole inset story in 8.5 s. Stage it on one floor line, left to right, one action at a time.
- Almost all sound effects fall in the first 43 s and after the last word, because the narration has no pauses in between.
- `hawk`, `rat` and `insect` are planned by other stories; use whichever drawing exists first.
- Keep rock detail sparse on the crane (1060 units in 2.5 s).
- The day moves from morning to dusk during one conversation: a mood device, not narrated.

**Owner questions (defaults in the JSON `owner_questions`)**
1. Flea tale staged the traditional way (flea bites the sleeping merchant, mouse takes one coin).
2. Wolf betrayal shown as the fox creeping off with the shared bone (not the pit of the source tale).
3. Sparrow tale softened: lamb carried off unharmed, sparrow stuck in wool, no shepherd.
4. Additions beyond the script: growling belly, hunter shadow, tongue, the heart motif.
5. Seventeen pieces of invented English lettering (list in the JSON).
6. The crow as a mother with three eggs; called "she" in the plan only.
