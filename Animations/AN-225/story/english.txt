Khuzaymah bin Bishr was known for helping people generously. When his fortune vanished, those he had helped stopped visiting, and he withdrew with his wife.

Ikrimah al-Fayyaz, governor of Mesopotamia, heard of his plight and secretly brought four thousand dinars to Khuzaymah’s door at night.

Khuzaymah demanded the stranger’s name. He replied, “The One Who Helps the Generous When They Stumble,” then departed. Khuzaymah paid his debts without knowing his benefactor.

Khuzaymah told Caliph Sulayman of the mysterious donor. The caliph praised him and appointed Khuzaymah governor of Mesopotamia in Ikrimah’s place, unaware that his predecessor was the donor.

Examining the previous governor’s accounts, Khuzaymah found a deficit. Unable to repay it, Ikrimah was jailed and shackled for a month.

Ikrimah’s wife sent a discreet messenger revealing that the prisoner was the anonymous benefactor. Shocked, Khuzaymah went to the prison to apologize.

Khuzaymah freed Ikrimah and offered to wear the shackles himself, but Ikrimah refused. He cared for his former prisoner and apologized to his wife.

Sulayman rewarded Ikrimah and gave him authority over several provinces, including Khuzaymah’s position. Ikrimah kept him in office, answering the wrong with forgiveness.
