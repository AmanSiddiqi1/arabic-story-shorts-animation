# ALI-01-ANIME "Open Sesame" (80s Cel Anime): Fable review, round 2

Film reviewed: `Animations/ALI-01-ANIME/renders/ALI-01-ANIME_v2.mp4` (142.93 s). Sources: the 24 contact strips in `review/v2_pack/strips/` (4 frames per second) plus 96 half-resolution stills pulled from the MP4 at the moments cited below. Times are seconds on the film.

## Verdict: REVISE (small and targeted: notes 1 to 4 only)

No BLOCKER. The round-1 BLOCKER is fixed. The story is told in order, every narrated event is on screen, subtitles are sharp and correct, the ending and THE END are present.

v2 is a large step up and now reads as its own style, not as Halftone under a filter. Four things still clearly hurt the story, the style or the acting, each inside one scene: the ending's payoff is not readable (note 1), Cassim and Ali Baba are two different-looking men in close-up and in the mediums (note 2), skin goes lilac in the night room and the cave medium (note 3), and the leader's cloak stands out like a stiff arm for 8 s on a standing horse (note 4). Notes 5 to 11 are polish and optional. After notes 1 to 4 a stills check of those four places is enough; a third full review is not needed.

What was not checked: stepped cel timing (twos and threes) was measured in round 1 and not measured again; stills cannot show it. Sound was not listened to.

## Notes (most important first)

### 1. 131.8-138.0 s (and the freeze to 142.9): the ending's payoff cannot be read
- **What is wrong.** The cart sits against the left panel edge for the whole shot, so everything that should happen behind it has no room.
  - The three glowing words are stacked on top of Cassim: "افتح" over his turban, "يا" on his cheek, "سمسم" across his chest, from 133.2 to about 137.5. The only face in the last scene is covered for 4 s.
  - On the cues the words do not fall. Each one slides sideways and is off the left edge within about 0.3 s (135.95-136.1, 136.75, 137.6-137.75). Nothing drops to the road, nothing bounces, nothing is left behind.
  - The empty sacks are a few pale strips at the very left edge, half out of frame, while the narrator says "وأكياسه الفارغة" (134.01-135.11).
  - In the freeze (138.6 onward) the cart is cut in half by the left edge.
- **Why it matters.** This is the picture that answers "would he remember how to get out?" without words, and the contrast with ONE SMALL SACK is the film's moral. At present the viewer sees a man with lettering on his face. It is also the frame the owner will put beside the Pop-up version.
- **Fix.**
  - Camera 131.8-137.9: put the cart bed at about 58% of the panel width (Cassim's head near 60%). The mule may be cropped by the right edge. This leaves about a third of the width behind the cart.
  - Sacks: the four pale ribbons stream fully in frame off the tail board from 132.5. On "وأكياسه" 134.01 one ribbon flaps high and falls back; add the label EMPTY SACKS (same tag style as ONE SMALL SACK, 28 px or more) above the ribbons from 134.01 to 135.7.
  - Words: from 133.07 they sit stacked on the sack heap behind Cassim's back, lowest word resting on the cart rim, at least 30 px clear of his head and turban at all times.
  - The fall, one per cue (135.87, 136.63, 137.51): the word tips 20 to 30 degrees over the tail board, drops on an arc to the road in about 0.35 s, squashes about 15% on contact with one small bounce, dims to about a third of its glow, then stays fixed in the world so the tracking camera carries it out to the left over about 0.8 s. Cassim never looks back; his grin holds; only the turban tail and whip hand move.
  - Freeze from 137.93: whole cart in frame (cart centre near 25% of the width), rock where it is, and the last dropped word lying dim on the road behind the cart. THE END and "?" stay as they are.

### 2. 95.4-97.3, 113.2-116.9 and 123.7-127.0 s: the close-up heads are different men from the puppets
- **What is wrong.**
  - Cassim is introduced at the window (CASSIM tag, 114.5) with a cream turban with gold lines and a pink jewel, brown skin and a dark chin beard. At 117.0 a man in a saffron turban with a red jewel and a white feather, pale skin and a big moustache walks in. The cream turban returns in the greed close-up (125.5-126.9) and the saffron one at 127.0.
  - Ali Baba's bust (95.5-97.0) has a full dark chin beard and no moustache; the puppet in the next shot has a moustache and a small goatee.
- **Why it matters.** The name tag is attached to a face that the mediums never show again, at the moment a new character enters. On a phone, turban colour and facial hair are how the audience tracks who is who.
- **Fix.** Keep the 80s heads; they are the best drawings in the film. Match them to the puppets' model:
  - Cassim close-ups: turban in the puppet's saffron (shade tone a darker orange, no cream), red jewel, the white feather visible in the window shot; replace the chin beard with the puppet's wide moustache.
  - Ali Baba bust: add the puppet's moustache, reduce the beard to the small goatee. Turban and red band already match.
  - Skin: one skin colour per character per colour model, used by both the close-up head and the puppet (see note 3).

### 3. 98.8-101.2 and 105.6-131.8 s: skin turns lilac
- **What is wrong.** In the night room Ali Baba's and Cassim's faces are flat grey-lilac from edge to edge (106.4, 108.6, 117.6, 127.4-131.5) while the wife beside them is warm cream. In the cave medium Ali Baba's face is pink-lilac (99.2, 100.4), one cut after a warm tan bust. The shade tone is covering the whole face instead of a shade shape.
- **Why it matters.** It is the style's own rule ("skin keeps its own colour in every set") and the style's named pitfall. It also makes the same man change colour between a close-up and the next shot, which adds to note 2.
- **Fix.** For the night and cave models take the lit skin from the `MODEL` table (night: the muted warm tan the window head already uses; cave: warm amber tan). The blue-violet goes only into the hard shade shape, at most a third of the face width, on the side away from the lamp: screen-left for Ali Baba while he sits left of the lamp, screen-right for the wife, and by position for Cassim. In the cave the violet stays a 2 to 4 px rim. Check stills at 99.2, 108.6, 117.6 and 127.4: all faces in one frame must sit in the same family of tone.

### 4. 62.5-70.4 s: the leader's cloak is a rigid bar on a standing horse
- **What is wrong.** From 62.6 to 70.2 a thick dark horizontal shape sticks straight out behind the leader. It is his cloak in the riding pose, but the horse is standing, so it reads as a stiff arm pointing away from the rock, held for 8 s. His calling hand rises only to shoulder height at 64.1. On foot his cloak is crimson (40.9-45.2); mounted it is the same dark purple as his robe. The riders behind him have the same bar.
- **Why it matters.** It is the second title beat and "the rock returned to normal" plays over it. A limb-like shape frozen for 8 s fails the "nothing frozen" check and muddies the gesture of the call.
- **Fix.**
  - Tie the cloak to speed. Standing (62.5-70.4): it hangs behind the saddle as a tapered ribbon, tip swaying on a calm 6-drawing cycle. Galloping (34.5-38.0, 70.5-72.7): it streams, tapered, as now.
  - Colour the leader's cloak crimson in the mounted model too, so he is the same man on foot and in the saddle.
  - Call on "أغلق" 64.06: the arm nearer the rock is thrown above head height with overshoot and settle; the cloak kicks out three drawings late and falls back.
  - 66.2-68.5: arm down by 66.8, gaze on the seam as it heals, one nod; then the turn at 69.6 that is already there. Riders behind: hanging cloaks with their own seeds.

### 5. 70.5-71.7 s: the dust is made of rectangles (polish)
The dust under the galloping horses shows hard-edged boxes (lower left at 70.5-70.8, horizontal slabs at 71.3). It reads as a rendering fault. Build it from soft puffs (ellipses with a soft edge, a 16 px horizontal smear), no rectangular fills.

### 6. 96.4-97.3 s: the rising hands in the bust are two green blocks (polish)
Two rectangular sleeves rise from the shoulders with the hands hanging downward at the bottom of the frame (96.6, 97.0). It reads as a vest or two pillars, not as reaching. Either drop them from the bust (shoulders rise, chin lifts, then the cut to the hands insert at 97.3 carries the reach), or bring the hands in from the bottom edge fingers up, no higher than a quarter of the frame.

### 7. 36.5-37.2 s: the heavy sack insert has no context (polish)
A tan hexagon with a coin on a dark maroon mass. Add what makes it a sack on a horse: the blue saddle cloth with its gold line, the tail whipping at the left edge, a strip of the rider's cloak at the top. Use the same pale canvas as the small sack at 98.8, three times its size, so "heavy sacks", "one small sack" and "empty sacks" are one family of objects.

### 8. 102.7-105.5 s: the ride home is small and ends behind a trunk (polish)
Ali Baba's head is about 80 px and he is a dim shape on the donkey; the small sack cannot be seen. Zoom to about 1.6, show the pale sack hugged to his chest, turban tail streaming, donkey's ears back on "مسرعًا" 103.64. Phase the trunk so the last 0.5 s before the cut to the room (105.0-105.5) is clear; now a trunk covers him at 104.7-105.2.

### 9. 42.8-45.4 and 64.2-65.6 s: word stacks at the frame edge (polish)
First call: the stack sits in the top-right corner and "افتح" is clipped by the tube edge during its pop (43.0-43.2). Move it about 60 px down and left. Second call: "سمسم" touches the leader's head (65.0-65.5); keep 30 px clear.

### 10. 7.8-9.0 and 35.5-36.0 s: two trunk crossings still land on the subject (polish)
The trunk covers the donkey's head and half of Ali Baba from the first frame of the forest shot through "صباح" 8.66; start the shot with the trunk already past. At 35.5-36.0 it crosses the lead riders while the counter runs 18 to 33; shift it into 36.2-36.5, just before the insert.

### 11. Small things (polish)
- 30.7-30.9: the donkey's closed eye in the thicket is a grey rectangle. Draw a lid shape in donkey grey, and hold the open eye to 31.25 ("راقب"); it is on screen for about 0.3 s.
- 56.7-58.7: two tiny pale dots drift across the dusk rock and read as dirt. Make them a readable moth with a faint glow, or remove them.
- 60.0-61.6: dark riders on dark horses in the dusk model merge. A 2 px warm rim on the riders from the sunset side would separate them.

## Against the style profile and the "alive" checklist
- **Four layers:** present. Painted plates, hard-shaded cels with coloured lines, backlit door, lamp, coin and title, restrained CRT with sharp text. The dusk repaint at 56.7 and the door-light match cut into the cave at 89.5 are convincing.
- **Accent colour:** Ali Baba's vermilion sash and turban band hold in every model. Good.
- **Fails:** skin per colour model (note 3); one frozen shape (note 4).
- **Alive:** eye close-ups, the donkey's ears, the first call, the mount, the climb and the two-shot now pass. The wife's hands and the hush (108.6-112.4) read. Remaining frozen or mechanical moments are notes 1 and 4.

## Comparison with the Pop-up version
While pulling stills I opened by mistake six contact sheets of the Pop-up film that another session had left in the shared scratch folder (a file name clash; nothing was overwritten). From that limited look: the Pop-up film keeps one character design in every shot, which is exactly where this film is weak (note 2). This film is ahead on light, time of day, the eye close-ups and the temptation scene. Fixing notes 1 to 3 removes the places where a side-by-side would go against it.

## Reusable techniques worth adding to the Library
- **Doorway silhouette swallow** (figure darkens to a rimmed silhouette near a lit opening, is clipped by the opening and scaled down): now proven at 50.3-54.0 and 59.0-59.7.
- **Mount helper** (hand to saddle, crouch, low arc, seat with horse dip): proven at 60.0-61.6.
- **Cloth by speed:** one helper that hangs a cloak or tail when the carrier stands and streams it when it moves, so a riding pose is never reused on a standing horse (from note 4).
- **Model-sheet rule for close-up heads:** any close-up head takes turban colour, facial hair and skin from the same per-character table as the puppet, with a model-sheet still (puppet and close-up side by side, in each colour model) checked before staging (from notes 2 and 3).
- **Guide rules:** display lettering never sits over a face for longer than its pop; a tracking shot that needs something to be left behind keeps a third of the frame free behind the vehicle (from note 1).
