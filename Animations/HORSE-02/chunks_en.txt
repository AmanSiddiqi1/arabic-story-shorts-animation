Prince Firuz felt the hidden lever and turned it slowly. | At last the wooden horse began to descend. The prince sighed with relief | and saw wide deserts, rivers, and cities whose names he did not know.
He chose the roof of a tall palace in a distant city and landed the horse there at dusk. | Seeing nobody at first, he tied it with a rope and quietly searched for water and a safe route.
The palace princess heard footsteps on the roof and came out with a servant carrying a lamp. | Surprised to see a stranger beside a wooden horse, she cautiously asked, | “Who are you, and how did you get here?”
Firuz bowed respectfully and explained that he was the son of a distant king, | and that the horse had flown him far from home before he learned how to return. | The princess thought he was joking.
He showed her the two controls and made the horse rise a little above the roof, then land gently. | The princess stared in wonder and laughed: | “Then we are looking at the strangest horse in the world!”
The two sat in the palace garden and spoke about their countries. | Firuz told her his family was worried and that he wanted to return to reassure them before visiting her again.
They heard guards approaching. | “If you return, say, ‘The moon knows the river’s path.’ Then I’ll know it’s you,” | she said, describing two towers by the river and a blue dome.
Firuz thanked her and sketched the palace, towers, dome, and river route. | He flew off as the princess wondered whether he would return.
