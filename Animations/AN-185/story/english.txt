A traditional tale follows the devotee Ibrahim ibn al-Khawwas traveling alone through unfamiliar lands until he reached a heavily guarded city.

The guards asked if he was a physician and brought him to the king. They warned that the ruler threatened doctors with death if they failed to heal his daughter.

Despite the danger, Ibrahim entered the princess’s rooms. Before meeting him, he heard her call him by name, astonishing him.

He sat outside a curtain and asked how she knew him. She said she had prayed for a righteous visitor who would understand her distress.

The princess explained that for four years she had held beliefs different from her family’s and understood her suffering as spiritual rather than bodily.

Her elderly attendant heard her say the visitor understood her condition. The king welcomed Ibrahim, who visited her for seven days.

She then asked Ibrahim to escort her to Mecca so she could practice her faith freely. He feared the guards, but the tale says they departed unnoticed.

They reached Mecca, where the princess spent seven years in worship before her death. The supernatural protection is presented as part of the traditional account.
