A dove built a nest high in a palm tree beside a river. She laid eggs and waited until little chicks hatched. She fed them each day and rarely left the nest.

One morning, a fox stood beneath the palm tree and shouted, “Give me one of your chicks, or I will climb up and eat all of you!” The dove was frightened. She thought he could reach her, so she dropped one chick to him.

The fox came back a few days later and threatened her again. The dove did the same thing and was deeply distressed. A heron saw her near the river and asked why she was crying.

The dove told him what had happened. The heron said, “The fox cannot climb this palm tree! When he returns, tell him to climb if he can. You will not hand over your chicks again.”

The fox came the next day and repeated his threat. This time the dove answered firmly, “Climb up yourself if you can! I will not throw anything to you.” The fox was surprised and asked, “Who taught you to say that?”

“The heron who lives by the river,” the dove replied. The fox went to him and pretended to admire his cleverness. “If the wind blows from the right,” he asked, “where do you put your head?” The bird answered.

Then the fox asked, “And what do you do if the wind comes from every direction?” The heron said, “I put my head under my wing.” The fox asked him to demonstrate, and the bird agreed.

As soon as the heron tucked his head beneath his wing, the fox leaped on him and caught him. “You knew how to advise the dove,” he mocked, “but not how to protect yourself!” Then he ate him and went away, leaving the river quiet.
