King Anushirwan became separated from his hunting party|while chasing a deer.|Thirsty, he stopped at a village house|and asked for a drink.
A young woman squeezed one stalk of sugarcane into a bowl,|diluted it with water,|added a little scented powder|and served the stranger.
He enjoyed the drink|but asked about the powder.|She said his thirst was so great|she wanted him to sip slowly instead of swallowing it all at once.
Impressed by her thoughtfulness, he asked how many stalks she had used.|Only one, she replied.|Seeing the village’s low taxes,|he considered raising them.
The king decided he might raise the taxes|on such fertile land.|Later that day he returned along the same road|and asked the young woman for another drink.
This time she took much longer.|She explained that even three stalks|had yielded less juice than the single stalk that morning.
Asked why,|she said prosperity weakens|when a ruler turns against his people.|The king remembered his own intention to increase their taxes.
Anushirwan abandoned the tax increase|and, the tale says, married the clever young woman.|Her words had changed a powerful ruler’s decision.
