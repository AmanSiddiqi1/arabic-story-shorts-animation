# AN-135 King Kisra Anushirwan and the Village Damsel: creative plan

Film 0 to 174.0 s (MP3 170.50 s, last word ends 170.20 s, THE END held 3.5 s). All times are seconds on the final MP3. The full action list with cue words, acting notes and Library calls (149 actions in 16 scenes) is in `creative_plan.json`; this file is the direction behind it.

## 1. Narrative breakdown
**Logline.** A thirsty king is served sugarcane juice by a village girl whose single stalk fills a bowl. When he secretly decides to raise the village tax the cane runs dry, and her plain explanation makes him drop the plan and marry her.

**Conflict.** It is inside the king: gratitude against greed. The girl never argues with him; she does not even know who he is. The land answers for her.

**Spine (cue word, time).**
1. Setup: the hunt, the king loses his men (يطارد 6.74).
2. Inciting: thirst, a village, a door (عطشه 9.34, ماء 17.94).
3. One stalk fills the bowl; water; a pinch of powder (وعصرت 25.74).
4. Irony: she serves "a stranger"; we see the crown in his saddlebag (الملك 41.46).
5. He has to sip, and asks why (بسرعة 53.86). Her answer is care, not ceremony (وخافت 58.34).
6. Surprise: only one stalk (فقط 75.38).
7. Turn: the tax is small (قليلة 84.46); he plans to raise it (زيادة 87.18). One cane leaf droops behind his back.
8. Evening: he returns (مساءً 99.26); she is slow (تأخرت 104.94); three stalks gave less than one (ثلاثة 117.16).
9. Climax: "good shrinks when the ruler means to wrong the people", said to the ruler's face without knowing it (الحاكم 133.66).
10. Shame and understanding (وتذكر 141.72).
11. Resolution: he tears up the plan, the cane springs back (ترك 148.52); crown on, marriage (فتزوجها 156.40).
12. Moral tableau (قرار 164.92), THE END.

**Emotional arc.** Thirst and relief, delight, a quiet slide into greed, unease, a held moment of shame, release, joy. **Surprises:** the bowl filling from one stalk; the half bowl from three; the crown going on. **Moral:** a ruler's intentions reach the land and its people; honest words from the humblest person can turn a king.

## 3. Character acting
- **King.** His face carries the story. Morning: open, grateful (lid 0.3 smiles, small bows with `tip` 0.06-0.08, `PERSON_HANDS.chest`). Greed is small and private: a side glance at the door before unrolling the scroll, `PERSON_HANDS.chin`, lid 0.55, fingers drumming. Unease at 124.4 (slow blink). At الحاكم 133.66 use **suspense by stillness**: `noBlink`, only the pupils go to the saddlebag and back; then the gulp and the single look to camera at 135.5. Shame at 145.1 follows the Library shame pattern: eyes up to the witness, break away, head down. The decision at 148.52 has a real wind-up (`anticipate`) and follow-through.
- **Girl.** Quick, upright, level gaze; never servile and never knowing. She reads people (gaze plan shoes to face at 22.3; the glance at the door before the powder at 32.68 shows it is a choice). Explains with her hands: big arm shapes for the upended bowl (61.46), finger counts with a dip before each (`countedBeats`). Fearless pose `hipsHands` at 155.36; only at the crown (157.0) does she lose her composure: `hop`, `landSquash`, `cheeks`, then a laugh.
- **Horse.** A silent third character and the audience's ally: ears find the gazelle first, pants with the king, looks at the camera at the crown insert (41.5), yawns during the wait, turns an accusing head at 137.98, nudges the saddlebag at 156.4, steals cane at the end.
- **Cane field.** Acts through `lush`, `bend` and `sway`, always staggered stalk by stalk with overshoot on the recovery.
- Everyone has a gaze target and blinks (`LIFE.t = t`); listeners react (nod, wince, head tilt) while the other speaks; pose changes use `settleTo`.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities
- **Dramatic irony (double).** We know he is the king (crown in the saddlebag) and we see his thought cloud; she knows neither. Her line about rulers lands on him by accident.
- **The plan is the picture.** The cane is the meter: bowl full from one stalk, a leaf droops at the thought, thin by evening, bowed at her words, springing back at the tear.
- **Visual rhyme.** Her hands wringing the cane (26.6) return as the balloon crown wringing a little house (134.3).
- **Humour.** SIP... SIP... instead of a gulp; the finger count answered by one limp stalk; the foot-tapping wait with a yawning horse; the horse's accusing stare; the stolen bite of cane under THE END.
- **Emotion.** The girl stroking a bowed leaf while the guilty man stands beside her (146.7); his bow to her with the crown off (165.9).
- Lettering diet: twelve English items in 174 s (listed in owner_questions).
