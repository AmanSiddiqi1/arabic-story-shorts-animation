King Anushirwan the Just went out hunting and strayed from his men while chasing a gazelle. On the way his thirst grew strong, and he saw a small village, and stopped at a nearby house to ask its people for water before going on.

A young woman came out of the house, then went back and squeezed a single sugarcane stalk into a bowl, and mixed the juice with water. She sprinkled a little fragrant powder on top and served the drink to the guest, not knowing he was the king.

Anushirwan drank slowly and liked the taste, but asked the young woman why she had added the powder that kept him from drinking quickly. She answered that she had seen how very thirsty he was and feared he would swallow all the water at once and hurt himself.

The king was pleased by her wisdom and asked how many sugarcane stalks she had used. She said she had used only one stalk. He marveled at how much juice there was, then looked at the taxes levied on the village and found them low.

The king thought of raising the taxes when he got back to his palace, since the land seemed very fertile. He went on with his hunt, then came back the same way in the evening and stopped at the young woman’s house to ask for another drink.

This time the young woman was slow, and he asked her why. She said one stalk had not given enough juice, so she had used three, and even so she had not got as much juice as the first stalk gave that morning.

The king asked her to explain this, and she said that blessings dwindle when a ruler means to wrong the people, and the land suffers from his bad rule. The king understood her words and remembered he had resolved to raise the village’s taxes.

Anushirwan gave up the idea of raising the taxes, and, admiring the young woman’s intelligence and courage, he married her, as the tale tells. And so the words of a young woman from a small village changed the decision of a king who could have added to her people’s burden.
