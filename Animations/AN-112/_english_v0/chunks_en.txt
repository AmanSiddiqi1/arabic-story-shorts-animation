Abu Hassan al-Ziyadi, a scholar in Baghdad,|had fallen into debt.|One day a traveler from Khorasan came to him|before setting out on pilgrimage.
The traveler asked Abu Hassan|to safeguard ten thousand dirhams.|If he returned, the money remained his;|if he died during the pilgrimage, it would become a gift.
Abu Hassan agreed|but used the deposit to pay his debts,|hoping to replace it before the pilgrim returned.|Unexpectedly, the traveler came back the very next morning.
The traveler had learned that his father died and canceled the pilgrimage.|He demanded his money.|Abu Hassan asked him to return tomorrow,|claiming another person held the deposit.
At dawn Abu Hassan rode aimlessly|until men sent by Caliph al-Maamun found him.|Taken to court,|he told the caliph his whole predicament.
The tale says the caliph had repeatedly dreamed|that he must help Abu Hassan.|He gave him ten thousand dirhams for repayment,|ten thousand more for expenses and thirty thousand to rebuild his life.
The traveler arrived,|and Abu Hassan offered the full repayment and explained what had happened.|Moved to tears,|the man forgave the debt and refused the money.
On an appointed day Abu Hassan returned to court,|where al-Maamun named him a judge over part of Medina|with an allowance.|He remained in office for years.
