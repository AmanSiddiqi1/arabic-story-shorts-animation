Abu Hassan al-Ziyadi, a scholar in Baghdad, but he fell into great hardship, until shopkeepers were demanding the debts he owed them. One day a man from Khorasan visited him, who was getting ready to travel for the Hajj pilgrimage.

The man from Khorasan said he was carrying ten thousand dirhams and feared the money would be lost on the journey. He asked Abu Hassan to keep it safe until his return, adding that if he died on the way, the money would become a gift to the master of the house.

Abu Hassan agreed and took the money, but used it to pay his debts and provide for his family, thinking he would find something to replace it before the pilgrim returned. The next morning, unexpectedly, the man from Khorasan came back.

The visitor said his father had died, so he had canceled his journey and wanted his money at once. Flustered, Abu Hassan asked him to come back tomorrow, claiming the purse was with someone else. Then he spent a sleepless, anxious night.

At dawn Abu Hassan rode out on his mule without knowing where to go. He met men searching for him on the orders of Caliph al-Maamun, and they took him to the palace. The caliph asked about his troubles, and he told him everything that had happened in detail.

The tale says al-Maamun had a recurring dream telling him to help Abu Hassan. That was why he had sent for him. He gave him ten thousand dirhams to repay the deposit, ten thousand more for his needs, and thirty thousand to set his affairs right.

Abu Hassan went back, and the man from Khorasan came asking for his money. He offered him the full sum and told him the caliph's story. The man wept and said that had he known Abu Hassan's hardship, he would not have asked for the money, then he forgave him and refused to take the dirhams.

On an official day Abu Hassan returned to the palace, as al-Maamun had ordered. The caliph surprised him by appointing him judge over part of Medina, with a fixed salary. He stayed in office for years, and his story became famous among the people.
