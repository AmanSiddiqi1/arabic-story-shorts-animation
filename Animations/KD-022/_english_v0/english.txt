In a distant city lived a physician known for his knowledge and experience. He had treated many patients, and people trusted him. But over the years his sight weakened until he could no longer see the medicines he used.

One day the king’s son fell gravely ill. They brought the old physician, who asked the young man about his pain and symptoms. After thinking, he recognized the illness and described the needed medicine, but refused to mix it himself because he could not see well.

An ignorant man heard the story and saw a chance to earn money and gain the king’s favor. He went to the palace and said confidently, “I am a physician, and I know how to mix medicines. Let me prepare the prince’s treatment.”

The palace officials believed him and led him into the medicine store. The shelves were full of boxes of herbs and powders. He looked around but did not know the names of most of them or their effects on the body.

He began taking a little from one box and a little from another. Among the things he chose was a poisonous powder that could not be swallowed. He did not recognize the danger, mixed it with the other substances, and brought it in a small bowl.

The king was impressed by the man’s speed and took it as a sign of skill. He ordered his son to drink the medicine. But the prince’s pain worsened after drinking it, and he died soon afterward. Grief filled the palace.

The king was furious and asked the man, “Are you certain that this was the correct treatment?” The ignorant man had no convincing answer. The king then ordered him to drink the very medicine he had prepared for the prince.

The man drank the mixture, was poisoned, and died too. The old physician remained alive, but he could not bring the prince back. Everyone now knew that false confidence had cost two people their lives.
