An old tale says Caliph al-Maamun visited Egypt and marveled at the pyramids. Hearing rumors of hidden treasure, he wanted to discover what lay inside.

Al-Maamun ordered workers to open a pyramid and even tried to take part of it down. Its enormous stones resisted their tools.

Work continued at great cost, but the laborers managed to open only a small tunnel. The expected riches did not readily appear.

Eventually, the story says, treasure was found. Its value matched exactly what the caliph had spent on excavation—no more and no less.

Astonished by the coincidence and his lack of profit, the caliph took what had been discovered and abandoned his attempt to dismantle the pyramids.

The tale describes the pyramids as wonders built from immense, carefully fitted stones, and imagines mysterious passages and chambers within them.

Ancient storytellers imagined jewels, weapons, writings and unseen guardians inside. These are legendary claims, not established facts about the pyramids.

The tale closes by reflecting on monuments that outlast their builders. Kings passed away, pyramids endured, and al-Maamun stopped seeking profit beneath their stones.
