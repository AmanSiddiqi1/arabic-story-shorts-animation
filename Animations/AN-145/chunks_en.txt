An old tale says Caliph al-Maamun visited Egypt|and saw the great pyramids, amazed at the size and height of their stones.|He heard people talk of treasures hidden inside them,|he wanted to discover what lay inside.
Al-Maamun decided to open one of the pyramids,|and even tried to remove a large part of it. He gathered workers and ordered them to dig,|but the huge stones resisted their tools, and the work was slower and harder than anyone first expected.
The work went on for many days, and the caliph spent huge sums on men, tools and moving stones.|Yet the workers managed to open only a small tunnel in the body of the pyramid,|and they did not find what they had expected.
Then, as the tale says, they found money and treasures inside the passage.|Al-Maamun ordered their value counted and compared with what he had paid for the digging,|and to everyone's surprise the value equaled the cost of the work exactly, no more and no less.
The caliph marveled at this strange coincidence and realized he had gained nothing from his costly attempt.|He took what he had found,|then gave up the idea of tearing down the pyramids, ordered the work stopped, and returned to other affairs of state.
The tale goes on to describe the pyramids as wonders of the world,|built from immense, carefully fitted stones,|It speaks of iron and lead used in the building, and of passages and rooms whose secrets no one knows.
Ancient storytellers claim that some rooms hold jewels, weapons and books,|and that hidden guards protect them from time.|These are legendary descriptions found in the story,|not confirmed facts about the real history of the pyramids.
The tale ends by reflecting on the monuments that remained after their owners vanished.|The kings and builders passed away, and the pyramids still bear witness to a distant age,|while al-Maamun stopped seeking profit beneath their stones.
