A tale of Basra describes a severe drought. People prayed repeatedly for rain, including respected scholars, but the sky remained clear.

Two men remained until evening and saw a young Black man in worn clothing. Though enslaved to a trader, he prayed with striking devotion.

He prayed for rain. According to the tale, clouds gathered immediately and heavy rain flooded the roads.

The narrator questioned how he could be so sure of divine love. The young man answered that being guided to worship was itself a sign of grace.

Invited to stay, he explained that he had to return to his owner. The two men followed him and visited the slave dealer next morning.

After viewing many enslaved men, they found the young man in a rear hut. Named Maymun, he was sold for twenty dinars; the dealer complained that he prayed too much.

The buyer told Maymun he wished to serve him rather than be served. Distressed that his private devotion had become public, Maymun prayed for death.

Maymun died while prostrating, the tale says. A stranger supplied burial cloth, and people later visited his grave to pray, remembering his devotion.
