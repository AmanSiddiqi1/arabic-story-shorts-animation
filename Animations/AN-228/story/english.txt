Unable to sleep, Caliph Harun al-Rashid summoned the scholar Al-Asmai at dawn and asked for the finest poetry by women that he had heard.

Al-Asmai recalled a sweltering day in Basra when he rested in a fragrant doorway and overheard three sisters speaking behind an upper window.

One sister proposed that each wager a hundred dinars on a love verse, with the best winning all three hundred. The sisters agreed.

The eldest wished for her lover’s visit in dreams but preferred a waking visit. The second said his image came in a dream and she welcomed it.

The youngest spoke as though her lover truly came to her at night, his breath sweet as perfume. The sisters sent a message asking Al-Asmai to judge, and he answered in verse.

Al-Asmai chose the youngest, prompting cheers inside. As he prepared to leave, a maid called him by name: they had recognized him from his poetry.

The women offered fruit and sweets. The youngest gave Al-Asmai her three-hundred-dinar prize in thanks, and he departed delighted.

Harun asked why he chose her. Al-Asmai said the eldest hoped for a meeting, the second dreamed one, but the youngest described one that had happened. Harun rewarded him with three hundred more dinars.
