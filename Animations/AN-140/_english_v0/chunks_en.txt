Said ibn Salim lived in Harun al-Rashid’s time|and had fallen deep into debt.|Creditors crowded his door daily,|leaving him desperate.
He sought advice from Abdullah ibn Malik,|who said only the powerful Barmakid family|could rescue him|from his debts.
Said hesitated,|afraid the brothers would treat him arrogantly.|Abdullah advised him to endure any unpleasantness|if it meant escaping his creditors.
Said visited al-Fadl and Jaafar, Yahya’s sons,|and explained his plight.|They prayed that God would relieve him,|but handed him no money.
Disappointed, Said returned to Abdullah and complained.|Abdullah asked him to stay awhile in case help arrived,|and Said sat|worrying about his debts.
Soon Said’s servant arrived breathlessly:|loaded mules stood outside their house|with a messenger bearing a letter|from the two brothers.
The letter explained that the caliph|had granted one million dirhams for debts|and three hundred thousand for living expenses.|Each brother also gave a million personally.
Said received|three million three hundred thousand dirhams altogether.|The brothers had worked quietly after his visit|to secure far more help than he imagined.
