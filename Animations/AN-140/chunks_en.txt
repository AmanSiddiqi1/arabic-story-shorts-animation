Said ibn Salim lived in the time of Caliph Harun al-Rashid,|and had fallen deep into debt.|Creditors came to his door every day, demanding their money,|until he was at a loss and did not know where to begin.
He went to a man he knew named Abdullah ibn Malik|and asked him for a way out of his trouble.|Abdullah said no one could help him like the Barmakid family,|and that he should ask for their help.
Said hesitated,|saying he feared its sons would meet him with arrogant words and give him nothing.|Abdullah answered that he must bear whatever he heard,|as long as he wanted to save his home from the creditors gathered around him.
Said visited al-Fadl and Jaafar, Yahya’s sons,|and explained his plight.|They listened, and prayed that God would relieve his distress and make him rich,|but they did not hand him any money while he sat before them.
Said left sad and went back to Abdullah, complaining that the visit had not helped him.|Abdullah invited him to sit for the rest of the day, in case something changed.|The man sat, troubled,|thinking of the debts waiting for him at home.
Before long, Said’s servant came hurrying|and said many mules loaded with money stood at the door of the house,|with a man carrying a letter from al-Fadl and Jaafar.|Said hurried home and opened the letter with a trembling hand.
The brothers wrote that they had told the caliph about his situation,|and he ordered one million dirhams for him to pay off the debt,|then added three hundred thousand for him to spend after paying it.|Each brother also gave a million personally.
The total that reached Said came to|three million three hundred thousand dirhams.|Then he understood that al-Fadl and Jaafar had not stopped at kind words,|but had worked in secret so the help would be greater than he expected, and he could start a new life.
