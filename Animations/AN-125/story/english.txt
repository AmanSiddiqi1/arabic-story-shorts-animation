Harun al-Rashid loved his wife Zubaydah and built her a garden with a large pool. Streams supplied it, and dense trees screened anyone bathing there.

On a very hot day Zubaydah admired the pool and shade. She undressed to bathe alone, pouring water over herself from a silver vessel.

Hearing she was at the pool, Harun slipped behind the foliage to watch her unseen, even though the garden was designed to give its bather privacy.

Zubaydah noticed him and turned, embarrassed at being naked. She tried to cover herself with her hands but could not conceal everything. Harun then withdrew.

The scene lingered in Harun’s mind. He began a line about a loving glance renewing his longing, but could not find words to finish the poem.

He summoned Abu Nuwas and asked him to compose a poem beginning with that line. The poet, known for improvisation, answered almost immediately.

Abu Nuwas described the shaded pool and water from the silver vessel, then added frank verses about Zubaydah’s body and Harun’s desire, completing the thought.

Harun smiled at the poem. Pleased that a single line had inspired the poet, he gave him a generous gift, and Abu Nuwas left delighted.
