Ma‘an bin Zaidah went hunting and became separated from his men. He met a Bedouin on a donkey carrying fresh cucumbers and asked where he was going.

The Bedouin said his land had suffered two years of drought before an early cucumber harvest. He was taking some to Ma‘an, whose generosity he had heard about.

Without revealing himself, Ma‘an asked what reward he expected. “A thousand dinars,” said the Bedouin. “And if that is too much?” “Five hundred.”

Ma‘an kept asking, and the Bedouin reduced the price through three hundred, two hundred, one hundred, fifty and thirty. Below that, he threatened to bring his donkey into the prince’s house.

Ma‘an laughed, rode back to his palace and told the guard to admit a man bringing cucumbers on a donkey. The Bedouin arrived and saw the prince on his throne.

The Bedouin did not recognize him at first. He offered the cucumbers and asked for a thousand dinars. Ma‘an bargained him down again to thirty.

Suddenly recognizing the hunter, the Bedouin joked that his donkey was waiting outside, ready to enter the palace. Ma‘an laughed heartily.

Ma‘an ordered payment of every amount named, not merely thirty. The Bedouin received 2,180 dinars in total and returned home amazed by his fortune.
