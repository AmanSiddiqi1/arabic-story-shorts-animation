Ma’an bin Zaidah went out hunting in the desert, and strayed from his men while chasing a gazelle. On the way he saw a Bedouin riding a donkey and carrying fresh cucumbers. He greeted him and asked where he was going.

The Bedouin said his land had suffered two years of drought then a good season came and the cucumbers ripened early. He wanted to give them as a gift to Prince Ma’an bin Zaidah, because he had heard much about his generosity to strangers.

Ma’an asked him, without revealing who he was: “How much do you expect the prince to give you?” “A thousand dinars,” said the Bedouin. Ma’an said: “And if he thinks that is too much?” He said: “Five hundred.”

Ma’an kept questioning him, and the Bedouin came down to three hundred, then two hundred, then one hundred, then fifty, and finally thirty dinars. After that he said he would go no lower, or else he would bring his donkey into the prince’s house and go home empty-handed.

Ma’an laughed and rode ahead of the Bedouin to his palace, and told the guard to admit a man bringing cucumbers on a donkey. A little later the Bedouin arrived and saw the prince seated on his throne, with servants and men before him.

At first the Bedouin did not recognize him, and he offered him the cucumbers. The prince asked the price, and he asked for a thousand dinars. Ma’an kept refusing every amount, until the Bedouin again reached thirty dinars and refused to go lower.

Suddenly the Bedouin realized the prince was the man he had met in the desert. He said, laughing: “If you don’t give me the thirty, my donkey is tied at the door, and it will come into your house as I promised!” Ma’an laughed so hard he leaned back.

Ma’an called his treasurer and ordered him to give the Bedouin every amount he had named, not just the last one. The man collected one thousand, five hundred, three hundred, two hundred, one hundred, fifty and thirty dinars, and went home amazed, with a great fortune.
