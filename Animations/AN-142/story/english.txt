An old story tells of a devout Israelite woman who visited a place of prayer daily, stopping at a nearby garden to perform ablutions.

The garden had two elderly keepers who made an improper demand. When she refused, they threatened to ruin her reputation with false testimony.

She would not yield. The keepers opened the gate, cried for help, and falsely claimed to have caught her with a young man who escaped.

The crowd accepted their accusation without checking it. For three days they publicly shamed her, then led her away for punishment as she prayed for vindication.

A twelve-year-old boy named Daniel followed the procession. He insisted that no punishment occur before the witnesses were questioned separately.

Daniel asked the first elder where the alleged incident happened. The man said it was on the east side, beneath a pear tree.

The second elder said it happened on the west side under an apple tree. Their contradictory stories exposed the accusation as false.

In the legend, heavenly fire then strikes the false accusers and the woman is saved. Daniel’s crucial act was to test their claims.
