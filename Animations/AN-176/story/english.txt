In old stories King Anushirwan was famed for justice. One day he pretended to be ill and announced that his physicians had prescribed an unusual remedy.

He said he needed a discarded mud brick from an abandoned village. His officials were surprised but set out across the provinces to find one.

The messengers searched for ruined houses and deserted settlements, questioning people across the land, but could find no abandoned village or discarded brick.

They returned to report failure, expecting disappointment. Instead, the king rejoiced and thanked God for their news.

Anushirwan revealed that he had not needed any medicine. The illness was a test to discover whether any settlements had fallen into ruin.

Had they found an abandoned village, he said, he would have tried to restore it and bring people back. Their report suggested the realm was thriving.

The tale connects royal strength with soldiers, revenue, a thriving population, and ultimately just treatment of the people.

The story concludes that justice sustains towns while oppression empties them. It is a moral parable, not historical proof that every village was intact.
