# KD-019 The Seagull and the Crab: creative plan

Film length 174.44 s of narration + 3.0 s end hold = 177.44 s. 17 scenes, 181 timed actions. All times are seconds on the final MP3. The machine-readable storyboard is `creative_plan.json`; this file explains the direction.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** An old seabird, too weak to hunt, tricks the fish of his pond into letting him carry them, two a day, to a safe pond that does not exist. When the crab takes his turn and sees the bones, he seizes the bird by the neck.

**Conflict.** Trust against appetite. The bird's weapon is a sad face; the crab's is a pair of pincers and the wit to use them in time.

## 3. Character acting

**The bird** plays two faces. *Mask on* (in front of anyone): neck 0.2 to 0.45, lid 0.55 to 0.7, brow -0.8, wet eyes, slow blinks, sighs, steps of two at most. *Mask off* (alone on the hill 83.04 to 86.0, and looking at the crab 96.95 and 129.44 on): brow +1, lid 0.4, neck up, quick precise moves. The change is always a visible act, for example at 86.00 he wipes his beak and lowers his own shoulders. Anticipation on every strike (neck coils back 0.25 s); the young strike takes 2 frames, the old one 8. Gulps are a bulge sliding down the neck with head thrown back. After 140.56: eyes wide, look to camera once (139.46), then uneven wingbeats, decaying flap, `limp` and `eyes:'x'`. Secondary motion: tail feathers settle last, loose feathers drift on `swing`.

**The crab** acts with his stalks. Height shows mood (1.3 curious or shocked, 0.6 afraid, 0.7 grieving, 1.0 resolved); they move one after the other, never together, and trail 2 frames in wind. Pincers are his hands: open palm to ask, cheeks in horror, net mime, wings mime, visor to search. His gait is a sideways scuttle from `walk` and `footfalls`, fast and low when alarmed (amp 1.4), slow when tired (amp 0.6). The key transition is 128.54 to 133.9: head drop, wet eyes, one tear, a 0.3 s blink, a breath, stalks level, brow to a hard V, pincers opening slowly. No smile at the kill or after it; he pants, lowers his pincers, and looks back once.

**The fish** are an ensemble that never moves in unison (`stagger`, per-fish delay 2 to 4 frames). Two are individuals from 69.36: the smallest yellow (bouncy, first to volunteer) and the round pink (faints at 49.92). Their shapes return as skeletons at 118.98 and as icons at 126.38 and 163.40. Under water, tears are bubbles that rise.

**Listeners** always have a gaze target: fish heads follow the bird's wing at 58.40 in a wave; the crab looks at whoever speaks; the bird checks his audience after each line of the lie (39.26).

## 4. Camera and composition

- Named framings on the pond (world units): WIDE `{x:500,y:615,zoom:1.0}`; bank two-shot `{x:300,y:450,zoom:1.9}`; GATHER `{x:400,y:620,zoom:1.5}`; UNDER `{x:660,y:845,zoom:1.6}`.
- The bird is always high and left, the fish low and right: he looms over them. The spread wings at 64.64 put the fish in his shadow.
- Thought clouds sit at the upper right (x 400..960, y 60..400) and never touch a face.
- Push-ins on decisions: the bird's glint (96.95), the crab's eyes (115.24), the rising pincers (136.42 to 139.4).
- Reveals: the cut to the hill at 77.22 changes the whole palette; the bones are shown from the crab's eye line with a pan that counts them.
- The grip is staged against plain sky, neck horizontal between two vertical pincers, so the silhouette reads on a phone.
- Shake is panel-only (140.56, 151.98). Counters and cards are in panel space at the top corners. The caption panel holds only subtitles.

## 8. Warnings and open questions

- The film depends on three new figures; the bird's neck is the riskiest drawing.
- No sound effect is possible in the climax (133.94 to 154.42 has no pause), so it must read silently.
- `fish`, `pond`, `thoughtCloud` and others are planned but not yet in `Library/`; build to the planned specification or reuse if another story got there first.
- Eight questions for the owner are in `owner_questions` (bird design, softened eating and death, the invented numbers and counters, fish carried in the beak, the early mask slip, the lettering list, the guard ending).
