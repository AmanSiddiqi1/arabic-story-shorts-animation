A large bird lived beside a pond full of fish. For many years he caught fish easily, but he grew old and weak. One day he sat sadly by the water because he could no longer hunt as before.

A crab living in the pond passed and asked, “Why do you look so sad?” The bird said, “I heard two fishermen say they will return soon and take every fish from here. I fear the pond will be empty.”

The crab hurried to the fish and told them what he had heard. The fish were frightened and gathered around the bird for advice. He said, “I know another pond far away, with plenty of water and no fishermen. I can carry you there.”

The fish agreed, so every day the bird carried two fish and flew far away with them. But there was no new pond. He landed on a rocky hill, ate both fish, then returned to take more.

After several days the crab said, “I too want to go to the safe pond.” The bird agreed and carried him in his claws. As they flew, the crab searched the ground for the water the bird had promised.

They approached the rocky hill, and the crab saw many fish bones scattered across the stones. He understood at once: the bird had not rescued his friends but eaten them. He knew he was next.

Before the bird could land, the crab raised his strong pincers and gripped the bird’s neck. The bird tried to escape, but the crab squeezed with all his strength until the bird died. They both fell near the pond.

The crab returned to the water and told the surviving fish what had happened. They learned that their friends had never reached a safe pond. And the bird no longer appeared each morning to carry away two more.
