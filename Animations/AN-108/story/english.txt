Ala al-Din, chief of police in Kus, was sitting at home one evening when a well-dressed, distinguished-looking man came to him. Behind him stood a servant carrying a heavy chest, and the visitor asked to meet the chief on a secret matter.

The police chief welcomed the man, seated him at his side and asked what he needed. The guest said he used to rob on the highways and steal people's money, but he wanted to leave that life, repent and start an honest living.

Pointing to the chest, and said it held ornaments, jewels and precious metals worth forty thousand dinars. He opened the lid, and gleaming things appeared, large rings and sparkling stones, and the police chief marveled at how many there were.

The visitor said he did not want to keep these things he had gained in his past life. He offered to hand the whole chest to the chief for a thousand dinars of lawful money, with which to start a new trade that would help him stay honest.

The police chief was impressed by what he took for sincere repentance, and was also pleased by what looked like a big profit. He called his treasurer and ordered him to bring a thousand dinars, paid it to the man and received the chest from him at once.

The man thanked the police chief and promised to remember his kindness, and left with his servant under cover of night. The chest stayed with the chief, who had not examined its contents closely, preferring to wait until morning to show it to experts.

The next day he summoned the city's leading goldsmith and asked him to value the jewels. The goldsmith examined the items one by one, then told him they were neither gold nor silver, but copper, tin and stones of glass.

The police chief grew furious and sent his men to search for the visitor, but they found no trace of him. Then he realized the swindler had not sold a chest of treasure, but had cheaply bought the trust of a man who thought himself an expert on thieves.
