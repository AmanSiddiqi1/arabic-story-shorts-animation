Restless one night, Harun al-Rashid wandered his palace and found a sleeping young woman. He uncovered her face and kissed her without asking; she woke startled.

The young woman played the lute and said the caliph's son had bought her for him, but Zubaydah had kept her away. She asked him to return the following night.

In the morning, the caliph sent for Abu Nuwas. The chamberlain found him in a tavern, in debt because of his infatuation with a young man.

Abu Nuwas was improvising poems about the young man's colorful clothing. The caliph paid his debt and summoned him to the palace.

Harun asked for lines containing the woman’s surprised question but gave few details. Abu Nuwas improvised a scene strikingly like what had happened.

The caliph exclaimed that Abu Nuwas spoke as though he had witnessed the encounter. They visited the woman, and poetry and music continued.

After drinking, the poet fell asleep holding a cup. The woman hid it, and Harun woke him with a drawn sword, demanding a poetic answer.

Abu Nuwas cleverly identified the woman as the one who had hidden the cup without embarrassing her. Harun rewarded him with a robe and money.
