# AN-103 Harun al-Rashid and the Damsel and Abu Nowas: creative plan

Film length 168.25 s of narration plus a 3.0 s end hold (171.25 s). 17 scenes, 178 timed actions. All times are seconds on the final MP3; every cue word and time comes from `planning/inputs/timeline.md`. The machine-readable storyboard is `creative_plan.json` (same folder); this file explains the direction.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A sleepless caliph has a secret night encounter, then tests his poet twice: first to put the secret into verse without being told it, then, at sword point, to say where a hidden cup has gone. Both times the poet sees through him, and the second time he also protects the woman's dignity.

**Conflict.** A powerful man who enjoys setting traps against a poet who has only his wits. The danger is real enough to frighten (the sword) but the audience is let in on the joke.

**Surprises.** The waking (23.8); the poem cloud replaying the night step by step (81.2 to 89.8); the sword (131.1); the poet's tact (151.7).

**Emotional arc.** Restless and guilty (Harun) → curious → smug → caught out → delighted. The poet: carefree → alert → terrified → composed → relieved and rich. The damsel: startled → frank → playful accomplice → relieved.

**Ending and moral.** Quick wit plus tact turns danger into reward: say what is true without shaming anyone. The poet leaves down the same moonlit corridor the film opened on.

## 3. Character acting

- **Harun** is a man who thinks he is cleverer than he is. Night: worried brows, saccades, a sigh (sq dip). In the chamber: hush finger, sneak steps, pleased half-lids after the peck, then a full backward `hop` + `landSquash` with the turban lagging. Throne scenes: smug folded arms and half-lids that are punctured in steps as the poem lands (eyebrow twitch 81.9, eyes wider 83.3, gulp and look to camera 84.2, mouth open 88.0, crown hop 89.8). Sword scene: a stern **mask** (brow 1) that drops to a grin only while the poet cannot see (137.56) and returns in 3 frames. Final laugh uses the laugh helper (mouth open, lid 0.9, tip back, shoulder shake).
- **The damsel**: asleep with `lie:1, lid:1`, breathing, hair lifting on the exhale. The waking is a 2-frame lid snap and a sit-up with `settleTo` overshoot, hands to cheeks, mouth o: store these pose keys, they are reused in the poem cloud. Speaking: earnest raised brows, eyes on Harun, sad half-lids at 35.36. Later a playful accomplice: bitten lip on the tiptoe, a freeze where only pupils move, innocent ceiling gaze, a giggle behind `mouthCover`, a light `flush` when she is spared.
- **Abu Nuwas**: loose and swaying in the tavern (lid 0.4, cup high), sharp the moment he is before the caliph. His thinking is always shown with eyes: `gazePlan` saccades with holds (75.04, 139.90), eyes shut and one finger rising (77.48), never a light bulb. Fear at 131.68: wide eyes, `noBlink`, sq 0.85, `tremble`, shield hands; recovery is one breath (sq 1.05 to 1) and the tremble stopping. Tact at 151.74: finger to lips and a wink. Ending: skip (`anticipate`, `hop`, `landSquash`), hand to his own neck.
- **Chamberlain**: stiff, fussy, long-suffering: nose wrinkle in the tavern, forehead slap at the verse (56.08), a look to camera before paying (58.9).
- **Listeners always act**: whoever is not speaking has a gaze target (`lookAt`), blinks (`LIFE.t`), and a small reaction on each beat; groups never move in unison (`stagger`).
- Secondary motion: cape and robe hems with `swing`, turban and crown lag 2 frames, candle flames lean from gusts, the abandoned cup wobbles.

## 4. Camera and composition

- World 1000 x 1230, floor at y 900; keep heads between y 560 and 760 and all lettering above them. The caption panel is never used for action.
- S1 and S17 are the same tracking shot on the corridor strip (bookend). S2 opens wide and pushes to the divan; S3 is a tight `twoShot` that snaps wider for the waking.
- Dialogue scenes are two-shots (S4, S8, S10) with room above for one picture balloon at a time.
- S9: pull out and tilt up so the poem cloud (640 x 430, centred 500, 340) fills the top half with the poet low left and Harun low right; small push on Harun at the payoff.
- S13: push in on hand, cup and sleeve for the theft. S14: low two-shot under the sword, one close-up on the poet (136.88), one wider shot to show the jokers behind his back (137.56).
- Panel-only shake twice (24.84, 131.68), 3 frames each. One LATER card (40.7); other changes are cut, whip (45.6), wipe (64.5, 99.6) and a dissolve with the window sky moving to night (117.88).

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour**: the candle count; the crossed-out "may I?" balloon; the debt scroll that rolls right up to the poet's knee; the chamberlain's forehead slap; Harun's balloon hidden by a SECRET bar; the crown hop; the dotted "were you there" poet crossed out; the hand still curled round a missing cup.
- **Suspense**: the tiptoe and hair lift; the three tugs on the cup with a frozen beat when the poet snorts; the sword shadow and the single sweat drop.
- **Surprise**: the waking; the poem cloud rebuilding S2 and S3 piece by piece on the narrated words (dark room, two candles, long hair, the girl, the question).
- **Emotion**: her frank account in the picture balloon; the poet's courteous silence (blank ribbon rolling up) and her relieved laugh; the shared laughter.
- **Setup and payoff**: the waking pose (23.8 = 86.36); the two candles (10.44 = 81.94); the corridor (0.0 = 163.1); the verse ribbon as the poet's "weapon" which finally curls round the sword.

## 8. Warnings and open questions

- **Script:** the English is a condensed translation and omits details the Arabic narrates (candles and hair in P1; the dark room, long hair and "what is this?" in P5; the blue robe and the contest in P6; the frightening joke in P7; the laughter, the thousand dinars and the departure in P8). Both are kept verbatim; the picture follows the Arabic, so some English chunks will not describe the moment on screen.
- **Mature themes** (an uninvited kiss, wine, a purchased woman, the poet's infatuation) are staged lightly and briefly; each has a default in `owner_questions` (10 questions).
- **Lettering load:** about 30 words and labels, above the usual 8 to 12. The court intrigue needs labels, but trim in review if busy.
- **Poem cloud legibility** on a phone is the main risk in S9; keep it large and uncluttered.
- **Sword:** always upright or across, never toward a face; the audience sees the joke at 137.56.
- The cup's hiding place (her sleeve) and the corridor bookend are additions for clarity; the script does not specify them.
