A man worked as a servant for a wealthy person,|then decided to leave his job and travel.|His employer gave him two dinars in wages.|The man wanted to give one dinar in charity and keep the other for food on the road.
He entered the market and saw a hunter selling two weak hoopoes in one cage.|He asked their price, and the hunter said, “Two dinars.”|He tried to buy both for one dinar,|but the hunter refused.
The man considered buying one bird and leaving the other.|Then he feared they might be a pair and he would separate them.|He felt compassion, paid both dinars,|and had none of his wages left.
He did not want to release the birds in the market:|they were hungry and weak, and another hunter might catch them.|He walked to a distant place full of trees|and gently opened the cage.
The two hoopoes flew with difficulty to a fruit tree.|They perched on a branch and thanked the man for rescuing them.|Then one said to the other, “We must reward him.|Beneath this tree is a jar full of gold.”
The man heard them and was astonished. He asked, “How did you know about gold hidden underground,|yet fail to see the hunter’s net that caught you?”|One hoopoe answered, “When destiny arrives,|even obvious things may be hidden from our eyes.”
The man dug beside the tree until he found a clay jar.|He opened it and discovered it was full of gold dinars.|He thanked the two hoopoes and wished them well,|then carried the treasure away.
Later the man told the king what had happened|and offered to place the gold in the royal treasury.|But the king said, “Keep it. It belongs to you.”|The man left the king carrying the gold, remembering the two birds.
