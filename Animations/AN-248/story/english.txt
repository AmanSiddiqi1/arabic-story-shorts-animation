Travelers visited an elderly man in Upper Egypt and wondered why his children were fair-skinned. He explained that their mother was a Frankish woman and began his story.

I brought flax to Acre to sell and fell in love with a Frankish woman who bought from me. I paid an old woman fifty dinars to arrange a meeting, and the woman came to my house at night.

I sat with her beneath the stars but refrained from intimacy out of fear of God. I paid a hundred dinars for a second meeting and refrained again; then the old woman demanded five hundred for a third.

Before the third meeting, the truce ended and he left Acre. In Damascus he traded goods and then enslaved captives for years, still remembering her.

After the Battle of Hattin, Saladin’s treasury owed him ten dinars from a purchase. Offered a captive instead, he recognized the Frankish woman, who had been married to a Frankish knight.

I reminded her of our earlier meetings, and she professed Islam, as the tale relates. I freed her, then we married before a judge, and later she became pregnant.

A Frankish envoy arrived to reclaim prisoners under a treaty, including her. Asked before Saladin whether she wished to return to her country and first husband, she chose to stay.

The envoy brought a chest from her mother. It held her clothes and both purses of money untouched. The old man pointed to their children and said she had cooked their meal.
