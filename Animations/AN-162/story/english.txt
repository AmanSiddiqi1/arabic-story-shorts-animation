Abu Isa, brother of Caliph al-Ma’mun, loved the singer Kurrat al-Ayn, who lived in Ali bin Hisham’s household. The tale says she loved him too.

Abu Isa devised a way to reach her household discreetly. He suggested that al-Ma’mun make surprise visits to court officials to judge their hospitality.

The caliph sailed with his brother and attendants. Their first visit to Hamid al-Tawil disappointed him, so Abu Isa proposed Ali bin Hisham’s house.

Ali welcomed them into a splendid hall, served abundant food, and presented singers in succession. Abu Isa waited anxiously for Kurrat al-Ayn.

At last Kurrat al-Ayn entered, and Abu Isa went pale. He told the caliph he felt unwell, but tears betrayed him when she began to sing.

She sang of longing and questioned whether her admirer’s love was sincere. With permission, Abu Isa answered in verse about concealed feelings revealed by tears.

Kurrat al-Ayn challenged mere declarations of love. Abu Isa wept and replied; Ali bin Hisham then offered to give her to him.

Al-Ma’mun deferred to his brother. He departed by boat, and Abu Isa took Kurrat al-Ayn home joyfully, within the story’s historical setting of enslaved performers.
