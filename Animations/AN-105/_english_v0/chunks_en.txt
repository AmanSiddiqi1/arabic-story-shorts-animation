A soldier arrived in Alexandria|with a purse of a thousand gold pieces.|Overnight someone cut open his saddlebags and stole it,|so he complained to Husam al-Din, the police chief.
The chief detained everyone at the inn|and prepared to beat them|into a confession,|despite lacking proof against any one guest.
Before the beating began,|a stranger stepped forward, confessed,|and placed the purse before the chief.|The detained travelers were relieved.
People praised the confession,|but the man boasted that his cleverest feat|was stealing the purse a second time.|The chief demanded an explanation.
The trickster said he had watched the soldier receive the gold in Cairo|and followed him from town to town,|waiting for an opportunity.
He described cutting the sleeping soldier’s bag.|Pretending to demonstrate the theft,|he reached for the purse|lying openly before them.
Suddenly he grabbed the purse, ran outside,|plunged into a nearby pool,|and escaped through Alexandria’s interconnected lanes.|The officers failed to catch him.
The chief insisted the soldier had briefly recovered his money|and could not blame the inn guests.|The soldier lost his gold, the innocents were spared,|and the thief escaped.
