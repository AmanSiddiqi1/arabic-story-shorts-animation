Hatim al-Tayy was famous for generosity. After his death he was buried on a mountain, with two stone figures nearby. Travelers heard strange nighttime wailing in the valley.

A Himyarite king named Zu al-Kuraa passed through with companions. Told the sound came from Hatim’s grave, he looked toward the mountain and laughed.

Jokingly, the king called to Hatim: “We are your hungry guests tonight. Where is our meal?” He then slept near the grave.

He woke terrified and shouted to save his she-camel. The men found it collapsing. Unable to save it, they slaughtered, roasted and ate the meat.

Asked what frightened him, the king said Hatim had appeared in a dream carrying a sword, regretted having no food for his guest and struck the camel.

In the morning the king rode with a companion. Hours later they met a man leading another she-camel and asking for the king.

The man said, “I am Adi, Hatim’s son. I have brought a camel to replace yours.” Amazed, the king asked how he knew.

Adi said his father had appeared in a dream and told him to replace the guest’s camel. The king accepted it, marveling that Hatim’s generosity seemed to continue after death.
