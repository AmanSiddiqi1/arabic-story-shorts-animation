# AN-036 The Water-Fowl and the Tortoise: creative plan

Film length 174.18 s (MP3 170.68 s + 3.5 s THE END). 19 scenes, 161 timed actions. All times are seconds on the final MP3; every cue names the Arabic word and its start time from `inputs/timeline.md`. The machine-readable storyboard with every action is `creative_plan.json`; this file explains the direction.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A water bird flees his river when birds of prey gather over a dead man, finds a new home and a true friend in a tortoise, goes back because the danger seems gone, and is taken by a falcon. The tortoise is left alone.

**Conflict.** Safety with a friend in a strange place against the pull of the old home. The enemy is patient: it is not in the picture when he checks, which is exactly why he trusts the place.

**Beats (cue word, time).**
1. Setup: happy life among the rocks (والبحث 6.74). The rocks get a HOME label.
2. Inciting: a dead man on the bank (جثة 11.80); he watches from far away (يراقب 16.60).
3. Suspense: birds of prey come down (جارحة 25.78).
4. Turn: he flees (فطار 33.20) and reaches another river (وصل 37.62).
5. Surprise and warmth: a "stone" is a tortoise (سلحفاة 46.84); she asks why he is sad.
6. Emotion: exile is lighter with a true friend (صديقًا 67.80); he feels safe and shuts his eyes (بالأمان 80.58).
7. Turn: he keeps thinking of home (يفكر 86.02). LATER... (وبعد 88.30).
8. Suspense: she will wait, and warns him with a look at the sky (ينتبه 102.70).
9. Reveal: only bones remain (عظام 114.66); he believes the danger is over (انتهى 118.84).
10. They return together (رجعا 124.64); peaceful days (أيامًا 130.72); the fear becomes a memory (ذكرى 147.18).
11. Climax: the falcon (صقر 151.20) dives (فانقض 158.00) before he can escape.
12. Ending: the tortoise alone (وحدها 167.06) in the place they chose together.

**Emotional arc.** Calm, dread, panic, loneliness, comfort, safety, restlessness, false relief, contentment, shock, grief. The warmest picture (S9: the two side by side under the tree) is repeated in S16 and again at the end with his place empty.

**Surprises.** The stone that is a tortoise (45.0, in a pause). The tortoise ducking into her shell at the scary part of his story (58.8). The falcon's dive after two minutes of gentleness.

**Moral.** The script states none. The pictures say: a danger you cannot see has not gone away, and a safe place with a true friend was worth more than the old home. No moral card.

## 3. Character acting

**Duck (hero).** A soft, nervous body: he always has a gaze target and reacts with the whole neck. Vocabulary: `neck` periscope up when checking, `sq` 0.85-0.9 when afraid or sad, `tremble`, `wet` eyes for home-sickness, tail wags when content. Every flight starts with `anticipate` (crouch 0.3-0.6 s) and a stretch; every landing uses `landSquash` (or splash + `impactSquash` on water). His three take-offs must rhyme: 33.2 (panic, fast), 104.46 (confident), 159.94 (the same crouch, too slow). Walking uses `walk` + `footfalls` with a waddle roll of 0.06. Eyes shut only when he feels safe: 5.98, 80.58, the night pass, 142.34.

**Tortoise (the heart of the film).** Slow and deliberate: every move has a 0.2 s anticipation and a settle; she nods with her whole neck. `hide` is her comic and tragic instrument: pop-out reveal (45.0), half-duck at the scary story (58.8), a flinch when he lands (120.68), full retreat at the flash (161.75), and the very slow return (165.12). Worry lives in a negative `brow` while the mouth still smiles (98.44, 122.8). She is the one who looks at the sky (102.7, 147.76, 156.96). Listener rule: when he talks her gaze stays on his face and flicks to the thought cloud and back.

**Vultures.** Three, never in unison (`stagger` 0.25 s), heavy hops with `landSquash`, half-open wings, cold half lids. One deliberate look at the duck (27.8). They are never shown feeding.

**Falcon.** Stillness and precision against the vultures' heaviness: a banked glide, 2-frame saccades (`gazePlan(t, plan, 0.07)`), a dead-still stare after the lock (154.82), a 0.3 s stall, then one straight accelerating line.

**Body.** No acting at all: it is the only thing in S2-S3 that never moves, against swaying reeds.

**Secondary motion.** Tail drops after the hop ashore, falling leaves (42.74, 82.06), reeds swaying on `K.noise`, ripple rings on every water contact, loose feathers at the panic take-off and after the strike, dust on the tortoise's footfalls.

## 4. Camera and composition

- **Geography is fixed:** the old river is screen RIGHT, the new river screen LEFT. Flee right-to-left, return left-to-right, come back right-to-left, walk home left-to-right. The duck stares right whenever he is homesick.
- **Two looks:** old river = blue day sky, grey rocks, reeds. New river = yellow dawn sky, one tree, lily pads. HOME thought clouds are blue inside.
- **Distance shot (S2, 12.9-20.1):** duck far left, the still shape far right, empty sand between; reused for the return (S12) so the place is recognised.
- **Two-shots (S6-S11, S14):** `twoShot(duckHead, tortoiseHead, {margin: 90})`; thought clouds sit above and between the heads, lettering in the sky third.
- **Push-ins on decisions:** 28.6 (fear), 82.94 (the eye opens). **Pull-outs on feeling:** 76.6-82.9 (safe), 167.06 (alone, the widest frame of the film).
- **Reveals:** fast pan along his eye line to the body (11.3-11.8); tilt up with his look to the vultures (24.62); insert on the bones (114.66); a slow drift that lets the bones slide into the corner of the peaceful shot (147.18).
- **Climax:** high sky, close on the falcon, over-shoulder look down, cut to ground level, whip tilt up into the dive and follow it down with keys on dive start (158.0) and impact (161.75). Panel-only shake; the caption panel never moves.
- **Echo composition:** S9, S16 (140.8) and S19 use the same side-by-side framing; at the end her right side is empty.
- Keep faces above y 880 in the world; nothing important in the bottom third.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour (first two thirds only):** the worm gulp; the stone that grows a head behind his back; her half-duck into the shell at "suddenly" and the one-eye peek; the leaf landing on the sleeping duck; the tail-up dabble; the duck running ahead, looking back and returning to walk at tortoise speed.
- **Suspense by stillness:** the frozen foot (10.92), pupils only on the rock (107.86-110.7), the falcon's dead stare after the lock.
- **Dramatic irony:** BIRDS: 0 and ALL CLEAR! are his belief; the audience has seen the dark feather in the bones, later the bones in the corner of the happy shot, then the shadow that only the tortoise notices.
- **Planted warning, paid off:** she looks at the sky when she says be careful (102.7); she is the first to see the falcon (156.96) and cannot be fast enough.
- **Setup and payoff pose:** the escape crouch at 32.4 works; the same crouch at 159.94 does not.
- **Emotion:** the tear she follows with her eyes (49.96); the heart on "friend"; eyes closing on "safe"; the feather she draws close at the end.
- **Restraint:** the body is two soles and a hand; the kill is a white flash; the loss is an empty place.

## 8. Warnings and open questions

- The film depends on four planned but unbuilt Library pieces (duck, hawk kind, tortoise head fields, thoughtCloud). Fallbacks: Library `turtle` on land for the tortoise's dialogue; a white recolour of the Library `bird` for the duck (weak: no swim or dabble).
- The ending is a death with no consoling moral. Staging is restrained (no blood, no contact, no close view), but the owner should confirm the level.
- No pause in the narration at the strike (161.50-162.06 runs straight into 162.06), so there is no sound effect there.
- Lettering count is 16, above the guide's 8-12. The HOME / OLD HOME / NEW HOME labels matter most; the others can be trimmed.
- Ten owner questions are listed in `creative_plan.json` (`owner_questions`): duck as the species, the mild body, no feeding and no skull, the hidden kill, the three foreshadowing additions, the tortoise's silent warning, the stone reveal and the shared shoot, the invented lettering, no moral card, walking back together.
