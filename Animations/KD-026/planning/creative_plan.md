# KD-026 The Thief and the Devil: creative plan

Film length 166.66 s of narration plus a 3.5 s held ending (to 170.16 s). All times are seconds on the final MP3. The machine-readable storyboard, with every action and its cue word, is `creative_plan.json` (15 scenes, 150 actions). This file explains the direction.

## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A thief who wants a cow and a devil who wants its owner sneak into the same hut, argue over who goes first, and wake the victim themselves.

**Conflict.** It is not hunters against prey. The ascetic sleeps through almost the whole film. The conflict is between two partners who want different things and still cannot share one quiet minute.

**Spine (cue word times).**
- Setup 0-11.9: the ascetic leads his cow home at dusk and daydreams of milk every day (7.48).
- Inciting 12.66: a thief sees the cow and follows.
- Reveal 24.02: a devil follows as well, and wants the man, not the cow (28.56, 30.56).
- Comic meeting 34.62: the two find each other behind the same bush.
- False harmony 41-63: different prizes, so they shake hands (56.90) and wait for the lamp to go out.
- Suspense 72-79: he sleeps, they slip in, and each gets within one step of his prize (77.78).
- Turn 82.18: who goes first? Two mirrored arguments (devil 85-103, thief 103-116).
- Rising 116.6-123.6: louder and louder, neither gives way.
- Surprise and climax 128.80 and 134.96: each wakes the victim to denounce the other.
- Resolution 142-154: the ascetic jumps up, the neighbours come, both flee with nothing.
- Emotion and moral 155-166: man and cow together; they were close, their quarrel saved him.

**Emotional arc.** Warm and innocent, then sly, then tiptoe suspense, then whispered comedy that slowly gets loud, one farcical explosion, and a warm quiet close. The devil is a vain comic imp and is never frightening.

**Ending.** THE END at 166.66 over the lamplit room, with two tiny silhouettes still arguing on the far road outside the window.

## 3. Character acting

- **Ascetic.** Soft and slow: rolling walk, eyes that drift upward when he dreams (`gaze [0.2,-0.8]`, `lid 0.4`), turban tail lagging. Asleep he still acts: brow twitches, a nose scratch (97.54), a roll with a flopping arm (112.20). Waking is built in steps: ear, brow, one eye (`wink`), both eyes, a left-right-camera saccade, then one explosive spring (`anticipate`, `sq 1.12`, `landSquash`).
- **Thief.** Quick, twitchy, lower status. Saccades (`gazePlan(t, plan, 0.07)`), a one-sided grin, the `sneak` gait with high knees and `tip 0.12`. He copies and mocks (fingers as horns at 110.44). His stamp that stops above the floor (101.70) shows he is still trying to be quiet.
- **Devil.** Tall, smooth and theatrical: `settleTo` stretches, moustache twirl, half-closed lids, a cape that lags, a tail with its own life (held in the hand while sneaking, curling when pleased, lashing when cross, steam from the ears). His pout over the empty sack (99.36, `wet`) is the one moment he asks for sympathy, and it is a joke.
- **The pair.** Mirror images at three chosen moments only (34.62, 54.46, 58.14); everywhere else offset by 2 to 6 frames. The listener always reacts: arms folding during the other's speech, eyes going to the sleeper at every noise.
- **Cow.** The silent witness and running gag. She always looks at whichever intruder moved last, notices the thief at 14.4 and the bush at 35.7, twitches on cue at 94.26, watches the quarrel like tennis (117.86), hides her eyes behind an ear (122.74), and nods to confirm the accusation (138.54). Secondary motion: bell and tail on `swing`, udder bounce on footfalls, cud chewing in rhythm with the supper.
- **Neighbours.** Three silhouettes that arrive and react one after another (`stagger`), never together.
- Blinks everywhere through `LIFE.t`; no blinks in held shocks; the one deliberate look to camera per act (thief 20.26, devil 32.34, ascetic 142.90).

## 4. Camera and composition

- Road (S1-S3): a tracking establishing shot, then a pan back to the tree for the thief and a pull-out to zoom 0.95 for the chain devil, thief, cow, ascetic, all facing right. Push in on the devil for his plan.
- Yard (S4-S6): wide with the bush in the left foreground and the house small on the right, then `twoShot` on the two heads with the cottage door in the gap between them, so every pointing finger leads to the prey. Pull out for the wait, with the lit window top right.
- Hut (S7-S15): first a fixed, stage-like full shot (zoom 1.22) to teach the geography: door left, mat centre, cow right, window at the back. The double reach at 77.78 stays wide so both gaps are in one frame. The quarrel is a medium two-shot (zoom 1.7 rising to 2.1) that keeps the sleeping head in the bottom of the frame. Whip out to a symmetrical wide shot for the shouting; extreme close-up for the waking; wide again for neighbours and escape; a slow pull-out for the calm.
- Panel space: icon strips at the top (y 70..250), the SLEEP meter above the quarrel, balloons on the outer side of the speaker, nothing over a face, and the window kept clear as the escape route. Faces and key action stay above the caption panel.
- One transition card: THAT NIGHT... at 62.98, with the set change hidden under it.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour.** The echo of the hush pose (20.26 and 32.34); two stalkers sharing hiding places without knowing; hands over each other's mouths; the handshake spark; a cow squeezing through a door; a silent MOO mime that the thief has to hush; the stamp that stops in mid-air; the burglars shouting for the owner to wake up; the innocent whistling when the lamp finds them.
- **Suspense.** The SLEEP meter turns the script's "voices grew louder" into a visible countdown from 10 to 0. Every near-wake (75.64, 94.26, 97.54, 112.20) freezes both intruders and costs a segment.
- **Surprise.** The reveal of the horns behind the thief's own tree (22.78), and the turn at 123.66 when spite beats self-interest.
- **Emotion.** The opening daydream and the closing lick of the turban frame the caper with the man and his cow; the audience should want them left in peace.
- **The moral as a picture.** The double reach is shown once in the action and once again in two insets (160.28) with the gaps marked, then the insets merge into the torn badge: so close, undone by the quarrel.
