The prince Ma‘an bin Zaidah went hunting on a hot day. He and his men grew thirsty and found no water to drink.

Three girls appeared carrying waterskins. Ma‘an asked for a drink, and without asking his identity they gave him water.

After drinking, he wanted to repay their kindness. He asked his men for money, but they had brought none on the hunt.

The prince opened his quiver. His arrows had golden tips, so he gave each girl ten, thirty valuable arrows in all.

The girls were astonished. One said only Ma‘an, famed for generosity, would give away gold even from arrows used in war.

They decided to thank him in verse. The first praised a warrior whose golden arrows could help even those wounded in battle.

The second praised his generosity toward friends and enemies; the third said the gold could pay for a wounded person’s treatment or a dead person’s shroud.

Ma‘an heard their praise after their kindness to him. They had recognized him by his gift, not his clothing or attendants. The golden arrows remained with them as a memory of the meeting.
