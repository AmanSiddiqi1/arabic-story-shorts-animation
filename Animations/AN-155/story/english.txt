The poet Diibil sat near a gate in Baghdad when a beautiful woman passed. He addressed her with a verse about sleeplessness and tears.

She immediately answered in matching verse. Amazed by her quick wit, he tried another poetic question, and she replied just as skillfully.

Diibil changed the rhythm to test her, but she matched it effortlessly. With her consent she accompanied him, although he had no suitable place to host her.

He took her to his friend Muslim ibn al-Walid, who owned a pleasant house. Muslim welcomed them but lacked money for refreshments.

Muslim gave Diibil a valuable kerchief to sell. Diibil bought food and drink with the proceeds and returned expecting gratitude.

Instead, Muslim had taken the woman into a private room. He accepted the purchases, mockingly thanked Diibil and shut the door in his face.

The two poets traded sharp verses. Muslim reminded Diibil that the house, kerchief and money had all belonged to him.

Diibil finally admitted he had been outwitted. He left in frustration and, the narrator says, never saw or heard of the woman again.
