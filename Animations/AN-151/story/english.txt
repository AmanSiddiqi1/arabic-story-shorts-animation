A man who served at a mosque could neither read nor write, but he was skilled at deceiving people. One day he decided to open a school for children, so he gathered tablets and papers, hung them on the walls, and put on a large turban.

Passersby thought the man in the turban was a great scholar, and enrolled their children. He would point to one child and say write, and to another and say read, while the children learned from one another, and the teacher explained nothing himself.

One day a woman arrived with a letter from her traveling husband. The man wanted to flee before she asked him to read it, but he could not. She handed him the paper and asked what her husband had written, and he held it upside down before his eyes.

He shook his head and looked distressed. and the woman grew afraid, thinking her husband had died. She asked whether she should tear her clothes and slap her face in grief, and he said yes, without knowing a single word of the letter.

She returned home weeping with her children. Her neighbor heard the news and said he had received a letter from her husband yesterday, saying he was well and would return soon. He asked for the paper and read it in front of her.

The husband was well and would return in ten days; he had sent a quilt and a candle extinguisher. The woman grew angry and took the letter back to the school to ask the teacher why he had frightened her so.

The man did not admit he was illiterate, but invented a strange answer. He said he had thought the husband was dead, wrapped in the quilt, because the candle extinguisher was with him, and he claimed this was why he grieved when reading the letter.

Stranger still, the woman accepted this explanation and left without exposing his trick. And the man stayed sitting in his school in his large turban, leaving the children to learn alone, having escaped a question that could have exposed him.
