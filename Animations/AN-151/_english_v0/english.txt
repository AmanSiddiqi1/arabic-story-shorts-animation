A mosque servant who could neither read nor write decided to open a school. He hung up tablets and papers and put on a large turban.

Passersby mistook his appearance for scholarship and enrolled their children. He told one child to write and another to read, while the pupils taught one another.

One day a woman arrived with a letter from her traveling husband. Unable to escape, the impostor held the page upside down and pretended to read.

He shook his head and looked distressed. The woman assumed her husband had died; when she asked whether to tear her clothes and beat her face, he agreed.

She returned home weeping with her children. A neighbor who had recently heard from her husband asked to see the letter and read it aloud.

The husband was well and would return in ten days; he had sent a quilt and a candle extinguisher. Furious, the woman went back to confront the teacher.

Rather than admit he was illiterate, the man invented a strange explanation: he claimed to have imagined the husband dead inside the quilt, with the extinguisher beside him.

Strangely, she accepted his excuse and left. The impostor remained at his school in his enormous turban, having escaped exposure once again.
