# KD-011 The Rats and the Iron: creative plan

Film length 161.0 s (narration 157.51 s + 3.5 s THE END). 18 scenes, 159 timed actions. Every time is seconds on the final MP3. The JSON file holds the full action list; this file gives the direction behind it.

## 1. Narrative breakdown
**Logline.** A merchant whose friend claims that rats ate his iron answers with an equally absurd tale, that a hawk carried off the friend's son, and so wins back the truth, his iron and the friendship.

**Conflict.** Trust between old friends is broken by a sale and then insulted by a silly excuse. The merchant cannot prove the lie and will not shame his friend in public (58.14), so he answers in the friend's own language.

**Spine.** Setup 0-21.7 (iron, journey, promise). Betrayal 21.7-32.6 (months pass, the sale). The lie 32.6-54.7 (rats ate it). The turn 54.7-61.6 (astonished, silent, planning). The counter-move 61.6-81.3 (the boy is taken somewhere safe and fed). The mirror 81.3-98.98 (a hawk took him). Climax 98.98-117.2 (IMPOSSIBLE! and the calm reply that sets the two tales side by side). Reveal and confession 117.2-136.7. Resolution 136.7-148.2 (boy home, debt paid). Moral 148.2-157.2, then a wordless closing gag and THE END.

**Surprises.** The moth in the purse (24.5); the friend finding his lie in a passing rat (42.3); the hawk growing to the width of the cloud (94.22); the equals sign (115.56); the real rat failing to eat iron (157.8).

**Emotional arc.** Warmth, guilt, bluff, quiet resolve, a father's real fear, shame, forgiveness, joy. The fear in S11 must be sincere: it is what makes the lesson land, and the audience already knows the boy is safe and eating.

**Moral.** A lie is best exposed by holding up its own mirror; an absurd excuse invites an absurd answer, and honesty restores what trickery took.

## 3. Character acting
- **Merchant: stillness and economy.** Upright, half-lidded, hands behind his back or stroking his beard (`PERSON_HANDS.chin`). He gets the film's single look to camera (55.44) and two winks (to the boy 75.8, to the friend 120.28). His decision not to accuse is one gesture: the finger rises with `anticipate` at 58.14 and curls back at 58.76. His smile appears only when he has turned away from the friend (60.4). In S12 he copies the friend's amazed poses (arms out, hands to cheeks) slowly and deadpan.
- **Friend: a mask that slips.** Big, quick gestures, sweat, saccading eyes (`gazePlan(t, plan, 0.07)`). The amazed face is worn only while the merchant looks at him; when the merchant looks at the cloud or the empty room, the friend's eyes check him. Suspense by stillness at 27.38 (only the pupils reach the padlock) and 117.24-118.9 (one blink). Real fear in S11: `tremble`, `wet`, raised brows, a hand held at the boy's height. Shame at 121.66 follows the Thief ending: look up, eyes break away, head drops, cap a frame late.
- **Boy.** Always in motion: bouncing ball, swinging the joined hands, two steps per adult step, chewing with alternate cheeks, a failed wink (both eyes). He copies the adults' promise pose at 147.7.
- **Rats.** Never in unison (`stagger(i)`): a sniffing leader, a straggler. Eating is bites out of the top row and puffed cheeks. The one real rat is plain (no bib) so the cloud rats read as invention.
- **Hawk.** Hover, anticipation rise, curved stoop, heavy stepped climb with the load; the boy's legs `swing`.
- **Anticipation and follow-through everywhere:** inhale before the lie (37.52), crouch before the jump (98.98), turban, cap, bundle and padlock trail by a frame or two; `settleTo` on every shock.
- **Listeners:** both gazes are set in every two-shot; the onlookers in S7 turn one after the other.

## 4. Camera and composition
- One home framing, the doorstep two-shot (merchant x 300, friend x 600, zoom about 1.45), used for parting, return, lie, worry, reply, confession and payment so that the changes in the men carry the story.
- The crane down from the roofs (0-1.8) sells "busy city"; the pull-out at 21.7 gives the sky room to turn through the months; the last pull-out leaves three small friends under a wide evening sky for THE END.
- Push-ins mark decisions: the friend hunting for a story (41.48), the merchant's astonishment (54.72), the calm before the reply (106.12), the silence (117.24, zoom up to 2.4 with the merchant's profile kept in frame for the wink).
- The two picture clouds get the same box and the same tilt-up (S6, S12), then appear together, smaller, with the equals sign (S14). Clouds, labels and lettering stay above y 540; heads sit near y 690.
- With the boy in shot, lower the camera centre to y 770-780 and kneel the adult (S9, hug in S17) so faces share the frame.
- Panel-only shake once, on IMPOSSIBLE! (101.6).

## 5. Storytelling opportunities
- **Humour:** rats in bibs eating iron like bread and one burping in the pause at 54.3; the merchant testing the padlock with a knuckle; the dotted outline labelled ALONE; the mocking bird-flap mime (104.18); the hush both men make at the end; the rat's tooth.
- **Suspense:** the eyes creeping to the padlock (27.38); the finger that almost accuses (58.14); the long silence (117.24).
- **Surprise:** the friend's own tale handed back to him, picture for picture.
- **Emotion:** the promise pose made (19.0), remade (131.44) and kept (147.12); the purse that was hidden at 31.72 comes out of the same place at 143.3; the father on his knees for the hug; the merchant covering the handshake with his other hand.
- **Clarity for children:** the boy is seen safe, fed and winking before the father worries, and he enjoys the imaginary flight, so the trick is never frightening.

## 8. Warnings and open questions
- Additions beyond the script (owner to confirm): the rat that gives the friend his idea at 42.3; the closing gag after the last word (a rat hurts its tooth on an iron nail) and the small hawk in the last sky.
- The merchant taking the boy is staged as plainly kind and consensual (ball returned, hand in hand, food, a shared wink) and the cloud boy enjoys the flight (WHEEE!), so the film stays comic and never threatening.
- The safe place is staged as the merchant's own house; the script does not say where it is.
- Months pass by a sky and counter montage (23.02); the single LATER card is kept for A FEW DAYS LATER at 61.62.
- marketStreet, merchantHouse, the person costume presets, villager, purse, ball, kneel, flush, thoughtCloud and dayCycle are planned by other stories and may not exist yet when this film is built; if not, they must be drawn here first.
- Five rats plus four people is the fullest frame (S6); keep the rats inside the cloud so they stay small and simple.
