A merchant lived in a busy city and owned a large amount of iron.|He wanted to travel for work, so he asked an old friend|to keep the iron in his house until he returned.|The friend agreed and promised to do so.
The merchant was away for months. During that time, his friend needed money,|so he sold the iron and kept the payment.|When the merchant returned and asked for his property,|the friend said, “Something very strange happened!”
“What happened?” the merchant asked.|“I put the iron in the storeroom,” said the friend,|“but rats came inside and ate all of it!”|The merchant was astonished, but he did not call his friend a liar in front of everyone.
A few days later, the merchant met his friend’s son on the road.|He took the boy to a safe place, gave him food,|and asked him to wait there for a while.|Then he returned alone to his friend’s house.
The man asked anxiously, “Have you seen my son?|He has not come home!”|The merchant answered, “I saw him on the road,|but a large hawk flew down from the sky and carried him away.”
“That is impossible!” the man cried.|“How could a hawk carry a child?”|The merchant answered calmly, “In a city where rats can eat iron,|I would not be surprised if a hawk could carry a child!”
The friend fell silent and realized that the merchant knew the truth.|Finally he said, “The rats did not eat the iron.|I sold it when I needed money.|I will return your iron or pay its full value.”
The merchant brought the boy back safely, and his father was delighted to see him.|The man paid the merchant for the iron as promised.|After that, neither of them spoke again|of rats that ate iron or hawks that carried children.
