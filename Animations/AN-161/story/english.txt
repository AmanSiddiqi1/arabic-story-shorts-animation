Abu Bakr ibn al-Anbari visited a monastery and met its prior, Abd al-Masih, with forty monks. A year later, he saw the prior at the Kaaba calling himself Abdallah.

Asked why he had embraced Islam, Abdallah told of a young Muslim traveler who became infatuated with a Christian bread seller and stayed outside her shop despite her refusal.

The woman complained. Village boys stoned the youth, and the prior rescued him, tending his wounds in the monastery for fourteen days.

The youth returned. She offered marriage if he changed faith, then an intimate encounter, but he refused both. After another stoning, he died before reaching the monastery.

That night she dreamed the youth led her to Paradise, where she embraced Islam and saw a beautiful pavilion. She heard that she would join him after five nights.

In the dream he gave her two apples. She awoke holding one; the monks shared it and marveled, though they disagreed about the meaning of her vision.

Five days later she was found dead at his grave. Villagers and two Muslim elders disputed the burial because they disagreed over her faith.

The tale says the monks could not lift her body, while one elder could and buried her beside the youth. The prior, monks, and villagers then embraced Islam.
