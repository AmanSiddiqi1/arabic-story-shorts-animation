In Bukhara a goldsmith lived with his devout wife. A water-carrier had brought water to their home for thirty years, filling the cisterns and leaving without trouble.

One day the wife stood in the courtyard while the water-carrier worked. Suddenly he gripped and squeezed her hand, then left. She was shocked by this unfamiliar behavior.

When the goldsmith returned, she asked whether he had done anything wrong in his shop. He denied it, but she insisted on the full truth.

He admitted making a gold bracelet for a customer. When she returned to try it on, he admired her wrist and improperly pressed and squeezed her hand, then let her leave.

His wife replied that the water-carrier had done precisely the same to her that day, though he had never behaved that way in thirty years.

Ashamed, the goldsmith repented and asked his wife to pray for forgiveness. She prayed for them both, though the incident still troubled them.

The next day the water-carrier knelt before her and begged pardon, blaming temptation. She told him her husband had committed a similar act in his shop.

The tale ends with a saying that one act is repaid by a like act. Its narrator linked the offenses, while the wife confronted the wrongdoing and exposed the truth.
