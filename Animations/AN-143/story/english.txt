One day Caliph Harun al-Rashid went into the desert with Jaafar the Barmecide, Abu Nuwas, and a companion. They saw an old Bedouin leaning against his donkey.

Jaafar asked where the man had come from. “Basra,” he said. He was going to Baghdad to find medicine for his eye, because he could not see well.

Harun wanted some amusement and ordered Jaafar to tease him. Jaafar hesitated, fearing an unpleasant reply, but the caliph insisted.

Jaafar asked what reward he would get for a cure. The old man said he prayed that God would reward him better than any payment he could offer.

With a solemn face, Jaafar prescribed measures of wind, sunlight, moonlight, and lamplight, mixed and left outdoors for three months.

Jaafar continued: pound it in a bottomless mortar for three more months, leave it in a cracked dish for another three, then take some each night to cure the eye.

The Bedouin understood the mockery. He stretched over his donkey, loudly broke wind, and said, “Take this as payment now! If I recover, I’ll send a servant who will foul your face after your death!”

He called Jaafar a fool and continued with harsh curses until Harun burst out laughing. Delighted by the Bedouin’s quick reply, the caliph gave him three thousand silver dirhams.
