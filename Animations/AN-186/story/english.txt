A moral tale tells of an unnamed prophet worshipping on a hill above a spring, where he could watch travelers stopping for water.

A horseman drank at the spring and rode away, accidentally leaving a bag containing one thousand dinars.

A second traveler found the bag, took it, and departed. Later a poor woodcutter arrived with firewood, unaware of the missing money.

The horseman returned, saw only the woodcutter, and accused him of theft. The woodcutter denied seeing or taking any money.

Enraged, the horseman killed the innocent woodcutter and departed. The prophet was distressed by the injustice he had witnessed.

The prophet wondered how the man who took the money escaped while another died. In the tale, a divine explanation revealed an unseen history.

The horseman’s father had stolen a thousand dinars from the second traveler’s father; the money now returned to that family. The woodcutter had previously killed the horseman’s father.

The tale portrays a justice hidden from a limited observer. It is a didactic story, not an authenticated report about a named prophet.
