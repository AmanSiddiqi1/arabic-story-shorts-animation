A religious moral tale describes a king preparing a splendid procession. He chose luxurious clothes, jewels and a fine horse, with soldiers surrounding him.

Riding proudly, he imagined no one could equal his power. He passed the people without acknowledging them and thought himself beyond advice.

A stranger in worn clothing greeted him, but the king did not reply. The man seized the bridle, insisting he had a private message.

The stranger revealed himself as the Angel of Death. The king begged time to say goodbye to his family, but was told his appointed hour had arrived.

The king fell dead from his horse. Neither jewels nor troops could save him, and his grand display ended abruptly.

The Angel next visited a devout man. When told who had arrived, the man welcomed him calmly, saying he had long awaited meeting his Lord.

The Angel told him he could choose how his life would end. He asked to perform ablution and pray, and to die while prostrating.

He died in prostration, as the tale recounts. The story contrasts a proud king denied delay with a worshipper who met death prepared and peaceful.
