Firuz returned to his father’s palace after days away, and the king and his household celebrated his safety. He told them how he found the descent lever and reached a distant land above the clouds.

The princess welcomed him, and they spoke with her family. She chose to visit his country later with an overland escort, since the horse carried only two.

Firuz hid the horse in the palace garden and went to arrange the journey. The inventor had learned his destination from his conversation, so he traveled there secretly by land with a caravan.

The inventor told the princess, “Firuz sent me to explain the horse before your return.” She doubted him, but he showed the controls and said the prince was nearby.

The princess sat down to learn, but the inventor climbed behind her and turned the control. The horse flew before she could dismount. She asked him to return, but he refused.

The inventor approached the princess. “Firuz sent me to show you how to ride before the journey,” he claimed. She doubted him, but he demonstrated the two controls and insisted the prince was waiting nearby.

The princess mounted the horse to learn, but the inventor climbed behind her and suddenly turned the control. The horse flew before she could dismount; she cried out for him to return, but he refused.

Firuz returned to the garden but found neither horse nor princess. He saw a piece of the inventor’s turban on the ground and realized a trick was underway. Looking skyward, he said, “I will bring them back safely.”
