Sinbad loved the sea but knew little of its dangers. He gathered his goods, | said goodbye to his family in Baghdad, and boarded a merchant ship.
The ship sailed for days on a calm sea. | One sunny morning, the sailors saw a green island. Some went ashore to rest while the captain watched the sea.
Sinbad sat with his companions near the trees, | and they lit a fire to cook. Suddenly the ground shook beneath them, | and water began rising frighteningly around the edges of the island.
The captain shouted from the ship, “Come back quickly! | That is not an island, but a huge sleeping whale!” | The merchants ran toward the boats, and the fire went out as the whale’s back began to sink.
Sinbad jumped into the sea but could not reach a boat. | He found a wooden plank floating nearby, clung to it with all his strength, | and watched the ship disappear behind tall waves.
Sinbad stayed on the plank until the wind calmed. | In the morning, the waves carried him to a real shore, where he fell asleep from exhaustion. | The king’s horse keepers found him and gave him food and water.
The king welcomed the strange guest and listened to his story in amazement. Days later, a merchant ship arrived. | Sinbad recognized its captain and saw the chests he had left aboard.
The captain returned Sinbad’s goods, and the king helped him reach Baghdad. Sinbad embraced his family, | told them about the island, and learned that the sea held many wonders.
