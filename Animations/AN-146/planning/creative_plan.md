## 1. Narrative breakdown

**Logline.** A reformed thief, now a cloth merchant, is robbed by an impostor who dresses like him and fools the night watchman. He follows the camel's trail to a storeroom by the port, takes back his four bales and the thief's cloak with them, then laughs and returns the cloak when the thief asks for it.

**Conflict.** Cunning against calm. The impostor wins the night with nerve (he asks the watchman himself for a light). The merchant wins the morning by reading the clues, because he knows the trade from the inside.

**Beats.**
- Setup (0..21.4): mask thrown away, shop opened, four bales counted and tied with his own tasselled knot, door locked at dusk.
- Inciting (22.4..43.2): another thief, the change of clothes, the keys, the candle request.
- Rising and comic suspense (43.2..66.1): mask off when the watchman leaves; the lock; the scramble into a scholar's pose; counting on fingers.
- Turn (66.1..86.7): time lapse to dawn, camel, four bales loaded, two dirhams.
- Comic irony (87.6..98.7): the real merchant is welcomed like a king; the watchman's thought cloud shows why.
- Reveal (101.3..110.3): wax, ledgers, four bales gone.
- Investigation (111.5..131.5): watchman, driver, storeroom near the port, the ship the bales were meant for.
- Climax (131.5..143.5): the four bales under a red cloak, the knot untouched.
- Surprise and resolution (143.6..177.4): he takes the cloak too; the thief runs up, admits defeat, begs for the cloak; a laugh, the cloak sails back, they part.

**Emotional arc.** Contentment, then tension and guilty fun with the impostor, then the merchant's puzzlement, shock, a narrowing of the eyes (he knows this trick), quiet relief at the knot, mischief with the cloak, and warmth at the end.

**Moral.** A cool head recovers what cunning stole, and a man who has left his own wrongs behind can afford to be generous: he takes back what is his and nothing more.

## 3. Character acting

- **Merchant.** Content and unhurried: lid 0.3..0.5, smile, humming walk. Shock at 108.34 is a full stretch (sq 1.12, eyes wide, `headHold`, turban landing late), followed by the key character moment at 109.58: sag, then lid 0.45 with brow -0.4 and a slow nod. From there he acts with purpose (tip 0.06 forward, long stride). In the storeroom he is tender with the knot (tug, pat, breath out). His two looks to the lens (98.68 puzzled, 142.72 sly) are the only ones he gets. The laugh at 168.52 is head back, eyes shut, four shoulder bounces (sq 1.05, 0.97), beard a frame late.
- **Impostor.** Mask on, mask off. Polite face (lid 0.25, smile, tip -0.05, `folded` hands) whenever the watchman can see him; sly face (brow -0.4, lid 0.4, hand rub) only when alone: 46.50 and 65.30 are the switches. As a thief he tiptoes with saccade eyes (`gazePlan` ease 0.07). The scholar pose at 58.14 is frozen except the muttering mouth and counting fingers; he never looks at the door until it shuts, then one eye opens. In P8 he is a different man: puffing run, slump (sq 0.9), sheepish palms up, timid pointing, clasped hands with `wet` eyes, a shiver (`tremble`).
- **Watchman.** Sleepy at night (lid 0.5, yawn), eager to please (double nods, salutes with a hop). By day, far too friendly: bow tip 0.6, arms out, dusting the merchant's shoulder. Under questioning: grin held too long, saccades between coins and merchant, gulp, coins behind the back, knees trembling.
- **Driver.** Plain working man: heavy carrying steps (tip -0.1), confusion when dragged back (brow -0.5), head scratch before pointing.
- **Camel.** The deadpan witness: chewing, lid 0.5, a look up and down at the impostor (75.44), a grunt and 3 % squash for each bale, `headTurn` back toward the port before anyone asks (120.06), and at 157.32 a slow look from one blue robe to the other with two blinks.
- **Everyone.** Blinks through `LIFE.t`; listeners `lookAt` the speaker; anticipation before every door push, lift, throw and run; settle after every pose change; cloak hems, tassel, keys and the shop sign as secondary motion on `swing`.

## 4. Camera and composition

- Establishing wides open each location (0.0, 22.4, 87.2, 123.6); the shop door (cx 630) keeps one screen position in every street scene so day, dusk, night, dawn and morning read as the same place.
- Two-shots for every exchange, framed with `twoShot` so both faces stay in: impostor and watchman (35.2), watchman and merchant (91.4, 111.0), the two lookalikes (156.4).
- Push-ins on decisions and proof: the lock (47.6), the impostor's face after the watchman leaves (63.8), the coins in the palm (94.6), the knot (137.64, the tightest shot of the film at zoom 2.6).
- One whip pan for the reveal of the empty corner (107.62) and one long pan for the harbour (126.4..127.6).
- S8 is locked off so the time lapse reads; S17 ends on the widest view with the two parties leaving opposite edges.
- Lettering lives in the sky band (panel y 120..330) with arrows; nothing covers a face; all key action stays above panel y 1250.

## 5. Storytelling opportunities

- **Humour.** The clothes flying up from behind the stall; the portrait that eyes him sideways; a thief asking a watchman for a light; the over-careful watchman tiptoeing in to leave a candle for the burglar; the bow for two dirhams; the royal welcome the merchant cannot explain; the camel's double look at two identical robes; the cloak landing over the thief's head.
- **Suspense.** The shadow before the figure; the freeze at the lantern glow; the first key failing; footsteps under the door and the scramble; stillness while the watchman looks; the held beat before the laugh (167.52).
- **Surprise.** The empty corner on a whip pan; the red mound in the dark; the merchant putting the cloak on.
- **Dramatic irony.** The audience knows who tipped the watchman; the thought cloud (96.84) shows exactly how wrong he is.
- **Setup and payoff.** Four fixed bale colours and the tasselled knot (14.60) return in the storeroom (137.64); the fallen tassel (109.58) is carried through the search; the keys locking at dusk (16.86) answer the keys by night (32.78); the mask thrown away at 1.38 answers the wink at 172.60.
- **Emotion.** The fond glance back at the shop (19.76), the pat on the bale (139.24), the generosity of the last minute.
