A man had given up stealing and repented of it,|and opened a shop selling cloth in the market.|He traded for a while and came to know his goods well,|and he closed the shop in the evening and went home at ease.
One night another thief came to the market,|dressed like the merchant, carrying keys.|He saw the market watchman and asked him to light a candle for him,|pretending to check his accounts.
While the watchman went to get a flame,|the trickster opened the shop, went in and lit another candle.|When the watchman returned, he found him sitting among the account books,|counting figures on his fingers, so the watchman did not suspect him.
The trickster stayed in the shop until dawn,|then asked the watchman to bring a man with a camel to carry some cloth.|The man loaded four large bales,|and left with them and the thief, who gave the watchman two dirhams.
The real merchant arrived in the morning,|and the watchman greeted him warmly because of the two dirhams he thought came from him.|The shop owner was puzzled, then opened the door,|and saw traces of candles and the books on the floor, and found the four bales gone.
The merchant asked the watchman what had happened and learned about the man and the camel.|He sent for the camel driver and asked him where he had taken the bales.|The driver led him to a storeroom near the port, where he had left the goods before shipping.
The merchant found his four bales still bound as before,|with the thief’s cloak thrown over them.|The merchant took the cloth and the cloak,|reloaded the goods, and went off with the camel driver toward the ship.
The thief caught up with the merchant after seeing that his trick had failed,|and told him that he had got all his cloth back,|and asking for his cloak back.|The merchant laughed and gave back the cloak without a quarrel, then each went his own way.
