A wealthy merchant in a city named Sindah packed cloth and valuable goods on camels and set out to trade in another city.

Two cheats heard of his journey and joined him, carrying a few goods and pretending to be merchants. He welcomed their company.

At the first rest stop, the cheats agreed to steal the merchant’s valuable cargo and become rich. They began planning the theft.

Secretly, each thought: “Why share the wealth? If I get rid of my partner I can keep everything.” Both planned to betray the other.

The first poisoned food and offered it to his companion, pretending generosity. The second independently prepared poisoned food for the first.

They ate the food they had exchanged. Neither suspected the meal before him was poisoned, each preoccupied with his own scheme. Soon both died.

Noticing their long absence, the merchant searched and found them dead. From what they left behind, he understood they had not been honest companions.

The merchant escaped the theft without knowing every detail, took the goods they left and continued his journey. Each cheat had died from the food prepared by the other.
