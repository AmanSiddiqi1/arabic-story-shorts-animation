An old tale tells of a king who amassed immeasurable wealth. He built a fortified palace with servants and guards so he could enjoy it undisturbed.

One day he ordered a grand feast and seated himself on his throne, telling himself it was finally time to enjoy his savings.

Before he could eat, a tremendous knock shook the gate. Servants saw a poorly dressed man and ordered him to wait for leftovers.

The stranger insisted on urgent business with the king. A second knock came; guards threatened him, but he announced that he was the Angel of Death.

Terrified, the king asked whether another person might die in his place. The answer was no: he must leave behind everything he had hoarded.

The king wept and blamed his treasures for distracting him from good deeds. In the fable, the treasure itself began to speak.

The speaking treasure said it could have fed the poor, helped the sick and built bridges and places of worship. The fault lay in the king’s choices.

The king died on his throne before tasting his feast. The tale reminds listeners that wealth can serve good purposes but cannot purchase more time.
